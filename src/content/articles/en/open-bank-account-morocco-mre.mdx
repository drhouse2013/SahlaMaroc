A Moroccan bank account makes everything simpler: paying rent, receiving a transfer, settling electricity, repaying a loan or buying property. But the **type of account** changes your rights, especially for sending money abroad. Here's how to choose and open the right one.

<Callout type="tip" title="The right account in one sentence">
- **MRE or non-resident foreigner** who wants freedom to transfer: **convertible dirham account** or **foreign-currency account**.
- **Resident settled in Morocco** (residence permit, local salary): a standard **dirham current account**.
</Callout>

## The three account types

| Account | Who it's for | Strengths | Good to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Convertible dirhams** | MREs and non-resident foreigners | Funded by transfers from abroad, usable in Morocco **and** abroad | Funds should come from abroad (converted foreign currency) to keep the freedom to transfer back |
| **Foreign currency** | MREs and non-resident foreigners | You keep your euros or dollars | Useful if you mostly spend in foreign currency |
| **Dirham current account (resident)** | Residents in Morocco | Salary, bills, direct debits | Transfers abroad are more restricted |

Banks are authorised to open these accounts for **Moroccans living abroad** and **non-resident foreigners**. They are offered by the country's major banks (Attijariwafa bank, Banque Populaire, Bank of Africa, CIH Bank, Crédit du Maroc, Société Générale Maroc, etc.), often with dedicated MRE packages.

## Documents to prepare

Lists vary slightly by bank. As a rule:

- Valid **passport** or national ID card.
- **Proof of address abroad** under 3 months old (bill, rent receipt, lease).
- **ID from your country of residence** (residence permit).
- **Proof of income** (recent payslips or employment contract) depending on the package.
- **Proof of MRE status** (consular registration card) for MRE offers.
- **IBAN of your foreign account** for incoming transfers.

## Opening steps

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 1" title="Compare offers">

Ask for **account, card and transfer fees**, the **exchange rate applied** and online services. Compare at least three banks.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 2" title="Book an appointment">

Many banks accept a first request online or an appointment booking before you arrive. Then go to a branch with the originals.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 3" title="Sign and activate">

Check the terms, sign, then activate the **bank card** and **mobile app**. Ask for an **account number (RIB)** to give to payers.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 4" title="Fund it with traceable transfers">

Fund the account by **transfer from abroad** and keep the proof: it is used later for **transferring your funds back**. See [sending money to Morocco](/en/send-money-to-morocco).

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## Tips and pitfalls

- **Keep every transfer receipt:** the "retransfer" rule rests on what you brought in as foreign currency. See [property taxes when selling and renting](/en/property-tax-selling-renting-morocco-mre).
- **Compare your bank's exchange rate:** that's where fees hide.
- **An MRE account** makes buying property and borrowing easier: see the [MRE mortgage guide](/en/mortgage-morocco-mre-guide) and [Daam Sakane](/en/daam-sakane-housing-aid-mre).
- **Nomads and short stays:** some banks require proof of residence in Morocco. A foreign multi-currency account (such as <AffiliateLink provider="wise">Wise</AffiliateLink>) helps while you open a local one. See [cash and ATMs](/en/money-cash-atms-morocco).
- **Avoid dormant accounts:** close unused accounts to avoid fees.

<Callout type="info" title="Confirm with your bank">
Opening conditions evolve and are set bank by bank within the exchange control rules (Exchange Office, Bank Al-Maghrib). Always ask for the exact list of documents before you travel.
</Callout>
