A Moroccan bank account simplifies everything: paying rent, receiving a transfer, paying utilities, repaying a loan or buying property. But the **type of account** changes your rights, above all for sending money abroad. Here is how to choose and open the right one, separating what the **Office des changes** sets (a national rule) from what each bank decides (documents, fees, delays).

<Callout type="tip" title="The right account in one sentence">
- **MRE or non-resident foreigner** who wants to keep the freedom to transfer: **convertible-dirham account** or **foreign-currency account**.
- **Resident settled in Morocco** (residence permit, local salary): an ordinary **dirham current account**.
</Callout>

## What the regulation says

The 2026 General Instruction on Foreign Exchange Operations (Office des changes, [oc.gov.ma](https://www.oc.gov.ma)) allows banks to open **foreign-currency and convertible-dirham accounts** for Moroccans residing abroad and for resident or non-resident foreigners. These accounts cannot be overdrawn. **Credits**: transfers from abroad, transfers from accounts of the same kind, banknotes against the currency import declaration (dated six months at most). **Debits**: any payment in Morocco or abroad, including cash withdrawals, and term deposits. More in [transferring your savings to Morocco](/en/transfer-savings-morocco-mre).

## The three account types

| Account | For whom | Strengths | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Convertible dirham** | MRE and non-resident foreigners | Funded from abroad, usable in Morocco **and** abroad | Keep proof of the foreign origin of funds |
| **Foreign currency** | MRE and non-resident foreigners | You keep your euros or dollars | Useful if you mostly spend in currency |
| **Dirham current (resident)** | Residents of Morocco | Salary, bills, direct debits | Transfers abroad more restricted |

Major banks (Attijariwafa bank, Banque Populaire, Bank of Africa, CIH Bank, Crédit du Maroc, Société Générale Maroc, etc.) often have MRE offers. Their terms differ: compare.

## Documents to prepare

Lists vary by bank and are not set by a single text. Typically you will be asked for:

- **Passport** or national ID (original).
- **Recent proof of residence abroad:** the Office des changes text provides that the bank keeps a document attesting residence abroad for these accounts.
- **Residence permit or consular card** of your country of residence.
- **Proof of income** or activity, depending on the offer.
- **RIB/IBAN of your foreign account** for incoming transfers.
- **Proof of address in Morocco** if you are resident.

Call the branch for the exact list **before** going.

## Common mistakes

- Opening a resident account by default when you are non-resident, then finding retransfers are tighter.
- Paying in banknotes without the currency import declaration: the bank asks for it.
- Not comparing the exchange rate: that is where fees hide.
- Leaving dormant accounts and paying keeping fees.
- Forgetting to report the account in your country of residence if local law requires it.

- **An MRE account** eases a property purchase and a loan: see [MRE mortgage](/en/mortgage-morocco-mre-guide) and [Daam Sakane](/en/daam-sakane-housing-aid-mre).

## FAQ

**Can I open an account remotely?** It depends on the bank. Some offer prior contact; signing is generally in a branch or through their MRE service. Confirm with the bank.

**How much does an account cost?** No single official tariff: each bank publishes its own grid. Ask for it in writing.

**Can I withdraw euros?** Debits of a currency or convertible account may include cash withdrawals under the IGOC; actual availability of currency at the branch depends on the bank.

**What card limit?** Set by the bank (see [cash and ATMs](/en/money-cash-atms-morocco)).

## After opening

Check that online banking works from abroad, note the SWIFT/IBAN for incoming transfers, and ask how to be warned of low balances. Review fees after three months and close accounts you do not use.

## Two sample cases

**A retired MRE moving back.** Opens a convertible-dirham account before returning, then a resident account once settled; keeps the transfer slips for any later retransfer.

**A digital nomad staying six months.** Uses a multi-currency card at first, then opens a local account if the bank accepts their proof of residence.

## Extra questions

**Can I open an account for my children?** Conditions depend on the bank and age: ask for the document list.

**Is a power of attorney possible?** Depending on the bank; plan for a legalised power of attorney if you cannot travel (see [power of attorney](/en/power-of-attorney-morocco-property-mre)).

## Reading the fee schedule

Ask for the fee grid for account keeping, card, incoming and outgoing transfers, cash withdrawals abroad and currency conversion. Compare the total yearly cost for your expected usage rather than a single headline fee, and ask whether fees are waived above a minimum balance.

## Checklist

1. Choose the account type.
2. Compare 3 banks (fees, rate).
3. Gather passport/ID, proof of residence abroad, income proof.
4. Sign, activate card and app, note the RIB.
5. Fund by transfer and archive proofs.

<Callout type="info" title="Confirm with your bank">
Opening conditions are set bank by bank within the foreign-exchange regulation (Office des changes, Bank Al-Maghrib). Always ask for the exact document list and tariff before going.
</Callout>
