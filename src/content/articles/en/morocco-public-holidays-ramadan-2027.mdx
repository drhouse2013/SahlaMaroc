Religious holidays in Morocco follow the lunar calendar and are confirmed by the Ministry of Habous and Islamic Affairs after the moon sighting, usually **the evening before**. Dates below are **expected dates, with a possible one-day shift**.

<Callout type="tip" title="2027 at a glance (expected)">
- **Ramadan:** about **8 February to 9 March 2027**
- **Eid al-Fitr:** about **10–11 March 2027**
- **Eid al-Adha:** about **17–18 May 2027**
- **Islamic New Year:** about **6 June 2027**
- **Mawlid (Prophet's birthday):** about **14–15 August 2027**
</Callout>

## Ramadan: what changes for visitors

- **No clock change anymore.** Since 20 September 2026 Morocco is on GMT all year, so Ramadan no longer moves the time. See [Morocco's time zone](/en/morocco-time-zone-gmt).
- **Restaurants and cafés:** many close during the day or serve discreetly, then fill up at sunset (*iftar*). Tourist-oriented places in Marrakech, Casablanca and Rabat stay open.
- **Shops, banks and offices:** shorter opening hours, with slower days in the afternoon.
- **Be respectful:** avoid eating, drinking or smoking in the street during the day.
- **Evenings are lively:** markets, sweets (*chebakia*) and soups (*harira*) are a highlight if you like local atmosphere.

## Eid al-Adha and Eid al-Fitr: plan ahead

Both holidays are **peak travel dates**: MRE families come home, trains and flights sell out, and prices rise. Book transport and accommodation early, and keep cash because ATMs can run empty. Many shops close for 2–3 days around Eid al-Adha.

## Planning tips

1. Check the exact date with the Ministry announcement or the consulate a few days before.
2. Avoid long road trips on the eve of Eid, when roads are crowded.
3. If you work remotely, tell clients about local holidays in advance. Our [nomad guide](/en/best-cities-digital-nomads-morocco) has more.
