In Morocco, accommodation is part of the trip. A **riad** (a traditional house around a courtyard) does not offer the same experience as a city **hotel**, a seaside **resort** or a long-stay **apartment**. The right choice depends on what you want to live, who travels with you, how you arrive and the season. This guide compares the formulas, lists the questions to ask before booking and points to each city's guide.

<Callout type="tip" title="The right choice at a glance">
- **Discovering a medina (Marrakech, Fes, Essaouira, Chefchaouen):** a riad.
- **Family with children, pool, simplicity:** a hotel or resort.
- **More than 4 nights, remote work, cooking:** an apartment.
- **Tight budget:** a guesthouse or hostel.
</Callout>

## The options compared

| Option | Pros | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Riad / guesthouse** | Charm, rooftop breakfast, personal welcome, inner calm | Access through lanes (no car), no lift, variable Wi-Fi, uneven heating |
| **City hotel** | Services, 24-hour reception, parking, pool | Less authenticity, sometimes far from sights |
| **Resort / all-inclusive** | Everything on site, good with children | Tourist bubble, little immersion, transfers |
| **Furnished apartment** | Space, kitchen, long-stay rate | No services, rules to check |
| **Hostel** | Low price, meeting people | Dorms, limited privacy |

## Riad: who it suits (and who it does not)

The riad is the most memorable way to discover a medina: you step into a calm courtyard, breakfast on a terrace, a host gives advice. It suits couples, culture lovers and short stays. It suits less if you travel with lots of luggage, young children, a mobility impairment or a car: lanes are narrow, the final access is on foot and stairs are often steep. Also check soundproofing and winter heating: some riads are cold.

## Hotel and resort: simplicity

A hotel brings round-the-clock reception, parking, a pool and standardised services. The resort concentrates everything on site, useful for a family in summer; in exchange, you experience the city less. Big seaside zones (Agadir, Taghazout Bay, Malabata in Tangier) offer many; see [where to stay in Agadir](/en/where-to-stay-in-agadir) and [where to stay in Tangier](/en/where-to-stay-in-tangier).

## Apartment and short-term rental

For more than four nights, a furnished apartment gives space, a kitchen and a more normal daily life. It suits remote work provided you check the connection: see [internet and coworking](/en/fibre-internet-coworking-morocco). Short-term rental rules exist; read the [short-term rental guide](/en/short-term-rental-morocco-rules) and ask for a clear contract and proof of payment.

## The 8 questions to ask before booking

1. **How do I arrive from the station, car park or taxi?** In medinas, the car stops at a gate: ask them to come and get you.
2. **Is the Wi-Fi reliable for video calls?** Ask for a speed-test screenshot.
3. **Are air conditioning and heating included?** In winter some riads are cold.
4. **What does breakfast include?** And dinner, if offered?
5. **What is the cancellation policy?** In writing.
6. **Is the tourist tax included?** It may be paid on site.
7. **Noise:** courtyard or street room? Near a mosque or café?
8. **Child safety:** pools, stairs, terraces.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **Booking without reading recent reviews**: a riad's mood changes with management.
- **Ignoring the real distance** to the medina gate or the beach.
- **Accepting a "better hotel" proposal** made at the station or port by a tout: see [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers).
- **Paying in full in advance** to a stranger with no contract.

## Budget and season
