Moving back to Morocco as a family starts with the children's school year. Three questions come up every time: which school, with which papers and at what level; how to get certificates and diplomas recognised; and how to cover the family for health (CNSS / AMO). Exact rules depend on the school, the region and the parents' work status: this guide sets out the framework and flags what to confirm.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **School**: three main routes, **public** school, **private** (Moroccan) school, or an establishment run under a **foreign mission** (for example the French schools network abroad). Teaching languages and fees differ greatly; contact the school **several months before** the move.
- **Equivalence**: for primary and secondary school, placement in a class is decided through the school administration (provincial delegation / regional academy, AREF) or the school itself. Higher education has its own rules (see below).
- **CNSS / AMO**: children's affiliation depends on the parent's status (employee, self-employed, other). Only CNSS can confirm the conditions for your file.
- **In every case**: prepare the family record book, birth certificates, report cards and certificates, **translated and legalised if needed**, before leaving.
</Callout>

## Choosing a school: public, private, foreign mission

**Public schools**: the main language of instruction is Arabic, with French taught as the first foreign language, according to general information available. For children schooled abroad without Arabic, the change of language can be the main obstacle, especially from middle school: diaspora accounts reported in the press (Hespress, August 2026) mention a harder transition around ages 11-12.

**Moroccan private schools**: often bilingual or French-speaking, with Arabic as a subject. Admission terms, fees and calendars are set by each school.

**Foreign-mission schools** (including the French network): foreign curriculum, often with limited places and applications due early in the year. If the child comes from a school of the same network, continuity is usually simpler, but admission remains the school's decision. Our guide to [international schools in Morocco](/en/international-schools-morocco-expats) covers these options.

We give no tuition fees: they vary too much between schools and we could not verify them from an official source.

## The enrolment file

Required documents are set by each school or delegation. Commonly requested:

- the **family record book** and the child's **birth certificate**;
- **school certificates** and **report cards** from previous years, showing the level reached;
- parents' ID;
- photos and sometimes a medical certificate.

Documents in a language other than Arabic or French may need translation by a sworn translator. To find out whether your documents must be legalised or apostilled, see our guide to [apostille and legalisation](/en/apostille-legalisation-documents-morocco). If the children's civil status is not up to date (consular registration, transcription of a record), see also the [marriage and civil status guide](/en/marriage-civil-status-morocco-guide).

## Level, tests and class placement

We found **no public national rule** describing systematic placement tests for children of Moroccans abroad. In practice, the school or regional administration reviews the school file and proposes a class; some private schools run their own assessments. Promotion or repetition decisions are not guaranteed: ask for them **in writing** before committing.

For context, a communication from the Rabat-Salé AREF (April 2014, MAP news agency, via CCME) about foreign immigrants' children said enrolment stays open during the school year and that children above school age are directed to non-formal education. This is not a general rule for children of Moroccans abroad: confirm with the delegation in your city.

## Equivalence of certificates and diplomas

**Primary and secondary**: this is mostly about getting the school level recognised so the child joins the right class. Your contact is the provincial delegation or AREF where you live, or the chosen school directly. We found no public text fixing processing times: plan ahead.

**Higher education**: equivalence falls under the ministry in charge of higher education. Two recent developments are reported in the press:

- orders published in Official Bulletin No. 7422 (17 July 2025) automatically recognise as equivalent certain diplomas (bachelor's, master's, doctorate, engineering) from public institutions in Spain, Belgium, the United Kingdom, Germany and France, under conditions (national diploma, regular course justified by proof of residence in the country, minimum length for the engineering title);
- an article in Le Matin (5 October 2026) describes, for 2026-2027, a ministerial note exempting international applicants from prior equivalence when applying for master's and doctorate programmes at public universities. We have not consulted the note itself.

Check the official text and the target institution's conditions before filing.

## CNSS and AMO: covering the whole family

AMO (compulsory health insurance) is run by CNSS for private-sector employees and, since late 2021, for part of the self-employed.

- **Employee parent**: affiliation goes through the employer. Children can be declared as dependants; the precise conditions (age, schooling, documents) are set by CNSS. Its FAQ also provides that an insured person whose spouse stops working can ask for the children's rights to be transferred to their own account.
- **Age limit for dependent children**: according to the CNSS website (AMO pages, consulted on 10 October 2026), dependent children are covered up to **21**, up to **26** for unmarried children pursuing higher education, and with no age limit in case of disability. Law 54-23 (social protection reform, published in the Official Bulletin on 29 January 2026 according to Médias24) raises the limit to **30** for children enrolled in higher education or vocational training; the press places its entry into force about a year after publication (early 2027). One website announces earlier application from September 2026: we could not find this in an official source. Confirm with CNSS.
- **Self-employed parent**: the press reports that registration of the first categories of non-employees began in December 2021 and that dependants are declared via the private space on macnss.ma. Eligible categories may have changed since.
- **Retiree or no activity**: we could not confirm the applicable scheme or contributions from an official source. Contact CNSS before you settle.

CNSS contact numbers given on its FAQ: 0802 030 100 or 0520 447 100. Check the numbers before calling.

### Interim health cover

Until Moroccan affiliation is active, do not leave the children uncovered: keep your foreign cover while it lasts, and ask your insurer how long rights continue after you leave (it varies). Our guide to [health and insurance in Morocco](/en/health-morocco-travellers-insurance-emergencies) recalls the basics for stays.

## Other steps for the children

- **National ID (CNIE)**: according to the press (January 2021), Moroccan minors are entitled to their own electronic national ID card, valid 7 years under 12 and 10 years above. Check age, documents and where to apply with the DGSN or the consulate.
- **Family record book and civil status records**: ask for recent copies before leaving.
- **Vaccines and medical certificate**: we have no official source on school requirements; ask the school and a doctor, and bring the health record.
- **Moving and resident status**: see the [permanent return guide](/en/permanent-return-morocco-mre-guide).

## General rule, exceptions, points to confirm

**General rule**: enrolment is with the school and, in some cases, the regional school administration; CNSS depends on the parent's status.

**Exceptions**: foreign-mission schools (own calendar and admission), children above school age, higher education diplomas governed by specific orders.

**To confirm**: exact documents and timelines at your city's AREF, class placement rules, CNSS dependants and contributions for your status, required vaccines.

<Callout type="warning" title="General information, not legal advice">
This guide relies on public information, sometimes relayed by the press, rather than a review of the complete official texts. Rules change and vary by school and region. Always confirm with the National Education delegation, the school, CNSS and the consulate before acting.
</Callout>
