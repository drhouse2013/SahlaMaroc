In two of the country's biggest cities, the **tram** is the simplest and cheapest way to get around, far from the traffic jams. Here's how to use it, with indicative fares and pitfalls to avoid.

<Callout type="tip" title="In short">
- **Casablanca:** 4 lines (T1 to T4); single ticket **8 MAD** in the 2024 proposed grid, a rise to 9 MAD was mentioned: **check the fare at the station**.
- **Rabat-Salé:** 2 lines, single ticket **7 MAD** (6 to 7 MAD rise dated July 2025 by most media).
- Always validate your ticket: **inspections** are frequent and fines real.
</Callout>

## Casablanca tram

The network has **four lines** (T1, T2, T3, T4) and over **a hundred stations** over about 70 kilometres. It serves the city centre, the medina, the corniche and many outer districts.

- **Single ticket: 8 MAD** per the 2024 proposed grid (a rise to 9 MAD was mentioned with no confirmed date: check the posted fare). A free 2-hour transfer appeared in that grid: to be confirmed.
- The ticket is **also valid on the two BRT lines** (bus rapid transit) with free transfers between the two systems.
- Buy your ticket at the station **machines** and **validate it** before boarding.

## Rabat-Salé tram

Two lines, **43 stations** and about **27 km** connect Rabat and Salé on either side of the Bouregreg. The single ticket is **7 MAD**; validity period and transfers are to be confirmed at the station.

It serves notably **Rabat-Ville station**, the medina (the Kasbah of the Udayas is a short walk away) and the campus: very handy for a day of sightseeing. See also [Morocco's airports](/en/morocco-airports-to-city-centre) and [trains in Morocco](/en/trains-in-morocco-al-boraq).

## Practical tips

1. **Avoid rush hours** (7:30–9 am and 5–7 pm) if you have suitcases.
2. **Keep your ticket** until you leave the station: inspections can happen.
3. **Watch your belongings** on crowded trams.
4. **Download the map** offline to spot your stops: see [SIM and eSIM](/en/best-sim-card-esim-morocco).
5. Complement with **taxis** and **ride-hailing apps** for late evenings: [taxis and apps](/en/taxi-apps-morocco).

## What about Marrakech?

There is no tram. To reach the medina from the airport, see [Marrakech airport](/en/marrakech-airport-to-medina); in the city, **petit taxis** remain the most practical option.

## Steps to use the tram

1. **Find your line and stop** on an offline map.
2. **Buy your ticket** at the station machine (bring coins).
3. **Validate it** before boarding, at the platform or on-board validator depending on the station.
4. **Board and stand** near the doors if you get off soon; watch your belongings.
5. **Get off at the right stop:** announcements are in Arabic and French.
6. **Keep the ticket until you exit** in case of inspection.

## Common mistakes

- Not validating: the fine can exceed the ticket price.
- Taking the tram with large suitcases at rush hour.
- Counting on a free transfer without checking current conditions.
- Waiting for a tram in Marrakech: there is none.
- Leaving a bag open in a crowded car.

## FAQ

**Is there a pass?** Passes exist in Rabat-Salé (the press reported a monthly pass of around 270 MAD after the increase); conditions and amounts to be confirmed with the operator.

**Can I pay by card?** Payment options vary by station: bring coins.

**Does the tram go to the airport?** No. See [Casablanca airport](/en/casablanca-airport-to-city-centre) and [Morocco airports](/en/morocco-airports-to-city-centre).

**What about families?** Any reduced fares are to be confirmed at the station.

## Three typical trips

**Casablanca: from the centre to the corniche or a station.** The tram links the centre, several districts and stations. Find the line on the station map, buy the ticket, validate it and allow a few minutes' walk at each end. For the airport, the tram is not the answer: see [Casablanca airport](/en/casablanca-airport-to-city-centre).

**Rabat-Salé: from the station to the medina and the Oudayas kasbah.** The tram crosses the Bouregreg and serves Rabat-Ville station: it is the easiest way to see Rabat without a car, then on to Salé. See also [where to stay in Rabat](/en/where-to-stay-in-rabat).

**An MRE returning with family.** The tram suits short trips with children, but not with many suitcases at rush hour. Plan a taxi for the bulk of the luggage and keep the tram for day outings.

## Reminder: fares and rules

The fares in this guide are dated landmarks (a grid proposed in 2024 for Casablanca, a 6 to 7 MAD rise in Rabat-Salé reported by the press): they do not replace the posted fares at the station. On inspection, an unvalidated ticket can mean a fine; the amount was not confirmed here.

## Extra questions

**Are timetables the same every day?** Frequencies vary by hour and day: check the station display.

**Can I board with a pushchair?** Generally yes, subject to space.

**Is the BRT the same as the tram?** No: the BRT is a bus rapid transit service with its own lines; check the operator for current transfer rules.

## Good to know

The tram runs late, but the last services vary: check the posted timetable if you go out in the evening. During Ramadan, frequencies and crowds change around the breaking of the fast. Keep valuables close at busy times. Accessibility features vary by station and vehicle: ask the operator if you need step-free access.

## Checklist

1. Offline map downloaded.
2. Coins for the machine.
3. Ticket bought and validated.
4. Belongings watched.
5. Ticket kept until exit.
