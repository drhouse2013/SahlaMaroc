Inheriting property in Morocco while living abroad is common and often badly prepared. The procedures are structured, but they follow their own rules, different from those in France, Spain or Belgium. Here is the path, step by step.

<Callout type="warning" title="The starting point to understand">
For **real estate located in Morocco**, succession is in principle governed by **Moroccan law** (the law of the country where the property sits), whatever the nationality or residence of the deceased and heirs. Each heir's share is set by the **Family Code (Moudawana)**. With **dual nationality**, a **non-Moroccan spouse** or a **difference of religion**, the situation gets complex: consult a notary or lawyer before any decision.
</Callout>

## The succession path

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 1" title="Gather the basic documents">

- Death certificate (with translation or legalisation if issued abroad).
- Heirs' ID documents and family record books.
- Land title or ownership certificate for the property.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 2" title="Have the inheritance deed drawn up">

The **inheritance deed** (*ishhad bil wirth*) is drawn up by two **adouls** with witnesses. It identifies the heirs and **each one's share** under the Moudawana. It is the central document of the whole file.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 3" title="Value the property and decide its fate">

Have the property **valued** and choose: stay in **co-ownership**, **divide** it between heirs, or **sell** it (then share the price). Each option has tax and practical consequences.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 4" title="Settle the tax side">

Death-related duties for the **direct line** (spouse, ascendants and descendants) are, according to the sources consulted, **exempt**. Other costs remain: **land registry fees**, **registration duties** for the partition, fees. Ask the notary for a **written quote**.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 5" title="Register the transfer at the land registry">

On presentation of the inheritance deed and supporting documents, the property is **registered in the heirs' names** at the **ANCFCC**. Allow **one to three months** after the deeds.

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## Co-ownership: pitfall number one

When several heirs stay owners together, **none can sell alone**, and the slightest disagreement blocks everything. The most frequent conflicts:

- one heir **occupies the property** without paying the others;
- one heir **can't or won't sell**;
- **expenses** (taxes, repairs) not shared;
- **heirs hard to reach** abroad.

**Good reflex:** put a **written agreement** between heirs in place from the start (who occupies, who pays what, who decides), ideally at the notary.

## Heirs abroad: acting remotely

- Give a trusted relative a **power of attorney** for the procedures, with **precise** powers: see [power of attorney for Moroccan property](/en/power-of-attorney-morocco-property-mre).
- Keep **digital copies** of every deed.
- If you sell, plan the **repatriation of the proceeds**: see [property taxes when selling and renting](/en/property-tax-selling-renting-morocco-mre).

## Prevent rather than suffer

If you own property in Morocco yourself, prepare now: an **up-to-date land title**, a **list of assets and accounts** kept current for your family, and a **consultation with a notary** on the best way to organise your estate between your country of residence and Morocco. See also [buying property from abroad](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad).
