Inheriting property in Morocco while living abroad is common and often badly prepared. The procedures are well defined but follow their own rules, different from those of France, Spain or Belgium. Here is the journey, with what the tax authority says officially and what your notary still has to confirm.

<Callout type="warning" title="The starting point to understand">
For **real estate located in Morocco**, succession is in principle governed by **Moroccan law**, whatever the nationality and residence of the deceased and the heirs. Each person's share is set by the **Family Code (Moudawana)**. With **dual nationality**, a **non-Moroccan spouse** or a **difference of religion**, the situation becomes complex: consult a notary or lawyer **before** any decision.
</Callout>

## The succession journey

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 1" title="Gather the basic documents">

- Death certificate, with legalisation or apostille and translation if issued abroad (see [apostille and legalisation](/en/apostille-legalisation-documents-morocco)).
- Heirs' ID documents, family record books, civil-status records proving kinship.
- Land title or ownership certificate of the property (ANCFCC online service), or adoulary deeds for a melk property.
- Local-tax receipts, and any current rental contract.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 2" title="Have the inheritance deed drawn up">

The **inheritance deed** is drawn up by **adouls** in the presence of witnesses. It identifies the heirs and **each one's share** under the Moudawana. It is the central document: ask for several certified copies.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 3" title="Value the property and decide its fate">

Have the **property valued** and choose: stay in **joint ownership**, **partition** between heirs, or **sell** then split the price. Each option has tax and practical consequences. For a later sale: see [tax on selling and renting](/en/property-tax-selling-renting-morocco-mre) and the [capital gains calculator](/en/property-capital-gains-tax-morocco-calculator).

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 4" title="Pay duties and register the deeds">

The 2026 MRE tax guide gives **1.5%** for **partitions** between co-heirs. Deeds must be **registered within thirty days** of being drawn up to avoid penalties and surcharges. A deed drawn up abroad may need a Moroccan registration attestation (press source). Demand a **written quote** from the notary with every cost: registration, land registry, fees, translations.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 5" title="Register the transfer at the land registry">

On presentation of the inheritance deed and documents, the property is **registered in the heirs' names** at **ANCFCC**. Allow **one to three months** after the deeds (unofficial estimate).

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## Joint ownership: pitfall number one

When several heirs remain owners together, **selling the whole property requires everyone's agreement**, and the slightest disagreement blocks everything. The most frequent conflicts:

- one heir **occupies the property** without paying the others;
- one heir **cannot or will not sell**;
- **expenses** (taxes, works) not shared;
- **heirs hard to reach** abroad.

**Good reflex:** formalise from the start a **written agreement** between heirs (who occupies, who pays what, who decides), ideally at the notary. For tax, each co-owner is taxed on his share and files his own declaration on a sale.

## What the tax authority says when an heir sells

According to the 2026 MRE tax guide:

- The land profit is taxed at **20%** of the net profit, with a **minimum of 3% of the sale price**.
- The **inheritance deed** or the post-death inventory serves as **proof of acquisition** in the prior-opinion request.
- The **profit on the sale of undivided rights in agricultural property outside the urban perimeter between co-heirs** is exempt.
- **Gratuitous transfers** of property between ascendants and descendants, spouses, and siblings are exempt from income tax on land profits.
- The land-profit **declaration** is filed electronically within **30 days** of the sale; a prior-opinion request can be filed within 30 days of the sale agreement (answer within 60 days).

## Heirs abroad: how to act remotely

- Give a **power of attorney** to a trusted relative, with **precise** powers and the required form: see [power of attorney for property](/en/power-of-attorney-morocco-property-mre).
- Keep **digital copies** of every deed.
- If you sell, plan the **repatriation of proceeds** and keep the receipts.
- Before locking a file in joint ownership, compare with the option of **buying out the other shares**: for a purchase or buy-out, see [buying property from abroad](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad).

## Common mistakes

- **Waiting for years**: heirs age, documents get lost, regularisation costs rise.
- **Occupying or renting the property without a written agreement** between heirs.
- **Selling without an inheritance deed** or prior transfer.
- **Forgetting registration deadlines** (30 days) for a deed made abroad.
- **Assuming a simple verbal agreement** is enough for a partition.

<Checklist id="heritage-immobilier-maroc" lang="en" title="Property inheritance: my checklist">

- Death certificate with apostille and translation if issued abroad
- Inheritance deed drawn up by the adouls, certified copies
- Ownership certificate or deeds of the property read
- Written agreement between heirs (occupancy, costs, decisions)
- Notary quote with all costs
- Deeds registered within 30 days, registration attestation requested if the deed was made abroad
- Transfer registered at ANCFCC in the heirs' names

</Checklist>

## Prevent rather than endure

If you own property in Morocco yourself, prepare today: an **up-to-date land title**, a **list of assets and accounts** kept current for your family, and a **consultation with a notary** on the best organisation between your countries of residence and Morocco. For a return project: [permanent return to Morocco](/en/permanent-return-morocco-mre-guide).
