For a marriage, a registration or an administrative file you are often asked for an **extract** or a **full copy** of your Moroccan birth certificate. This guide explains the difference, who can request it and the two official routes: **online ordering (Watiqa)** and the **consulate**.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **Born in Morocco or transcribed by a consulate**: order online on the official counter [watiqa.ma](https://www.watiqa.ma), delivered by registered mail in Morocco or abroad.
- **Full copy or extract?** Ask the receiving authority which one it requires: it depends on the body.
- **Prepare** the certificate number, the transcription year and a copy of the family record book, an older certificate or the national ID card (CNIE).
</Callout>

## Extract or full copy

| Document | What it is | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Birth certificate extract | Summary version of the record | Depends on the authority asking for it |
| Full copy | Complete reproduction of the record | Some files require it, for example marriage (the Watiqa form has a "for marriage" box) |

No source consulted says which authority requires which: **always ask the receiving body**.

## Who can request the full copy

According to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (consulat.ma), the full copy is issued to:

- the holder of the record;
- their ascendants, descendants or spouse;
- their guardian (testamentary or appointed);
- any person holding a mandate.

It is issued by the civil registrar of the **place of declaration or transcription** of the birth, in Morocco or at consular services.

## Route 1: online order on Watiqa

Watiqa is the official electronic counter for ordering the extract or full copy. It covers people registered at a civil registry office **in Morocco** (region, province, commune, office) or **abroad** (country of birth and consular transcription service).

| Step | What the form asks |
|---|---|
| Identity | First and last name in Arabic (Latin optional), mother's first and last names, father's first name |
| Birth | Date of birth, sex |
| Record | Record number, transcription year, language of the record (Arabic or French) |
| Purpose | "For marriage" box: yes or no |
| Attachment | Copy of the family record book, **or** an older birth certificate, **or** the CNIE (image or PDF, 3 MB maximum) |
| Delivery | Registered mail, in Morocco or abroad |
| Payment | Barid e-Pay, phone call or bank card |

The amount is not given here: check the site. On the day we read it, the page showed a maintenance notice for online payment; **check that the service is available** before you start.

Watiqa also offers a "consular registration certificate" for Moroccans living abroad: a separate service, not detailed here.

## Route 2: the consulate

Consular services can request copies for Moroccans residing abroad who were born in Morocco or in another consular district.

- **Documents**: family record book, or an older extract, or an older full copy, or place + record number + year of declaration; plus the applicant's ID card.
- **Chancellery fees**: see the tariff on consulat.ma.
- **Consular call centre**: 00 212 537 66 33 00, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. (Morocco time).

People born in Morocco can also order through Watiqa and receive the document at home.

## The civil registry portal

The portal alhalalmadania.ma offers to request a copy of a birth certificate (with a link to Watiqa) and to submit an online birth pre-declaration request.

## Step by step for an MRE

1. **Identify the record**: place of birth (Morocco or consulate), number, year. Easiest: your parents' family book or an old extract.
2. **Ask the receiving body**: extract or full copy, language (Arabic or French), maximum age, whether an apostille is needed.
3. **Choose the route**: Watiqa if you have the attachment (family book, old record or ID) and an accepted payment method; consulate if you are already registered or the record cannot be found.
4. **Check the document received**: spelling of names, dates, marginal notes (marriage, divorce, death). A mistake is corrected before, not after, filing a marriage or nationality file.
5. **Have it translated and apostilled** if it will be used abroad.

## Child born abroad: registering with Moroccan civil status

A child born to a Moroccan father or mother is Moroccan (Nationality Code, law 62-06). To obtain a Moroccan birth record, the birth must be transcribed into the Moroccan civil registry through the consulate, on presentation of the full copy of the foreign record and, according to some sources, the parents' marriage record. An older implementing decree of law 37-99 used 30 days for declaration; the press reports a one-year deadline for consular transcription and the possibility of transcribing later with the same documents. Law 36-21 has replaced law 37-99, so **confirm the current deadline with your consulate**, not on a third-party site. This registration then determines the child's family book and ID card: see [marriage and civil status](/en/marriage-civil-status-morocco-guide).

## Common mistakes

- **Ordering a copy without knowing what the body requires**: extract and full copy are not interchangeable.
- **Entering the name differently** from the record: the form expects Arabic, with the father's first name and the mother's first and last names.
- **Forgetting the attachment** (family book, old record or ID, 3 MB maximum): the request may be blocked.
- **Not allowing for registered-mail delivery** before a consulate appointment or wedding date.
- **Finding a mistake too late**: read the record as soon as you receive it.

## After you receive it: translation and legalisation

For use in another country you often need an apostille or legalisation and a translation: see [apostille and legalisation of documents](/en/apostille-legalisation-documents-morocco). For a marriage project, see [marriage and civil status](/en/marriage-civil-status-morocco-guide). ## Checklist before ordering

<Checklist id="acte-naissance" lang="en" title="Birth certificate: my checklist">

- Type of document the body asks for (extract or full copy)
- Record number, transcription year and place found
- Attachment ready: family book, old record or ID (3 MB maximum)
- Watiqa service available, payment method accepted
- Delivery time compatible with my deadline
- Spelling and marginal notes checked on receipt
- Translation and apostille planned if used abroad
