You travel to Morocco with your mouth too. Moroccan cuisine blends gentle spices, dried fruit, slow-cooked meats and homemade breads. Here are the dishes to try first, a few practical reflexes and the table customs.

<Callout type="tip" title="The 7 must-tries">
- **Tagine** (meat or vegetables stewed in a conical clay dish)
- **Couscous**, traditionally served on **Fridays**
- **Pastilla** (sweet-and-savoury flaky pie, often with pigeon or chicken)
- **Harira** (tomato, lentil and chickpea soup, very present during Ramadan)
- **Tanjia** (a Marrakech speciality, slow-cooked meat)
- **Msemen** and **baghrir** (breakfast pancakes)
- **Mint tea**, offered as a sign of hospitality
</Callout>

## Main dishes

| Dish | What it is | Where to taste it |
|---|---|---|
| **Tagine** | Chicken with olives and preserved lemon, lamb with prunes, vegetables, fish… | Everywhere, from small eateries to guest houses |
| **Couscous** | Steamed semolina, vegetables and meat | On Fridays with family, and in many restaurants |
| **Pastilla** | Thin pastry pie, chicken or pigeon, almonds, cinnamon | Traditional restaurants and celebrations |
| **Rfissa** | Chicken, lentils, fenugreek over shredded msemen | Family cooking, celebrations |
| **Tanjia** | Meat cooked slowly in a jar | A Marrakech speciality |
| **Mechoui** | Slow-roasted lamb | Celebrations, markets, specialist restaurants |
| **Zaalouk / taktouka** | Cooked salads of aubergine or pepper-tomato | As a starter, everywhere |
| **Harira** | Tomato-lentil-chickpea soup | Streets and homes, especially in Ramadan |

## Breakfast and street food

- **Msemen, baghrir, harcha** with honey, butter and jam; **coffee, tea** or fresh orange juice.
- **Sfenj** (doughnuts) and **maakouda** (potato fritters) in the street.
- **Grilled sardines** at Essaouira's or Agadir's port, **skewers** and **snail soup** in Marrakech.
- **Fresh juices** on the squares (orange, pomegranate): ask for no ice if you're sensitive.

## Sweet things

**Chebakia** (especially in Ramadan), **gazelle horns**, **sellou** (almond and sesame mix), **dates** and **mint tea**, offered to guests: politely declining is possible, but accepting is a pleasure.

## At the table: good manners

- You eat with the **right hand**, often from a **shared dish**: take what's in front of you.
- **Bread** serves as cutlery and is shared.
- **Guest in a private home:** bring pastries or fruit; take off your shoes if the host does.
- **Tipping** in restaurants: see [tipping in Morocco](/en/tipping-in-morocco).

## Practical tips

1. **Go where it's busy:** a full restaurant is a good sign of freshness.
2. **Bottled water** and fruit you can peel if you have a sensitive stomach: see [health for travellers](/en/health-morocco-travellers-insurance-emergencies).
3. **Menus with prices:** ask prices before ordering in tourist spots: see [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers).
4. **Ramadan:** some restaurants close by day: [public holidays and Ramadan](/en/morocco-public-holidays-ramadan-2027).
5. **Learn a few words:** [Darija for travellers](/en/darija-phrases-for-travellers).

<Callout type="info" title="Bon appétit: bssaha!">
Cuisine varies by region: fish on the coast, tagines and meats inland, Berber dishes in the Atlas. Eat local and ask for the house speciality.
</Callout>
