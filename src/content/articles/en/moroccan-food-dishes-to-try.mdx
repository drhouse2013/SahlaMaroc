You travel through Morocco with your mouth too. Moroccan cuisine blends gentle spices, dried fruit, slow-cooked meats, grilled fish and homemade breads. It changes from region to region, family to family, and from a street table to a riad. Here are the dishes to taste first, the regional differences, practical habits for eating well without risk and table customs. Couscous is on UNESCO's intangible cultural heritage list, under a file shared by Morocco and other Maghreb countries ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/lists)), a sign of its cultural weight.

<Callout type="tip" title="The 7 essentials">
- **Tagine** (meat, fish or vegetables slow-cooked in a conical clay dish)
- **Couscous**, traditionally served on **Fridays**
- **Pastilla** (sweet-savoury flaky pastry, often with pigeon or chicken)
- **Harira** (tomato, lentil and chickpea soup, very present during Ramadan)
- **Tanjia** (a Marrakech speciality, slow-cooked meat)
- **Msemen** and **baghrir** (breakfast pancakes)
- **Mint tea**, offered as a sign of hospitality
</Callout>

## Main dishes

| Dish | What it is | Where to eat it |
|---|---|---|
| **Tagine** | Chicken-olive-preserved lemon, lamb-prune, vegetables, fish | Everywhere, from small restaurants to guesthouses |
| **Couscous** | Steamed semolina, vegetables and meat | Fridays with family, and in many restaurants |
| **Pastilla** | Thin flaky pastry, chicken or pigeon, almonds, cinnamon | Traditional restaurants, celebrations |
| **Rfissa** | Chicken, lentils, fenugreek over torn msemen | Family cooking, celebrations |
| **Tanjia** | Meat slow-cooked in a jar | Marrakech speciality |
| **Mechoui** | Slow-roasted lamb | Celebrations, markets, specialist restaurants |
| **Zaalouk / taktouka** | Cooked salads of aubergine or pepper-tomato | Starters, everywhere |
| **Harira** | Tomato-lentil-chickpea soup | Streets and homes, especially in Ramadan |

## By region: what to taste where

- **Marrakech and the South:** tanjia, tagines with dried fruit, snail soup on the medina square, pressed orange juice. See [where to stay in Marrakech](/en/where-to-stay-in-marrakech).
- **Fes and Meknes:** refined city cooking, pastilla, sweet-savoury tagines. See [Fes](/en/where-to-stay-in-fes) and [Meknes](/en/where-to-stay-in-meknes).
- **Atlantic coast (Essaouira, Agadir, Casablanca):** grilled fish and seafood, sardines, squid. See [Essaouira](/en/where-to-stay-in-essaouira).
- **North (Tangier, Tetouan, Chefchaouen):** Andalusian and Spanish influences, goat cheese, seafood. See [Tangier](/en/where-to-stay-in-tangier) and [Tetouan](/en/where-to-stay-in-tetouan-mdiq).
- **East and Atlas:** Berber dishes, grilled meats, amlou (almond paste, argan oil and honey) in the Souss. See [Ouarzazate](/en/where-to-stay-in-ouarzazate).

## Breakfast and street food

- **Msemen, baghrir, harcha** with honey, butter and jam; **coffee, tea** or pressed orange juice.
- **Sfenj** (doughnuts) and **maakouda** (potato fritters) in the street.
- **Grilled sardines** in ports; **skewers** on the grill; **chickpea soup** in the morning.
- **Fresh juice** on squares (orange, pomegranate): ask for no ice if you are sensitive.

## Sweets

**Chebakia** (especially in Ramadan), **gazelle horns**, **sellou** (a mix of almonds, sesame and spices), **dates** and **mint tea**, offered to guests. Politely declining is possible, but accepting is a pleasure.

## Diets and allergies

- **Halal:** meat is generally halal; alcohol is rare in small restaurants and reserved for licensed venues.
- **Vegetarian:** vegetables in tagine or couscous, cooked salads, soups, eggs, cheeses. Say "no meat" and "no meat stock": the stock is often meat-based.
- **Gluten-free:** bread and semolina are omnipresent; rice, grilled vegetables and fish are references, but cross-contamination is possible.
- **Allergies:** nuts (almonds, walnuts, peanuts), sesame and argan oil are widely used. Write your allergy in French or Arabic on a paper to show the cook.

## At the table: good manners

- You eat with the **right hand**, often from a **shared dish**: take what is in front of you.
- **Bread** serves as cutlery and is shared.
- **Invited to a home:** bring pastries or fruit; take off your shoes if the host does. See [etiquette and culture](/en/morocco-etiquette-culture-tips).
- **Tipping** in restaurants: see [tipping in Morocco](/en/tipping-in-morocco).
- For a few useful words at the table, read [darija for travellers](/en/darija-phrases-for-travellers).

## Practical tips

1. **Go where there are crowds**: a full restaurant is a good sign of turnover.
2. **Sealed bottled water** and peelable fruit if you have a fragile stomach: see [health for travellers](/en/health-morocco-travellers-insurance-emergencies).
3. **Prices before ordering** in tourist places: see [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers).
4. **Ramadan:** some restaurants close by day, others come alive at iftar: see [public holidays and Ramadan](/en/morocco-public-holidays-ramadan-2027).
5. **Cooking classes:** many riads and agencies offer them; ask for the exact programme and group size before booking. To compare accommodation formulas, see [riad or hotel](/en/riad-or-hotel-morocco-choose).
