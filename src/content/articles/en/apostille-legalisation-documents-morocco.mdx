A French, Spanish or Belgian birth certificate can be refused in Morocco if it isn't properly **authenticated**, and the reverse is true: a Moroccan document must be **apostilled** to be accepted abroad. Here's how to find your way without losing weeks.

<Callout type="tip" title="The essentials">
- Morocco joined the **Hague Convention of 5 October 1961** (apostille) on **27 November 2015**, in force since **14 August 2016**.
- Between member countries, the **apostille replaces chain legalisation**: one stamp is enough.
- For a **Moroccan document** going abroad, the competent authorities are the **prefectures and provinces** (administrative documents, including civil status) and the **courts** (judicial documents); an online platform exists: **apostille.ma**.
- For a **foreign document** going to Morocco, the apostille is issued **in the country of origin**, and a **translation** is often needed.
</Callout>

## Apostille or legalisation: what's the difference?

| | Apostille | Legalisation |
|---|---|---|
| **Principle** | One certificate, affixed by the issuing country's authority | Several steps (ministry, consulate…) |
| **When** | Between members of the Hague Convention | With countries that haven't signed it |
| **Result** | Recognised directly in the other country | Recognised after the full chain |

Morocco is a member: in most cases between Morocco and Europe, the **apostille** is enough.

## Moroccan document for use abroad

Documents concerned: birth and marriage certificates, register extracts, medical certificates, diplomas and certificates, tax and administrative documents, some private documents.

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 1" title="Get a recent original">

Request the certificate from the civil registry or the relevant administration. Some countries require the document to be under 3 or 6 months old.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 2" title="Have the apostille affixed">

Submit the document to the competent authority (prefecture or province for administrative documents such as civil status, court for judicial documents; you can start via apostille.ma). The apostille is a square certificate, attached or glued, with number, date, signature and stamp.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 3" title="Translate if needed">

Have the document translated by a **sworn translator** into the language of the destination country. Keep the original, apostille and translation together.

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## Foreign document for Morocco

1. Obtain a **recent** certificate from your country's administration.
2. Have it **apostilled** by the designated authority of that country (in France, since 1 May 2025, notaries via their regional councils).
3. Have it **translated into Arabic or French** by a recognised translator, if the Moroccan administration requires it.

## For which projects?

- [Residence permit](/en/morocco-residence-permit-guide): civil status records, criminal record, diplomas.
- [Marriage](/en/marriage-civil-status-morocco-guide): capacity certificates and ID documents.
- Purchase or estate: [inheriting property](/en/property-inheritance-morocco-mre).

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Document too old:** renew it before apostilling.
- **Forgetting the translation:** an apostille is not a translation.
- **Wrong authority:** each document type has its own apostille authority.
- **Uncertified copies:** for a private document, a signature certification may be needed first.

<Callout type="info" title="Confirm with the receiving body">
Each body (prefecture, notary, university, employer) may have its own freshness and translation requirements. Ask for the exact list before spending money.
</Callout>
