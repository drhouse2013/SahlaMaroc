A French, Spanish or Belgian birth certificate can be refused in Morocco if it is not properly **authenticated**, and the reverse is true: a Moroccan document generally needs an **apostille** to be accepted abroad. For an MRE, this is the crux of many files (marriage, nationality, inheritance, permanent return, a spouse's residence card). Here is how to find your way without losing weeks.

<Callout type="tip" title="The essentials">
- Morocco acceded to the **Hague Convention of 5 October 1961** on **27 November 2015**; it has been **in force since 14 August 2016** (Hague Conference status table).
- Between states bound by the Convention, the **apostille replaces chain legalisation**: one certificate is enough.
- For a **Moroccan document** used abroad: **courts** for court-related documents; **local authorities of prefectures and provinces** for administrative documents, notarial acts and official certificates. An information site exists: **apostille.ma**.
- For a **foreign document** used in Morocco: the apostille is issued **in the country of origin**, then a **translation** is often requested.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="What changes (updated 16/10/2026)">
Since law 55.19 (procedural simplification), announced by the Interior Ministry in April 2021 with the Idarati portal, Moroccan administrations should no longer require certain certificates (single status, marriage, non-divorce, non-polygamy, kinship, widowhood, Moroccan administrative life certificate…) and may accept a sworn statement. This is not new in 2026, and the full official list is not public in the sources consulted. Note: the apostille and legalisation (documents for use abroad) are a different procedure. The abolition targets signature legalisation and certified copies demanded by Moroccan administrations. Depending on the receiving body, the document may still be requested, especially for a file abroad or at a consulate: confirm before filing.
</Callout>

## Apostille or legalisation: what is the difference?

| | Apostille | Legalisation |
|---|---|---|
| **Principle** | One certificate, issued by the designated authority of the issuing country | Several steps (ministry, consulate…) |
| **When** | Between states bound by the Hague Convention | With states that did not sign, or that objected |
| **Result** | Recognised directly in the other country | Recognised after the full chain |

**The objection case.** The Convention lets a state declare that it will not apply between itself and a newly acceding state (article 12). The Hague Conference status table shows an objection mark next to Morocco's accession; we could not identify which states made it. Before starting, check the [Convention status table](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41) to confirm the Convention applies between Morocco and your country of residence. In most cases between Morocco and Western Europe the apostille is enough, but that does not replace this check.

## Moroccan document for use abroad

Documents concerned: birth and marriage certificates, register extracts, medical certificates, diplomas and certificates, notarial acts, administrative papers, official certificates attached to private documents.

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 1" title="Get a recent original">

Request the document from the civil registry or the administration concerned (see [birth certificate: full copy, Watiqa and consulate](/en/moroccan-birth-certificate-full-copy-extract-watiqa-mre)). First ask the recipient how old a document it accepts.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 2" title="Have the apostille affixed">

Depending on the document: **court** for judicial acts, **prefecture or province** for administrative or notarial documents or official certificates. The apostille is a square certificate, stuck on or attached, bearing a number, date, signature and stamp. The site apostille.ma is the information point listed by the Hague Conference.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 3" title="Translate if needed">

Have it translated by a **sworn translator** into the language of the destination country. Keep the original, apostille and translation together, in that order.

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## Foreign document for Morocco

1. Get a **recent** document from your country's administration (civil registry, court registry, notary, university…).
2. Have it **apostilled** by that country's designated authority. **In France**, notaries issue apostilles, through the regional councils, since 1 May 2025 (replacing the prosecutors general of the courts of appeal); legalisations followed on 1 September 2025. Precise rules are on [notaires.fr](https://www.notaires.fr/fr/article/apostille-legalisation-explications).
3. Have it **translated into Arabic or French** by a recognised translator if the Moroccan body requires it: ask beforehand, it is not systematic.

For a power of attorney, ask the Moroccan notary which form it accepts (apostilled foreign notarial deed or a deed drawn up at the consulate) before choosing the route: we have no official source settling this point.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Document too old:** ask the recipient how old a document it accepts before ordering.
- **Forgetting the translation:** the apostille is not a translation and says nothing about content.
- **Wrong authority:** each document type has its own; a counter error costs weeks.
- **Plain copy or scan:** apostille the original or a copy certified by the competent authority.
- **Not checking the objection:** if the Convention does not apply between the two states, an apostille is useless; legalisation is needed.

## Checklist

<Checklist id="apostille-legalisation" lang="en" title="Apostille: my checklist">

- Recipient contacted: age of document and translation requested
- Convention checked between Morocco and the country concerned (no objection)
- Recent original obtained from the competent authority
- Apostille affixed by the right authority (court or prefecture/province in Morocco; notary in France)
- Sworn translation ordered if requested
- Original, apostille and translation gathered in one file, with a scanned copy

</Checklist>
