In Chefchaouen the best choice is almost always the **medina**: everything is minutes away on foot, and riads and guest houses around a courtyard are what make the stay magical. Here are the sectors and the details that matter.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **Atmosphere and main square:** the **Outa el-Hammam** sector (beware of noise late at night).
- **Views over the medina and the spring:** **Ras el-Maa**, up on the heights.
- **Calmer with good value:** **Bab Souk**, at the north of the medina.
- **By car:** park in a **guarded car park** at the town gates (check the rate on site).
</Callout>

## The medina sectors

| Sector | Atmosphere | Good to know |
|---|---|---|
| **Outa el-Hammam (centre)** | Lively heart around the square | Incredible atmosphere, but more noise: pick a courtyard-facing or well-insulated room |
| **Ras el-Maa** | Perched, views over the medina and the spring | Good for the view and walks; it's uphill |
| **Bab Souk (north)** | Calmer, sometimes with a pool or garden | Good value |

## Tips

1. **Book a riad** with an inner courtyard or terrace: that's the spirit of the place.
2. **Courtyard or insulated room** if you're sensitive to noise.
3. **Arriving:** from Tangier or Fes the coach is the simplest option: [Chefchaouen from Fes or Tangier](/en/chefchaouen-day-trip-from-fes-tangier) and [CTM and Supratours buses](/en/ctm-supratours-bus-morocco).
4. **Car:** the medina streets are pedestrian; plan a guarded car park.
5. **Sleep two nights** if you can: the calm of evening and morning is the real privilege of the town.
6. **Comfortable shoes:** the medina is sloped.
