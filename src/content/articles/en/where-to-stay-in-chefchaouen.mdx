In Chefchaouen the blue medina is almost always the right choice: everything is a few minutes' walk. But "in the medina" covers very different experiences depending on whether you sleep on the busy square, on the heights or at its edges. The town is on a slope, with no road to the riad doors, and evening calm is its real luxury.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **Atmosphere, cafés, the square:** Outa el-Hammam (evening noise).
- **View, trails, spring:** Ras el-Maa, higher up.
- **Calm, more local life:** Souiqa and the medina edges.
- **Car:** guarded parking at the gates, luggage on foot.
</Callout>

## Outa el-Hammam: the square and the kasbah

The heart of the medina is organised around Outa el-Hammam square, the kasbah and the great mosque (details on the [Chefchaouen city page](/en/cities/chefchaouen#spots)). It is the liveliest area: restaurants, shops, thoroughfares. Ideal for a first night and for photos. The trade-off: terrace noise, tour groups by day and a walk with suitcases over uneven cobbles.

## Ras el-Maa: up towards the spring

East of the medina, Ras el-Maa is where water comes down from the mountains. Rooms there offer views and access to trails; the climb can be steep with suitcases. A good choice for walkers and anyone wanting a quiet morning.

## Souiqa: the more discreet district

The Souiqa district, within the medina, is more residential than the square, with neighbourhood life more visible. It suits those preferring a less touristy setting, at the price of a longer walk to the square. Check exact access and slope before booking.

## Outside the medina: parking and amenities

On the medina edges, some hotels have parking or easy car access. Useful if you travel with a lot of luggage, children in pushchairs or a vehicle. At night you walk back into the medina: check the distance on a map.

## Which area for your trip?

| Trip | Area | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Couple, atmosphere | Outa el-Hammam or Souiqa | Evening noise |
| Walkers | Ras el-Maa | Slope |
| Family with children | Medina edges | Slope, distance |

## Season

Spring and autumn are generally pleasant. Summer is busy, especially at weekends. Winter is cool, rainy and sometimes foggy: ask if the room is heated. Weekends and school holidays fill places quickly.

## Access

From Tangier or Fes, the bus is the simplest option: see [Chefchaouen from Fes or Tangier](/en/chefchaouen-day-trip-from-fes-tangier) and [CTM and Supratours](/en/ctm-supratours-bus-morocco). For the night before or after, read [where to stay in Tangier](/en/where-to-stay-in-tangier) and [where to stay in Fes](/en/where-to-stay-in-fes). By car, avoid driving at night in rural areas; see [car rental](/en/car-rental-morocco-guide).

## Safety and good habits

The town is known for its welcome, but street offers exist. Politely decline offers you did not ask for, and be careful with what is proposed (products, excursions). Wear comfortable shoes: the medina is sloped. See [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers) and [riad or hotel](/en/riad-or-hotel-morocco-choose).
