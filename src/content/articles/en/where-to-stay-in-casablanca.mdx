Casablanca is a city of distances: neighbourhoods are spread along the coast and big avenues, and an 8 km trip can take a long time at rush hour. The right area therefore depends less on "charm" than on **the purpose of your stay**. Here is how to think it through.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **Business, short stays, convenience:** Maarif or the city centre, near the tram.
- **Restaurants, galleries, city feel:** Gauthier and Racine.
- **Beach and evenings out:** Ain Diab and the corniche, with taxis to get back.
- **Residential calm, upmarket:** Anfa.
- **Art deco heritage:** the centre around Place Mohammed V.
</Callout>

## Maarif: the practical choice

Busy and commercial, Maarif brings together restaurants, cafés, shops and large shopping centres. It suits a business trip, a 2–3 night stopover or easy errands. The trade-off is dense traffic and few monuments within walking distance.

## Gauthier and Racine: neighbourhoods with taste

**Gauthier** is a district of galleries, concept stores and good tables, popular with young professionals and expats. **Racine**, nearby, is more residential and calmer. Both are good ground for furnished short-stay apartments; see the [short-term rental rules](/en/short-term-rental-morocco-rules) for your rights and safeguards.

## Ain Diab and the corniche: seaside, but far

The corniche lines beach clubs, restaurants and hotels facing the ocean (the [city page](/en/cities/casablanca#spots) describes it). It is pleasant in summer for evenings and the sea, less so if you want to visit the centre: expect taxis, especially at night. Waves and wind can limit swimming; follow local warnings and flags.

## Anfa: hills, villas and calm

Anfa is a residential area of villas and gated residences. It suits longer family stays and anyone wanting silence, but it is not a practical base for seeing everything on foot.

## The centre and its art deco architecture

Around **Place Mohammed V**, the main post office and the central market, the city centre concentrates the art deco and neo-Moorish buildings the city page lists ("Casa art déco"). You are close to stations, the tram and several listed places. The mood is that of a business centre: lively by day, calmer at night.

## The old medina: a stay for atmosphere

Smaller and less touristy than Marrakech's or Fes's, Casablanca's old medina has a few guesthouses. It suits those who like authentic, everyday neighbourhoods. Visit by day, watch bags and phones, and ask your accommodation for the safest route in the evening.

## Finding your area by profile

| Your situation | Area | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Business meetings | Maarif or centre | Measure the trip at rush hour |
| Food and galleries weekend | Gauthier | Book ahead at weekends |
| Family, 1 week | Racine or Anfa | Taxis for outings |
| Sea and evenings | Ain Diab | Taxi back |
| Architecture, tram rides | Centre | Traffic noise |
| Overnight before a flight | Near station or airport | Train times, margin |

## Arrival and transport

Mohammed V airport is far from the centre: the guide [from the airport to the city centre](/en/casablanca-airport-to-city-centre) compares train, taxi and other options. To get around, the tram is a real help; see [tram and BRT](/en/tram-brt-casablanca-rabat-guide). For Rabat by train, see the [train guide](/en/trains-in-morocco-al-boraq) and [where to stay in Rabat](/en/where-to-stay-in-rabat). For hailing taxis, read [taxi apps](/en/taxi-apps-morocco).

## Season and safety

The oceanic climate makes summer milder than inland, though humidity can be felt; winter is cool and sometimes wet. Casablanca is a big city: watch your belongings on transport and in crowded areas, use identified taxis or an app, and read [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers). For the 2030 World Cup, the region is among the proposed sites (FIFA's final list not yet published); book early, see [host cities](/en/world-cup-2030-morocco-host-cities).
