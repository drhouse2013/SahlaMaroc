The **hammam** is a pillar of Moroccan life: a place for hygiene, relaxation and meeting people. There are two main families. The **public hammam**, in the neighbourhood, is used by locals; the **spa hammam**, in a riad or hotel, sells the same ritual in a more comfortable setting. This guide explains the sequence, what to bring, the customs to respect and the questions to ask about price, without quoting tariffs that change from one place to another.

<Callout type="tip" title="In brief">
- **First time, no bearings:** a riad or hotel spa, towels and products provided.
- **Local experience:** a neighbourhood public hammam, with your own kit and some curiosity.
- **Modesty:** keep underwear or a swimsuit on; men and women are separated.
- **Health:** avoid with a fever, feeling unwell or high blood pressure.
</Callout>

## The ritual, step by step

The sequence varies slightly between places, but generally follows this pattern:

1. **Hot room.** You settle in the steam to open the pores and relax.
2. **Rinse** with hot water, using a bucket and a small bowl.
3. **Black soap.** An olive-oil-based paste applied to the body and left a few minutes. Some places offer scented versions (eucalyptus for example).
4. **Kessa glove scrub.** The rough glove removes dead skin. In a public hammam, a *tayeb(a)* can do it for you, for a fee to agree beforehand.
5. **Full rinse.**
6. **Ghassoul (or rhassoul).** A mineral clay applied to skin or hair, often at the end.
7. **Rest and hydration.** A break, water, sometimes mint tea.

## Public hammam or spa: how to choose

| | Public hammam | Riad or hotel spa |
|---|---|---|
| Atmosphere | Local, popular | Calm, comfortable |
| Kit | Bring your own | Generally provided |
| Pace | Free, sometimes noisy | By appointment |
| Welcoming non-Muslims | Varies by place | Generally yes |
| Add-on treatments | Scrub offered on site | Massage, treatments, packages |
| Best for | Authenticity seekers | First time, couples, families |

A travel guide recommends a neighbourhood hammam for the local experience, while warning that some places do not accept non-Muslims ([Lonely Planet](https://www.lonelyplanet.fr/destinations/afrique/maroc/marrakech/que-faire/les-hammams)). Ask your accommodation: it will know where to send you.

## What to bring

- a **towel** and **sandals** (the floor is slippery and hot);
- **black soap** and a **kessa glove** (sold in most neighbourhoods);
- **spare underwear** and a waterproof bag;
- **drinking water** and what you need to dry your hair;
- **small change** for the person who scrubs or massages (see [tipping in Morocco](/en/tipping-in-morocco)).

## Good manners

- **Modesty and discretion:** speak softly, never photograph.
- **Sharing water:** use the bowl and bucket sparingly, rinse the floor after you.
- **Respect of spaces:** men and women are separated; children usually follow the parent of the same sex, depending on the establishment.
- **Contact and massage:** accept or decline politely; it is normal to ask what is included.
- **Hygiene:** shower before entering a spa and do not leave valuables unattended.

## Price: how not to be surprised

Tariffs vary widely between a neighbourhood hammam, a medina hammam used by tourists and a luxury spa. No official source publishes them: rather than show figures that would become wrong, here is the method.

1. **Ask the price before** you start, and what it includes (entry, scrub, ghassoul, oil, massage, towels).
2. **Compare** two or three addresses recommended by your accommodation.
3. **Be wary of "transfer included" packages** sold by platforms: the price sometimes includes a ride and a massage you do not need.
4. **Plan a tip** for the person who scrubs or massages you.
5. **Ask for a receipt** if you pay for a spa package.

## Health and precautions

Heat and humidity are tiring. Avoid the hammam after a big meal, with a fever, a heart condition or high blood pressure without medical advice, or if pregnant without a doctor's advice. Hydrate before and after, stay briefly the first time and leave if you feel unwell. For skin infections or allergies, ask a health professional; see also [health for travellers](/en/health-morocco-travellers-insurance-emergencies).
