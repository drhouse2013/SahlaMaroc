Many Moroccan families abroad wish their loved ones to be laid to rest in Morocco. It is a hard subject, but better prepared calmly in advance: when a death occurs, time is short, the paperwork is heavy and transport costs are high. This guide explains, soberly, how body repatriation cover works, what to compare, and which formalities come with repatriating remains to Morocco.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- Repatriating a deceased person can be covered by a **funeral / provident plan**, a **body repatriation insurance**, or an **annual contribution** paid to a mutual society, association or foundation. These are different products: read the terms of each.
- Repatriation requires **administrative documents** (death certificate, non-contagion certificate, coffining, authorisations, mortuary pass) and the involvement of the **Moroccan consulate**. The exact list depends on the country of death and the consulate.
- **Call your policy's assistance line before spending anything**: some policies will not reimburse costs incurred without their approval.

## The three main kinds of cover

The products on offer are different in nature. Confusing them is the most frequent mistake.

- **Funeral or provident contract**: finances a funeral (sometimes with an option for transporting the body abroad). Check that repatriation to Morocco is expressly included, and up to what limit.
- **Body repatriation insurance**: a specialised cover for transporting the deceased to the country of origin, with an assistance service that organises the operation.
- **Annual contribution to a mutual, association or foundation**: the member pays each year and the organisation arranges or funds repatriation under its rules. Entitlements depend on its statutes and on regular payment.

Names and conditions vary from one provider to another. Always ask for the **information notice** and general conditions before signing.

## What the cover typically includes

As an indication, a body repatriation contract may provide for:

- **transport of the body** to Morocco (sometimes to the burial place);
- help with **consular** and administrative formalities;
- the **coffin** and **preservation care** required for transport;
- travel of an **accompanying person** (a relative);
- the **burial** (depending on the contract).

None of this is automatic: each item has limits and conditions. Compare actual content, not the product name.

## Formalities for repatriation to Morocco

Repatriating remains requires the agreement of both countries. The list below is the one commonly found in practical guides; it varies with the country of death, the city and the consulate. **Confirm it with the competent Moroccan consulate and the funeral director.**

1. **Declare the death** to the civil registry of the country of death, then contact the **Moroccan consulate** quickly (it can issue a consular death certificate).
2. **Local death certificate** (ask for several copies) and a copy of the deceased's ID or passport.
3. **Non-contagion certificate** issued by a doctor.
4. **Coffining** by an approved funeral firm, with a compliant coffin (the airline may require a coffining certificate).
5. **Authorisation to transport the body** issued by the local authority (in France, for example, the prefecture) and a **mortuary pass (laissez-passer mortuaire)** issued by the Moroccan consulate.
6. **Booking the air freight** with an airline that accepts human remains (not all do), then registering the death with the Moroccan civil registry on arrival.

Arabic translations, legalisations or a statement from the family in Morocco naming the burial place may be requested. For foreign documents meant for Moroccan authorities, see our guide to [apostille and document legalisation](/en/apostille-legalisation-documents-morocco).

## Criteria for comparing two contracts

- **Geographic cover**: is your country of residence covered? Does it apply if death occurs while travelling, in Morocco or elsewhere?
- **Limits**: is there an overall cap, or one per item (transport, coffin, companion, burial)?
- **Waiting period**: from when does cover apply after you subscribe?
- **Family cover**: is only the policyholder covered, or also spouse and children? Up to what age?
- **24/7 assistance**: a number reachable at any hour, including weekends and public holidays, is essential.
- **Formalities**: does the assistance service really organise the paperwork, or merely reimburse?
- **Direct payment or reimbursement**: does the provider pay costs directly, or must the family advance large sums?

## Common exclusions to check

Contracts often contain limits, for example:

- a **waiting period** after subscription;
- an **age limit** at subscription, or a contribution that changes with age;
- **country restrictions** (countries of residence covered, destination countries);
- **exclusions by cause** of death, to be read carefully in the general conditions.

These points are common but not universal: only your contract is binding.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **A contract that reimburses instead of paying directly**: in a bereavement, the family may not have the cash to advance costs of several thousand euros or dirhams.
- **Spending without informing the assistance line**: many contracts require contacting them **before any expense**, otherwise cover may be refused.
- **Mixing up funeral and repatriation**: a funeral plan may finance a local burial without covering transport to Morocco.
- **Unpaid contributions** to an association or mutual: rights may be suspended.
- **Not telling your family** that the contract exists and how to reach the assistance line. Keep a paper copy and an emergency number within reach.

## General rule, exceptions and points to confirm

**General rule**: cover is contractual. It depends on what you subscribed to, the provider's terms and regular payment of contributions.

**Possible exceptions**: associations or public bodies may provide help. For example, according to press reports of ministerial statements (2015), the ministry in charge of Moroccans residing abroad said it covers the transfer of remains for people without means; in April 2020, during Covid-19, it announced it would fund burials abroad for deceased people in precarious situations without repatriation insurance. The [mre.gov.ma](https://www.mre.gov.ma/fr/programmes/accompagnement-social) portal lists the "repatriation of the living and of remains" among its social support actions, without detailing conditions. On 10 October 2026, the mre.gov.ma page only confirmed that this action exists (“repatriation of the living and of remains”), with no conditions or contacts. Separately, according to Bladi.net (5 June 2025), the Minister of Foreign Affairs announced the forthcoming electronic issuance of authorisations to transport remains to Morocco; we found no official confirmation that it is in service.

**To confirm with the consulate**: current conditions of this help, exact documents, timelines, airlines accepting freight. The [Hassan II Foundation for Moroccans Residing Abroad](https://www.fh2mre.ma) also offers legal and social assistance; its units and contacts are on its website.
