Moroccans are known for their **hospitality**. A few simple customs will help you build real connections, avoid blunders and enjoy your stay fully. Here's the practical guide.

<Callout type="tip" title="The 7 etiquette reflexes">
- **Always greet first** before asking for something: *salam alikoum*.
- **Right hand** for eating, giving and receiving.
- **Accept the tea:** it is a welcoming gesture.
- **Suitable dress** outside beach resorts: shoulders and knees covered, especially in religious places.
- **Ask before photographing** a person.
- **Discretion** during Ramadan (eating, drinking and smoking in the street by day).
- **Calm and a smile:** raising your voice is badly perceived.
</Callout>

## Greetings and conversation

- People greet at length, asking for news. *Salam alikoum* (reply: *wa alikoum salam*), *labas?* ("how are you?").
- A **handshake**, often followed by a hand to the heart. Between men and women, let the other person take the initiative.
- Languages: **Arabic, Darija, Amazigh** (Tamazight) and **French**. A few Darija words open doors: [Darija for travellers](/en/darija-phrases-for-travellers).

## Being invited to a Moroccan home

- **Bring** pastries, fruit or flowers.
- **Take off your shoes** if your host does.
- You'll be served **tea and generous dishes:** taste, say thank you. See [Moroccan food](/en/moroccan-food-dishes-to-try).
- Offering help is appreciated; insisting on paying for the invitation is not.

## Dress

In big cities and tourist resorts, outfits are varied. In medinas, villages and religious places, favour **covering** and loose clothing. A light scarf is useful for women in some contexts.

## The calendar that matters

- **Friday:** day of congregational prayer, family couscous, some shops close midday.
- **Ramadan:** changed hours, lively evenings: see [public holidays and Ramadan 2027](/en/morocco-public-holidays-ramadan-2027).
- **Eid:** mass travel, higher prices.

## Photos and privacy

Don't photograph people without consent, nor official or military buildings. Avoid drones: see [customs](/en/morocco-customs-what-to-bring).

## Money, tips, bargaining

Haggle with a smile in the souks, tip modestly: [tipping](/en/tipping-in-morocco). Avoid traps: [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers).

## Good to know about the law

The Moroccan Penal Code penalises **sex outside marriage** and **same-sex relations**. Tourists should stay **discreet** and know these rules, and hotels may ask Moroccan–foreign couples for proof of marriage. Check your own country's travel advice.

<Callout type="info" title="A plural country">
Morocco is diverse, from cosmopolitan cities to mountain villages and oases. Observe, ask, and let courtesy guide you: it is appreciated everywhere.
</Callout>
