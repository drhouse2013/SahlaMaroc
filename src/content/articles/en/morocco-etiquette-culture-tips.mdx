Moroccans are known for their **hospitality**. A few simple customs will help you build real connections, avoid blunders and make the most of your stay. This guide suits the passing tourist as well as the expat or returning Moroccan abroad. It describes common customs, not uniform rules: the country is plural, between cosmopolitan cities, mountain villages and oases.

<Callout type="tip" title="The 7 etiquette reflexes">
- **Always greet** before asking for something.
- **Right hand** for eating, giving and receiving.
- **Accept the tea**: it is a gesture of welcome.
- **Suitable dress** outside resorts: shoulders and knees covered in religious places and villages.
- **Ask before photographing** a person.
- **Discretion** by day during Ramadan.
- **Calm and a smile**: raising your voice is badly perceived.
</Callout>

## Greetings and conversation

- People greet at length and ask for news: *salam alikoum* (reply: *wa alikoum salam*), *labas?* ("how are you?"). See [darija for travellers](/en/darija-phrases-for-travellers).
- The **handshake** is common, often followed by a hand on the heart. Between men and women, let the other person take the initiative.
- Spoken languages: **darija, Tamazight** (an official language) and **French** in cities. A word in the other's language changes the atmosphere.
- Sensitive subjects (religion, monarchy, the Sahara) are handled with care and without judgement.

## Being invited to a Moroccan home

- **Bring** pastries, fruit or flowers.
- **Take off your shoes** if your host does.
- You will be served **tea and generous dishes**: taste, say thank you. See [Moroccan food](/en/moroccan-food-dishes-to-try).
- People often eat with bread and fingers; the **right hand** is the rule.
- Offering help is appreciated; insisting on paying for the invitation is not.
- For a family meal, arriving a little late is not seen as a fault, but let them know.

## Dress

Outfits vary by place: freer in resorts and big cities, more covering in medinas, villages and religious places. A light scarf serves women in some contexts; loose, breathable clothes suit everyone in summer. In a mosque that can be visited, follow posted instructions; many mosques are closed to non-Muslims.

## The calendar that matters

- **Friday:** day of congregational prayer and family couscous; some shops close at midday.
- **Ramadan:** changed hours, calmer days, lively evenings after the fast is broken. Be discreet by day in front of those fasting. See [public holidays and Ramadan 2027](/en/morocco-public-holidays-ramadan-2027).
- **Eid:** mass travel, higher prices, shops closed for a few days.
- **Legal time:** GMT all year since September 2026; see [time zone](/en/morocco-time-zone-gmt).

## Photos and privacy

Do not photograph people without consent, especially women, children and craftsmen at work; a small sum is sometimes asked, to negotiate beforehand. Avoid photographing official and military buildings. Drones require authorisation: see [customs](/en/morocco-customs-what-to-bring).

## Money, tips and haggling

Haggle with a smile in the souks, give modest tips: [tipping](/en/tipping-in-morocco). Avoid common traps: [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers). For traditional baths, see [hammam](/en/moroccan-hammam-guide-prices).

## Legal framework to know

Some local rules differ from those of many home countries. The sources read for this article (Wikipedia, Moroccan press, NGO publications) indicate in particular that the **Penal Code** punishes **sex outside marriage** (article 490) and **same-sex relations** (article 489), and sanctions **openly breaking the fast in public** during Ramadan (article 222) for people of Muslim faith. These sources date from 2019 to 2021 and **could not be confirmed against an up-to-date official text**; reform is regularly debated. Before leaving, read your own country's travel advice (for example [France Diplomatie](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/conseils-aux-voyageurs/conseils-par-pays-destination/maroc/)) and, for the texts, the [General Secretariat of the Government](https://www.sgg.gov.ma). In practice, be discreet in public and ask your accommodation about its conditions for couples.

## For returning Moroccans abroad

Family and village customs have their own codes. When you return, observe, ask, do not compare: as much restraint as warmth is expected. To organise your return, see the [permanent return guide](/en/permanent-return-morocco-mre-guide).
