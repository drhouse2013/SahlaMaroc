**Toubkal (4,167 m)** is the highest peak in North Africa, in the High Atlas, about an hour and a half by road from Marrakech. The standard climb takes **two days** from the village of **Imlil**, with a night in a refuge. It is accessible to fit walkers in summer, but altitude, weather and snow are not negotiable. This guide gives you the schedule, the preparation and the questions to ask before you book.

<Callout type="tip" title="The essentials">
- **Format:** 2 days, night at the refuge at about 3,207 m.
- **Start:** Imlil, at about 1,740 m, then the climb to the refuge; day two is the summit and descent.
- **Level:** moderate to demanding, significant ascent, no technical difficulty in summer conditions.
- **Best period:** late spring to autumn; winter is a proper mountaineering outing.
- **Prices and walking times:** variable, confirm in writing with the operator.
</Callout>

## Day-by-day itinerary

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Day before" title="Marrakech → Imlil, acclimatisation night">

Reaching Imlil from Marrakech takes about an hour and a half by road, the last part up the valley. Arriving the day before and sleeping in Imlil or Aroumd lets you start fresh and begin acclimatising. See [where to stay in Marrakech](/en/where-to-stay-in-marrakech) for the night before the transfer.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Day 1" title="Imlil → Toubkal refuge">

Leave Imlil, pass through Aroumd, then climb steadily up the valley to the **Toubkal refuge** (also called the Neltner refuge, run by the Club Alpin Français) at about 3,207 m. Large bags are often carried by mule; you keep a day pack. Expect a good part of the day on foot: the exact time depends on your pace, ask your guide.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Day 2" title="Summit, then back to Imlil">

Leave early, often before dawn, to enjoy the cool. The trail becomes stony then crosses scree. At the top the view takes in the High Atlas. The descent returns to the refuge then Imlil the same day, with a break at the refuge. Keep energy for the descent, which is often more tiring than expected.

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## When to go

- **Late spring to autumn:** normal walking conditions for a high-altitude trek; September-October often balance weather and crowds, with no guarantee.
- **Late winter and spring:** snow and ice possible. Crampons and an ice axe may be needed, with an experienced guide.
- **Winter:** a full mountaineering outing. Do not attempt it without experience.

Conditions change from year to year: ask the refuge or your operator about snow cover a few days beforehand.

## Guide, refuge and organisation

- **Local guide:** several travel sources say a local guide is required or strongly recommended, but we found no clear official text. Confirm the current rule with the operator or local authorities. A guide is useful for weather, navigation and safety.
- **Refuge:** the Toubkal refuge is run by the Club Alpin Français; another refuge, Les Mouflons, is nearby. Check booking terms and what is provided (blankets, meals).
- **Guided package:** ask in writing what is included: transfer from Marrakech, guide, mule, refuge night, meals, tea, insurance.

## Difficulty level

The trek is demanding through **duration** and **altitude** more than technique: several hours of uphill walking, thinner air and cold nights. A regular walker used to multi-hour mountain days manages in summer. Without training, knees, breathing and altitude sickness complicate the descent. Prepare a few weeks ahead with walks involving ascent.

## Altitude and safety

- Climb **at your own pace** and drink regularly.
- **Altitude sickness** is possible from about 3,000 m: headache, nausea, unusual fatigue. If symptoms worsen, go down.
- **Weather:** check forecasts and turn back if conditions deteriorate; storms and ridge wind are serious.
- **Insurance** covering high-altitude hiking and repatriation: check it. See [health for travellers](/en/health-morocco-travellers-insurance-emergencies).

## What to pack

- Hiking boots **already worn in**, spare socks
- Warm layers (light down jacket, fleece) and a waterproof jacket, even in summer
- Gloves, hat, headlamp with batteries
- Water (or purification), snacks, sunscreen, sunglasses
- Walking poles, first-aid kit, sleeping bag depending on the refuge
- Crampons and ice axe only if the season requires, with a guide

## Common mistakes

1. **Underestimating the descent**, longer and more tiring than planned.
2. **Setting off in new boots** and coming back with blisters.
3. **Ignoring the cold**: it is cold at altitude at night even in summer.
4. **Climbing too fast** on day one and arriving exhausted.
5. **Ignoring the weather** because you have booked and paid.
6. **Choosing on price alone** without asking what is included or how many people per guide.

## How to choose an operator without promoting a name

We recommend no agency. Ask: the guide's name and qualification, participants per guide, a written list of inclusions (transport, mule, night, meals), the policy in bad weather, insurance, whether you can stop if you are ill. Cross-check recent reviews on several platforms and be wary of abnormally cheap offers.
