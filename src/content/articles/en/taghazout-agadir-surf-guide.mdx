Taghazout, a small fishing village north of Agadir, has become a Moroccan surf landmark. Rocky points, sandy beaches, schools and camps for every level, a relaxed atmosphere: here is how to choose your spots, season and package without picking the wrong level. This guide covers surfing; for the country's other gliding sport, see [Dakhla and kitesurfing](/en/dakhla-kitesurf-guide).

<Callout type="tip" title="In brief">
- **Beginners:** favour small-wave periods (spring, autumn, summer) and suitable spots with a school.
- **Advanced:** winter brings more swell on the rocky points.
- **Lessons or camp:** compare inclusions (gear, transport, meals, insurance).
- **Prices:** variable, not fixed here; ask for a written quote.
</Callout>

## The spots

| Spot | Level | Good to know |
|---|---|---|
| **Devil's Rock (Tamraght)** | Beginners | More forgiving setting for first waves, according to schools |
| **Banana / sandy beaches** | Beginner to intermediate | Sandy bottoms, more accessible |
| **Hash Point** | Intermediate | A stepping stone towards reef waves |
| **Anchor Point** | Advanced | Long right-hander over reef, needs swell and experience, very active locals in the lineup |
| **Killer Point** | Advanced | Powerful wave below the cliff, access via a rocky channel |

A spot's real level changes with swell, tide and wind. **Respect the locals and priorities** in the water, especially on advanced points. If in doubt, ask a local instructor.

## When to go

- **Spring and autumn:** waves generally more manageable and fewer crowds, but conditions vary.
- **Winter:** more powerful North Atlantic swells, mostly for advanced surfers.
- **Summer:** usually smaller waves, suited to initiation.

The Moroccan National Tourist Office notes the sun is present all year in the Agadir-Taghazout area, softened by the trade winds. Check swell forecasts before choosing dates.

## Lessons, gear and camps

- **Lessons:** group or private, with wetsuit and board generally provided; ask the students-per-instructor ratio.
- **Surf camps:** accommodation, lessons and sometimes meals; compare what is included and the group's level.
- **Certified instructors:** favour qualified instructors, equipment in good condition and a group suited to your level.
- **Insurance:** check that your travel insurance covers surfing. See [health for travellers](/en/health-morocco-travellers-insurance-emergencies).

## Level and fitness

Surfing demands endurance and good health: paddling, standing up and handling the shore break tire you fast. Being able to swim is essential. Beginners generally do their first sessions with an instructor, over a sandy bottom.

## What to pack

- Swimsuit, UV rash vest, water-resistant sunscreen
- Wetsuit for the season, if your school does not provide one
- Quick-dry towel, flip-flops, hat
- First-aid kit, protection against sea urchins on rocky bottoms
- Cash for small purchases: see [tipping](/en/tipping-in-morocco)

## Common mistakes

1. **Overestimating your level** and heading to an advanced point.
2. **Neglecting the swell** and the day's currents.
3. **Choosing a camp on price alone** without checking students per instructor.
4. **Not respecting priorities** in the water.
5. **Forgetting insurance** that covers surfing.
6. **Underestimating the sun**, even when overcast.

## How to choose a school without promoting a name

We recommend no school. Check: instructor qualifications, students per instructor, equipment condition, bad-weather policy, included insurance, and recent detailed reviews.
