For many MRE families (Moroccans living abroad), the plane ticket is the biggest cost of the summer trip home, and the easiest to get wrong: an attractive "from" price can double once bags and options are added. This guide explains how to go about it, with a map of the airlines and routes **confirmed by announcements published in 2026**. No fare is guaranteed here: prices change constantly.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- Always compare the **final price with a bag**, not the headline price.
- Check **several airports** at both ends. For the north-east (Nador, Oujda, Al Hoceima) or the north (Tangier), a direct flight is often more practical than flying into Casablanca.
- Avoid peaks (summer, Eid, school holidays) if you can: tickets sell out sooner and cost more. If you can't avoid them, **book early**.
- Check the fare conditions (bags, changes, refunds) **before you pay**.
- Checked on 9 October 2026: routes change from season to season, so confirm on the airline's own site.
</Callout>

## Choosing your Moroccan arrival airport

According to ONDA (the national airports authority), Moroccan airports handled about **22.28 million passengers from January to July 2026** (+8.77% year on year). The five busiest were Casablanca Mohammed V, Marrakech-Ménara, Agadir-Al Massira, Tangier Ibn Battouta and Rabat-Salé.

- **Big city and connections:** Casablanca (CMN) is Royal Air Maroc's hub; Rabat (RBA) is served by several low-cost carriers.
- **Tourist cities and beach resorts:** Marrakech (RAK), Agadir (AGA), Essaouira (ESU): well served by low-cost airlines, with routes from several regions of France, Germany and central Europe (see the table above).
- **North and north-east for MRE:** Tangier (TNG), Nador (NDR), Oujda (OUD), Al Hoceima (AHU), Tetouan (TTU). If your family comes from this region, a direct flight to the nearest airport can save a long journey from Casablanca. Royal Air Maroc, Vueling and Ryanair announce routes there; check them before you plan.

To get from the airport to town, see [Morocco airports to the city centre](/en/morocco-airports-to-city-centre). From France, Spain or Italy, the [ferry](/en/ferry-to-morocco-guide) remains an option if you are bringing the car.

## When to fly: high and low demand

- **High demand:** summer, when MRE return in large numbers (Operation Marhaba 2026 ran from 10 June to 15 September, peaking in late July and early August: see the [Operation Marhaba guide](/en/operation-marhaba-guide)), European school holidays (calendars vary by country and region: check the official ones), long weekends and religious holidays.
- **Eid and Ramadan:** Eid al-Adha and Eid al-Fitr are travel peaks. Dates depend on the moon and are confirmed at the last moment: see the expected 2027 dates in our guide to [public holidays and Ramadan 2027](/en/morocco-public-holidays-ramadan-2027).
- **Usually lower demand:** outside holidays, on weekdays, in autumn and winter. Nothing is guaranteed: a local event can push prices up.

## Comparing without getting caught out

1. **Use a comparison site to spot options, then check the airline's own site.** Comparison tools show fares collected at a given moment; Royal Air Maroc's own page says the displayed fares were collected "in the last 48 hours" and may no longer be available, and that optional extras may carry extra charges.
2. **Price calendar and flexible dates:** shift by a day or two and compare weekdays with weekends; the gap varies by route and season.
3. **Nearby airports:** a different departure city (Charleroi instead of Brussels, Orly instead of Roissy, and so on) or arrival airport can cut the price, but add the cost of getting there.
4. **One-way or return:** compare both. Two one-ways on two airlines are sometimes cheaper, but you lose the protection of a single contract.
5. **Price alerts:** useful for tracking one specific route.
6. **Airline or agency:** an agency adds a middleman for any change or refund. Know who answers if something goes wrong.
7. **Cookies and dynamic pricing:** fares can change during your search. Compare within the same session, and don't trust rumours about "the best day to book".

## Bags and fees: what really changes the price

Rules differ by airline **and by fare**. A documented example: on its site, Royal Air Maroc lists **one 23 kg checked bag** in economy on most routes, but states that the "Eco essentiel" fare includes **no checked baggage**; the maximum weight per bag is 32 kg, and the first extra bag on Europe–Morocco is listed at €70. The page has some incomplete tables, so **check at booking**.

With low-cost airlines the points to check are almost always the same: cabin bag and personal item size and weight, price of a checked bag online versus at the airport, seat selection, airport check-in and payment fees. All of these can change: **check at booking**.

On EU rules, the European Parliament adopted a position in January 2026 calling for two free cabin bags, but it is **not yet law** (negotiations with the Council are ongoing). Until then, each airline's own rules apply.

## Booking conditions to read before paying

- **Changes and cancellation:** basic fares can be inflexible or non-refundable. Look at the fees before choosing.
- **Connections:** a single ticket and two separate tickets do not protect you in the same way if you miss a connection. Ask the airline what is covered, and don't assume you will be looked after between two separate tickets without checking.
- **Delays, cancellations, lost bags:** see the passenger rights on the official [Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/passenger-rights/air/index_en.htm) portal (compensation of €250, €400 or €600 depending on distance in some cases, baggage liability limit of about €1,300, written complaint within 7 days for loss or damage). Rules depend on the route and the airline.
- **Travel insurance:** it can cover cancellation or medical costs; read what is excluded. See also [health and insurance in Morocco](/en/health-morocco-travellers-insurance-emergencies).
- **Entry documents:** passport or CNIE for Moroccans, passport (and a visa depending on nationality) for others. Rules depend on your nationality: see the [90-day rule](/en/morocco-visa-90-day-rule), the [e-Visa guide](/en/morocco-e-visa-guide), and your Moroccan consulate's site.

## Price-comparison traps

- **"From" prices:** the minimum on certain dates, rarely what you will pay.
- **Bag not included:** compare with a bag.
- **Taxes added at the end:** in Europe, Regulation (EC) No 1008/2008 requires the **final price** to be shown, including taxes and unavoidable charges, and optional extras must be actively accepted, not pre-ticked ([Article 23, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32008R1008)). Be wary if it isn't clear.
- **Intermediary agencies and fake comparison sites:** check the seller's name, address and independent reviews, and never pay by bank transfer to a stranger.
- **Pre-ticked options** (insurance, priority) to untick.

## Checklist before you buy

1. Date, airport, bags: is everything included?
2. Final price shown in the currency you pay in?
3. Change and cancellation conditions read?
4. Entry documents and passport validity checked?
5. Transport to the airport arranged? For a SIM on arrival: [SIM and eSIM in Morocco](/en/best-sim-card-esim-morocco). For customs: [what to bring](/en/morocco-customs-what-to-bring). For the rest of the trip home: the [summer return checklist for MRE](/en/summer-return-checklist-mre).
