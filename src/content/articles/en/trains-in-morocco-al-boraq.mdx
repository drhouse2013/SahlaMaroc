Morocco has the **first high-speed line in Africa**. With the intercity trains of the national operator **ONCF**, it is often the best way to travel between big cities, whether you are a visitor or an MRE coming home with family and avoiding the road. This guide gives the useful landmarks and flags what must be confirmed on the ONCF website, whose pages our tools could not read.

<Callout type="tip" title="Journey times: orders of magnitude">
The times below are **indicative** and vary with train type and stops. Confirm them on [oncf-voyages.ma](https://www.oncf-voyages.ma) or at the station.
- **Tangier → Rabat:** about 1 h 20 on Al Boraq
- **Tangier → Casablanca:** about 2 h 10 to 2 h 20 on Al Boraq
- **Casablanca → Marrakech:** about 2 h 40 (conventional train)
- **Casablanca → Fes:** about 3 h 30 to 4 h
</Callout>

## Al Boraq, the high-speed train

Opened in November 2018, **Al Boraq** runs up to 320 km/h on the high-speed section between Tangier and Kenitra, then on upgraded tracks to Rabat and Casablanca. For summer 2026, media reported an ONCF plan of about 30 daily runs, 6 am to 10 pm, with an hourly frequency on Tangier, Kenitra, Rabat and Casablanca (check the day's timetable). The **Kenitra–Marrakech extension** has been under construction since April 2025, with service announced for late 2029: see [World Cup 2030 transport](/en/world-cup-2030-morocco-transport).

## Intercity trains

Air-conditioned trains link Casablanca, Rabat, Fes, Meknes, Marrakech, Oujda and other cities. For busy weekends and holidays, choose a **reserved** seat where offered, as trains fill up.

## Steps to travel by train

1. **Choose route and date** on the ONCF site or app.
2. **Compare** train type (Al Boraq, Al Atlas, TNR), class and fare formula (flexible fares cost more than low-price ones).
3. **Buy the ticket** online for an e-ticket with a QR code, or at the counter.
4. **Keep a margin:** arrive at the station 15 to 20 minutes early; a security check comes before the platforms.
5. **Board the right car** (car and seat shown on the ticket when reservation applies).
6. **Keep your ticket until you exit.**
7. **Plan onward travel:** taxi, tram or bus from the station (see [tram and BRT](/en/tram-brt-casablanca-rabat-guide) and [taxis and apps](/en/taxi-apps-morocco)).

## Fares: what is confirmed

No 2026 fare could be read on the ONCF pages. At the 2018 launch the press announced a Casablanca–Tangier range of 149 to 224 MAD in second class (depending on demand), but those figures are old and do not apply today. ONCF offers reduced fares ("Semi-Flex" and "Flex" formulas, a child fare and a student card according to its pages as read by the press): **check conditions and amounts at booking**. To estimate your travel budget, see [cost of living](/en/cost-of-living-morocco).

## The Casablanca airport train

A rail shuttle links **Mohammed V airport** to Casa-Voyageurs and Casa-Port stations in roughly 30 to 35 minutes, with about one departure an hour. A second-class ticket costs 50 MAD (70 MAD in first) according to ONDA: the cheapest way into town. See our [airport-to-centre guide](/en/casablanca-airport-to-city-centre) and the [ONDA](https://www.onda.ma) site.

## Common mistakes

- Arriving at the last minute: the security check eats time.
- Buying in a rush without comparing formulas: a flexible fare only pays off if you can change.
- Assuming the train serves Essaouira, Agadir or Chefchaouen: it does not, you need a bus.
- Forgetting that Moroccan time is GMT all year since September 2026: check the departure time (see [time zone](/en/morocco-time-zone-gmt)).
- Not anticipating holidays: trains are full around Eid and in summer.

## Where trains don't go

For Essaouira, Chefchaouen, Agadir or the desert, take **Supratours** or **CTM** buses (see [CTM and Supratours buses](/en/ctm-supratours-bus-morocco)) or a tour: [Essaouira](/en/essaouira-day-trip-from-marrakech), [desert](/en/marrakech-to-merzouga-desert-tour).

## FAQ

**Must I book ahead?** Recommended on weekends, in summer and around holidays; the press has also reported sales opening several months ahead: confirm on the site.

**Can I take my luggage?** Yes, within reason; precise rules are on the ONCF site.

**Is there Wi-Fi?** Not confirmed; plan a SIM or eSIM: [SIM guide](/en/best-sim-card-esim-morocco).

**Where do I find today's timetable?** [oncf-voyages.ma](https://www.oncf-voyages.ma) or the ONCF app; ONCF changes timetables during high-speed line works.

## Three typical journeys

**Tangier–Casablanca on Al Boraq.** Book ahead in high season, arrive at the station early, compare fare formulas and keep the ticket until you exit. From the station, continue by tram or taxi.

**Casablanca airport to Rabat.** Take the rail shuttle to Casa-Voyageurs, then a train to Rabat: allow a margin for the connection.

**An MRE with family.** The train is comfortable for children, but bags must be carried to the platforms: with many suitcases or bulky gifts, prefer a grand taxi or a rental car ([guide](/en/car-rental-morocco-guide)).

## Extra questions

**Can I carry a bicycle?** Not confirmed: ask ONCF.

**Is there a dining car?** Not confirmed; bring snacks and water.

**What if the train is late?** Ask ONCF staff; compensation conditions are not detailed here.

## Good to know

Station names can be confusing in big cities: check whether your train stops at the main station or a secondary one before buying. Allow extra time at busy stations, and watch your belongings in crowded cars. Keep your phone charged for the e-ticket QR code.

## Checklist

1. Timetable and duration confirmed on the ONCF site.
2. Ticket bought online or at the counter.
3. Arrive at the station 15 to 20 minutes early.
4. Ticket kept until exit.
5. Onward transport planned.
