Selling or renting a property in Morocco from abroad triggers two separate taxes: the **tax on real estate profits** (when you sell) and **income tax on rental income** (when you let). Well prepared, both can be calculated in advance. Badly anticipated, they can also block the repatriation of your money.

<Callout type="tip" title="The essentials in 5 lines">
- **Sale:** **20 %** tax on the **net profit**, with a **minimum of 3 % of the sale price**.
- **Filing:** within **30 days** of the sale; the notary usually handles it at signing.
- **Main residence:** possible exemption under conditions (at least **5 years** of occupancy; above **4,000,000 MAD**, the excess part remains subject to the 3 % minimum).
- **Rental:** progressive scale after a **40 % allowance**, or an option for a **20 %** rate with no allowance.
- **Repatriation:** keep proof of foreign-currency funding and the tax receipt.
</Callout>

## 1. Selling: the real estate profit tax

### How the profit is calculated

The taxable profit is the **sale price** minus the **revalued purchase price**, plus certain documented costs. Revaluation uses **coefficients published every year** in the finance law: the longer you hold the property, the more the coefficient for your purchase year reduces the taxable profit.

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Rate | **20 %** of net profit |
| Minimum tax | **3 %** of the sale price, even with no profit |
| Purchase price | Revalued with the coefficient of the purchase year |
| Deductible costs | Documented acquisition and sale costs |
| Filing | Within **30 days** after the sale |

**Order-of-magnitude example:** you sell for 1,200,000 MAD a property bought for 800,000 MAD. Without revaluation, the gross profit is 400,000 MAD, so 80,000 MAD of tax at 20 %. With revaluation and costs deducted the profit falls, but the tax cannot go below **36,000 MAD** (3 % of 1,200,000 MAD).

### The main-residence exemption

The sale of a **home occupied as your main residence** can be exempt if you meet **all** the following conditions:

- a **minimum occupancy period** (at least **5 years** at the date of sale);
- a property that was **neither let nor used for business** during the period;
- only one exemption per taxpayer over the period set by law.

If the price exceeds 4,000,000 MAD, the part above remains subject to the 3 % minimum tax.

### Repatriating the sale proceeds

The principle: you can transfer abroad what came in as foreign currency, **if you can prove it**. Gather before selling:

- the **purchase deed** and the **sale deed**;
- **proof of the original foreign-currency funding** (transfers, statements);
- the **receipt for the real estate profit tax**;
- the **declaration of the investment** to the Exchange Office, made within the deadlines.

This is why we recommend **always buying with traceable transfers**. See [buying property from abroad](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad) and [sending money to Morocco](/en/send-money-to-morocco).

## 2. Renting: tax on rental income

Your Moroccan rents are taxed **in Morocco**, even if you live abroad. Two options exist for individuals:

1. **The progressive income tax scale**, applied after a **flat 40 % allowance** on gross rent.
2. **A 20 % rate** on gross rent, **with no allowance** (worth modelling at your rent level).

Other points to know:

- **5 % withholding tax:** from **1 July 2026**, it applies to rents paid by **companies** or **professionals under the actual-profit regime**. It is credited against your final tax. If your tenant is a private individual, it does not apply.
- **Municipal services tax** (formerly urban tax): roughly **10.5 % or 6.5 %** of the rental value depending on the zone, payable yearly.
- **Residence tax** if the property is not let.
- **Short-term rentals** add specific rules: see [short-term rentals in Morocco](/en/short-term-rental-morocco-rules).

Keep **rent receipts**, leases and bank statements: they are your evidence in case of an audit.

## 3. Avoiding double taxation

Morocco has signed **tax treaties** with many countries (including France and Spain). They decide which country taxes your property income and how to avoid double taxation. Check the treaty between Morocco and your country of residence and declare your Moroccan rent **there too** when the treaty requires it.

## MRE owner checklist

<Checklist id="property-taxes" lang="en" title="Property taxes: my checklist">

- Purchase deed, cost invoices and foreign-currency payment proofs filed
- Revaluation coefficient for the purchase year identified (finance law)
- Profit tax simulated with the notary before setting the price
- Main-residence exemption eligibility checked
- Profit tax filed and paid within 30 days (or confirmed by the notary)
- Tax receipt kept for repatriating the funds
- Tax option for rental income chosen (scale or 20 %)
- Municipal services tax and residence tax up to date
- Tax treaty consulted for the declaration in your country of residence

</Checklist>
