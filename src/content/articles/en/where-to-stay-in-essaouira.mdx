Essaouira is a small Atlantic coastal town, ringed by ramparts, with a UNESCO-listed medina and a busy port. Here the accommodation choice rests on three criteria: **access** (pedestrian medina or road), **wind** (the trade winds behind its nickname) and **how long you stay**. The town is small, and walking covers almost everything.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **Atmosphere, ramparts and port on foot:** a riad in the medina.
- **Beach, pool, parking:** the beach area or the road south.
- **Wind sports or a quiet village:** Diabat or Sidi Kaouki, with a car.
</Callout>

## Medina: everything on foot, sheltered lanes

The Essaouira medina (see the [Essaouira city page](/en/cities/essaouira#spots)) is organised around Place Moulay Hassan and the Skala, with white and blue lanes. Riads are numerous. Walls soften the wind, and you reach the port, ramparts and souks on foot. In return: no car to the door, café and gull noise from early morning, and a few steep or uneven lanes. See [riad or hotel](/en/riad-or-hotel-morocco-choose) for access differences.

## Beach and seafront: space and air

Outside the medina, the long beach and seafront hotels offer larger rooms, a pool and parking. This is handy for families or with a car. The wind is stronger there: good for kitesurfing, less so for quiet sunbathing.

## Diabat: the village across the bay

South of town, Diabat is a village stretching towards the sea, with guesthouses and apartments. It suits those seeking calm, dunes and an open landscape. The catch: you need a taxi or car for dinner or to return to town.

## Sidi Kaouki: surf and wind spot

Further south, Sidi Kaouki is a coastal village with a wind-open beach. It draws board-sport fans and those who want to be alone. Check distance and transport from Essaouira before booking.

## When to come

The wind cools summer, but you will want a windbreaker even on full-sun days. Winter can be cool, damp and windy; some riads are poorly heated. During festivals (the Gnaoua festival is generally in June, dates to check) and holidays, book early.

## Access

From Marrakech or Agadir, the bus is the simplest option: [CTM and Supratours](/en/ctm-supratours-bus-morocco). If you come from Marrakech for the day, read [Essaouira day trip from Marrakech](/en/essaouira-day-trip-from-marrakech), then [where to stay in Marrakech](/en/where-to-stay-in-marrakech) and [where to stay in Agadir](/en/where-to-stay-in-agadir). For real wind country, see [Dakhla](/en/dakhla-kitesurf-guide).

## Safety and tips

Essaouira is generally relaxed, but watch your belongings on the beach and in the souk; follow swimming advice (currents). Confirm with your host where to meet and where to park a car. Read [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers). For remote work, see [best cities for nomads](/en/best-cities-digital-nomads-morocco).
