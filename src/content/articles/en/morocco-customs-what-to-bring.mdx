Most visitors pass Moroccan customs in minutes. A few items, however, cause real trouble at the airport or port. Here is what to check before you pack.

<Callout type="warning" title="Drones">
Importing a motorised remote-controlled aircraft is **prohibited without prior administrative approval**, even small camera drones. Customs can confiscate it at the airport. Leave it at home unless you have written authorisation.
</Callout>

## Medicines

- Medicines for **strictly personal use** can generally enter with the prescription or a medical certificate.
- Keep them in the **original packaging**, and carry the documents in your hand luggage.
- **Controlled substances** (certain psychotropics, strong painkillers) face stricter control. Check with the consulate or customs before you travel, and bring a letter from your doctor.

## Cash and currency

- The dirham is a closed currency. See [cash and ATMs in Morocco](/en/money-cash-atms-morocco).
- Foreign currency above the equivalent of **100,000 MAD** must be declared on arrival.

## Goods and gifts

Personal belongings and reasonable gifts for family are normally fine. Large quantities, new electronics in several copies or goods that look commercial may be taxed. When in doubt, ask customs (ADII) or use the red channel and declare.

## Your car

Bringing your car? Read the [180-day rule](/en/car-in-morocco-180-day-rule) before you cross.

## Packing checklist

1. Passport valid for your whole stay. See the [visa guide](/en/morocco-visa-90-day-rule).
2. Prescriptions and doctor's letters.
3. Insurance documents and, if driving, vehicle papers.
4. No drone, unless authorised.

<Callout type="info" title="Check the source">
Customs rules change. The official source is the Moroccan customs administration (ADII). This guide is general information, not legal advice.
</Callout>
