Most travellers pass Moroccan customs in minutes. A few items nonetheless cause real trouble at the airport or port, and an MRE returning with gifts, electronics and cash must know the thresholds. This article draws on the **MRE customs guide published by ADII (2025 edition)** and complements the [180-day rule guide](/en/car-in-morocco-180-day-rule) for cars.

<Callout type="warning" title="Drones">
Remote-controlled motorised flying objects (drones, model aircraft) are **subject to an import licence**, and cannot be imported among children's toys. Without prior authorisation, customs may hold them. Leave them at home unless you have written approval.
</Callout>

## What is banned or subject to formalities

The ADII guide distinguishes two categories.

**Strictly prohibited imports:** weapons and ammunition of war, narcotics, printed or recorded material contrary to public morals and order, counterfeit goods (on import and export).

**Imports subject to formalities:**

- **Animals and animal products:** veterinary certificate (ONSSA). See [travelling with children and pets](/en/travel-morocco-children-pets).
- **Plant products:** phytosanitary certificate (ONSSA).
- **Endangered species (CITES):** certificate from the Water and Forests department.
- **Hunting weapons and ammunition:** authorisation from the national police.
- **Industrial goods in commercial quantity** (new clothes or shoes, cooking appliances, washing machines…): market-access authorisation.
- **Books:** visa from the Ministry of Communication, except for personal use (sworn statement).
- **More than one telecom device:** ANRT type approval.
- **Goods under import licence:** drones, bladed weapons, etc.

## Medicines

- Medicines for **personal use** can benefit from the allowance: present the **medical documents** (prescription, medical certificate).
- At the entry customs office you sign an **undertaking** to use the medicines only for your personal needs and to **re-export any unused quantity** at the end of your stay. The form is provided by customs and available on its portal (individuals section, MRE, forms).
- Keep them in their **original packaging** and carry documents in hand luggage.
- Use other than personal is **subject to Ministry of Health authorisation** and payment of duties.
- For controlled substances (some psychotropics, strong painkillers) the guide gives no further detail: ask the consulate or customs before leaving, and carry a letter from your doctor. See also [health for travellers](/en/health-morocco-travellers-insurance-emergencies).

## Cash and currency

- **Entry:** currency in banknotes or bearer negotiable instruments requires a **written declaration** when it reaches the equivalent of **100,000 MAD**. Below that, declaring is possible and useful: it proves the origin of currency to credit a foreign-currency or convertible-dirham account, or to re-export it. For a non-resident, the import declaration is valid for six months at most.
- **Exit:** exporting currency of 100,000 MAD or more requires a declaration and must be justified (entry declaration, exchange slips). Failure to declare is punishable by a **fine equal to half the undeclared amount**.
- **Dirhams:** you may import and export up to **2,000 MAD** in Moroccan banknotes.
- **MRE:** an MRE may buy back from banks and export currency they repatriated and sold over the last twelve months, up to 100,000 MAD. See [money and ATMs](/en/money-cash-atms-morocco) and [transferring savings](/en/transfer-savings-morocco-mre).

## Personal effects, gifts and goods

- **Temporary admission of usual personal effects** (MRE on holiday): personal jewellery, portable musical instrument, laptop, wheelchair and orthopaedic accessories, light sports equipment, children's toys. They count as accompanied baggage and must **leave** at the end of the stay, otherwise duties and taxes apply.
- **Allowance on non-commercial items:** for an MRE working abroad, the value must remain **below 25,000 MAD per calendar year** (ADII 2025 guide), without concentrating it on a single type of item. For **permanent return**, a 30,000 MAD threshold and other allowances exist: see [permanent return](/en/permanent-return-morocco-mre-guide). The press reported a summer 2026 increase that we could not confirm in a text: check the threshold in force.
- **Excluded** from this allowance notably: furniture, household appliances, televisions, cycles; carpets are admitted only one at a time.
- Documents to present: residence card or proof of habitual residence abroad, and work contract, trader card or other proof of socio-professional status.
- **Goods for commercial purposes** or outside the allowance: customs declaration and payment of duties and taxes. If in doubt, use the declaration lane.
- **Visitor tax refund:** VAT refund on purchases of at least 2,000 MAD incl. VAT from the same seller on the same day, shown to customs **before boarding** with invoice and sales slip; refund through an approved provider within 3 months. Food, tobacco, medicines, weapons, vehicles and cultural goods are excluded.

## Checklist

<Checklist id="customs-travel" lang="en" title="Customs: my checklist before leaving">

- Passport valid for the whole stay (see the [visa guide](/en/morocco-visa-90-day-rule))
- No drone, unless authorised in writing
- Prescriptions and doctor's letters for medicines, customs undertaking planned
- Cash: 100,000 MAD threshold known, declaration prepared if needed, dirham banknotes limited to 2,000 MAD
- Gifts and purchases spread over several types of item, invoices kept
- Insurance documents and vehicle papers if you drive
- Proof of residence abroad (for MRE allowances)

</Checklist>
