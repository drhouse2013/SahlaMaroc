Al Hoceima is a seaside resort on the Mediterranean Rif coast, very popular in summer with families visiting from abroad. Choosing the right area saves pointless trips between beach, shops and port.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **Practical stay, on foot:** the **town centre**, close to shops and the sea.
- **Beach near the centre:** around **Quemado** and **Sfiha**, two beaches presented by the [Moroccan National Tourist Office](https://www.visitmorocco.com/en/travel/al-hoceima); hotel complexes are reported there according to [Wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hoceïma).
- **More secluded coves:** the **national park** side (Cala Iris, Badés): bring a car and supplies.
- **July-August:** heavy diaspora demand, book early.
</Callout>

## The areas

### Town centre

The town has three districts and one municipality. The centre is the simplest for a short stay without a car: hotels, restaurants, shops and taxis are here. It is lively in the evening in summer.

### Quemado, Sfiha and Cala Bonita

These are listed among the town's main beaches and seaside areas. Quemado and Sfiha are described as hotel complexes (Sfiha with a corniche and an archaeological site). If you want to sleep near the sea, look at these first, then compare the real distance to the centre and car access.

### Al Hoceima National Park

Created in October 2004, the park covers 48,460 ha, of which 19,000 ha is marine, according to the [High Commission for Water and Forests](http://www.eauxetforets.gov.ma/Biodiversite/GestionBiodiversite/Pages/Parc-National-Hoceima.aspx) (limestone cliffs, dolphins, an osprey colony). Cala Iris and Badés are often cited among its beaches (source: Wikipedia, to be confirmed locally). It is a nature setting away from shops: suited to those with a car and some planning, not to a late arrival with tired children.

### Nearby villages

Ajdir and Imzouren are nearby localities. They suit you if your family is from there, but accommodation is more limited than in town.

## Which type of accommodation?

| Situation | Best option |
|---|---|
| A few nights, no car | Hotel in the centre |
| Family, several weeks | Furnished apartment with kitchen |

## Getting there

- **By air:** Cherif Al Idrissi airport (AHU), run by ONDA, 17 km from the town according to its [airport network page](https://www.onda.ma/Je-suis-Passager/Guide-du-voyageur/R%C3%A9seau-a%C3%A9roportuaire). According to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherif_Al_Idrissi_Airport), seasonal flights link notably the Netherlands and Belgium, and Royal Air Maroc Express links Casablanca. Routes change from season to season: confirm with the airlines. Our [airports guide](/en/morocco-airports-to-city-centre) explains city-centre transfers in general.
- **By ferry:** the port of Al Hoceima is linked to Motril (Spain); [Baleària](https://www.balearia.com/fr/routes-horaires/bateau-motril-al-hoceima) advertises 4 departures a week (a crossing of about 5 hours) without stating the season on the page consulted. Sources differ (Ferryhopper: seasonal link, at least two sailings a week in high season; Wikipedia: year-round line): check with the operator. See the [ferry guide](/en/ferry-to-morocco-guide) and [Operation Marhaba](/en/operation-marhaba-guide).
- **By car:** National Road 16, the "Mediterranean bypass", has improved access since 2007. According to Wikipedia, Nador is 126 km away; the [ONMT](https://www.visitmorocco.com/en/travel/al-hoceima) gives Tangier at about 290 km (5 h 10 by road). For Tanger Med, read the [motorway route](/en/tanger-med-motorway-tolls-route). Mountain roads call for caution, especially at night.
- **By bus:** see the [CTM and Supratours guide](/en/ctm-supratours-bus-morocco).

## Summer precautions

- **Book early:** summer concentrates diaspora demand.
- **Seasonal prices:** they rise in high season; check at booking, never assume.
- **Check the listing:** exact address, distance to the beach, water and air conditioning, recent photos.
- **Deposit and cancellation:** agree them in writing.
- **Beaches:** some coves have no services or easy access: plan for water, shade and the way back.
