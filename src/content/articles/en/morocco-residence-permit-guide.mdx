Beyond 90 days visa-free, or as soon as you settle, you need a **residence permit**. It is doable, but it takes a complete file and patience. Here is the typical path, with the documents to expect depending on your reason for staying.

<Callout type="tip" title="In short">
- The file is filed **in person, in Morocco**, at the **foreigners' office** of the police prefecture or station for your place of residence.
- Documents depend on the **reason**: work, study, marriage, retirement or sufficient resources.
- Reported timing ranges from **about five weeks to three months** depending on the city. Casablanca and Rabat are often slower.
- The reported **administrative cost** is modest (around a hundred dirhams).
</Callout>

## Before you start

- Enter Morocco legally with a valid passport and the **entry stamp**: your 90 visa-free days (depending on nationality) start on that date. See the [visa and 90-day guide](/en/morocco-visa-90-day-rule).
- Don't let your lawful stay lapse before filing: an **overstay** complicates everything.
- There is no "digital nomad visa" at present: remote workers stay on visa-free entry or apply for a residence permit depending on their situation.

## Documents usually required

The basic file includes:

- Valid **passport** and **entry visa** or entry stamp;
- **application form**;
- **passport photos** (usually four);
- **proof of address** in Morocco (lease, hosting certificate);
- **proof of the reason for stay**;
- **medical certificate** and **criminal record extract**;
- **proof of resources**;
- **tax stamp** for the fees.

### By reason for stay

| Reason | Specific documents |
|---|---|
| **Employee** | Work contract endorsed by ANAPEC, employer's certificate |
| **Student** | Enrolment certificate from an institution |
| **Marriage to a Moroccan** | Marriage certificate, spouse's ID documents |
| **Retiree / annuitant** | Proof of pension or regular income abroad, proof of regular fund transfers to a Moroccan account, valid health insurance |

## The 4-step path

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 1" title="Prepare and translate">

Gather originals and copies. Documents issued abroad often need an **apostille or legalisation** and **translation**. See [apostille and legalisation](/en/apostille-legalisation-documents-morocco).

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 2" title="File the application">

In big cities, an **online pre-application** is increasingly the norm. Then file the dossier at the **foreigners' office**. You receive a **receipt**, which proves your application is in progress.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 3" title="Wait for the card">

Allow from a few weeks to several months. Keep the receipt on you: it justifies your situation in the meantime.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 4" title="Collect and renew">

Collect the card, note its **expiry date** and start **renewal** before it lapses. Residence permits have a limited duration.

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## Common mistakes

- **Incomplete file:** most delays come from missing documents.
- **Insufficient proof of resources:** for retirement, prove **regular** income and **regular transfers** to a Moroccan account. See [opening a bank account in Morocco](/en/open-bank-account-morocco-mre).
- **Non-compliant translations:** have them done by a sworn translator.
- **Staying without a permit after your lawful stay ends:** risk of fines and trouble on departure.

<Callout type="warning" title="Rules evolve">
Foreigners' residence rules (Law 02-03 and its implementing texts) and prefecture practices change. Confirm the exact documents with the foreigners' office in your city or a professional. This guide is general information, not legal advice.
</Callout>
