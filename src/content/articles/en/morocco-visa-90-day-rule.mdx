Many travellers remember "90 days visa-free" without knowing where the rule comes from, or what happens on day 91. This guide starts from the text of the law, explains how to count your days and what to do to stay longer. A retiree trying out a Moroccan winter, a remote worker, the foreign spouse of an MRE or grandparents visiting their grandchildren are not in the same situation.

<Callout type="warning" title="Always check for your nationality">
The law sets the framework (90 days, regular travel document), but **the list of visa-exempt countries is not in the law**: it is set by the Moroccan authorities and changes. Check the website of the Moroccan consulate in your country before booking. This guide is general information, not legal advice.
</Callout>

## What the law actually says

Foreigners' stay is governed by **law 02-03** (dahir no. 1-03-196 of 11 November 2003). Read in the official text published in the Bulletin officiel (copy hosted by the Ministry of Justice):

- **Art. 3:** an arriving foreigner must present a valid passport (or other recognised travel document) at the border post and, where applicable, the required visa.
- **Art. 4:** checks may also cover **means of subsistence**, the reasons for the visit and repatriation guarantees; entry may be refused if these conditions are not met.
- **Art. 8 and 9:** any foreigner wishing to stay must apply for a **registration card**, which must be presentable within 48 hours; foreigners staying in Morocco for **90 days at most** with a regular travel document are exempt from applying (as are diplomatic and consular agents).
- **Art. 42:** exceeding the authorised period is punished by a **fine of 2,000 to 20,000 MAD** and **one to six months' imprisonment**, or only one of the two, except for force majeure or a recognised excuse; the penalty is doubled for repeat offences.

What the text does not say, so we cannot state: whether the 90 days are counted per entry or over a rolling period (like the Schengen 90/180 rule), and how the fine is applied in practice on departure. Do not transpose Schengen logic to Morocco.

## Entering Morocco

1. **Passport valid for your whole stay.** Airlines often ask for six months of validity: that is a prudent carrier practice, not a rule of Moroccan law as read here.
2. **Entry stamp.** Your days are counted from the stamped date. Photograph the page.
3. **Supporting documents.** Onward ticket, accommodation address and proof of funds are rarely requested, but article 4 allows checks: keep them on your phone.
4. **Visa or e-Visa if your nationality is not exempt.** See the [Morocco e-Visa guide](/en/morocco-e-visa-guide): it does not convert into a residence permit.

### Families of MRE

- **Foreign spouse of a Moroccan:** article 17 of law 02-03 provides a **residence card** for the foreign spouse, subject to regular entry and stay (unless exempted). A stay over 90 days must therefore be prepared: do not count on tolerance on the last day.
- **Children:** minors whose parent holds a residence title are covered by article 6; the outcome depends on their nationality. If your child is a dual national, ask the consulate which documents to show at entry before booking.
- **Retiree with dual nationality:** again, the nationality under which you enter determines the rules. We did not read an official text on how dual nationals are treated: ask the consulate.

## Is there a digital nomad visa?

**Not to our knowledge.** In October 2026 we found no nomad visa in law 02-03 or on the official portals consulted. Remote workers generally stay on the 90-day visa-free entry, or apply for a registration card if they settle (see below). If a recent announcement says otherwise, ask for the published text or the page of a Moroccan administration.

## Staying beyond 90 days: the options

| Option | Basis | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| **Registration card** | Law 02-03, art. 8, 10, 13 | 1 to 10 years depending on the reason; mentions "visitor", "student" or occupation. See the [residence permit guide](/en/morocco-residence-permit-guide). |
| **Residence card** | Art. 16 and 17 | After 4 years of regular uninterrupted residence, or as of right for certain profiles (foreign spouse of a Moroccan, for example). |
| **Leave and come back** | No text | Readmission is at the border officer's discretion. Do not build a long-stay plan on it. |

## The most common trap: day 91

- **Counting from your ticket** instead of the entry stamp.
- **Thinking an e-Visa extends your stay:** it is limited to a short stay and does not extend into a residence permit.
- **Waiting until the last moment** to apply for a card: the file (proof of address, apostilled documents, translations) takes time. See [apostille and legalisation](/en/apostille-legalisation-documents-morocco).
- **Assuming "everyone does border runs":** the text does not protect that practice.

## Before you go: checklist

<Checklist id="visa-90-days" lang="en" title="Visa and 90 days: my checklist">

- Nationality checked on the consulate website (visa, e-Visa or exemption)
- Passport valid for the whole stay, with a margin
- Stamped entry date photographed, 90-day deadline noted
- Onward ticket, accommodation and proof of funds on your phone
- Health insurance valid abroad
- If staying longer: reason chosen (family, retirement, student, work) and documents to prepare
- Digital copies of passport, insurance and lease

</Checklist>
