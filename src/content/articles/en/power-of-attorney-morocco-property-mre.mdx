A power of attorney lets someone sign **in your place** in Morocco. It is handy when you live abroad, but it is also one of the riskiest documents: one that is too broad or badly drawn can cost you an entire property, or be refused at the last minute. The **required form has just changed** (2026); here is how to secure it.

<Callout type="warning" title="What changed in 2026 (press source, text not re-read)">
According to the press, **law 41.25**, promulgated on 3 July and published in **Official Bulletin no. 7526 of 16 July 2026**, amends **article 4 of the Code of Real Rights**: operations transferring ownership, or creating, transferring, changing or extinguishing a real property right, must be drawn up **as an authentic deed**, unless otherwise provided, and the related **promise of sale** and **special power of attorney** are concerned, **on pain of nullity**. The certified-date deed drafted by a lawyer would no longer be provided for. The press adds that the text does not limit the authentic deed to notarial deeds alone. **We did not re-read the Official Bulletin**: before signing, ask your notary which form he accepts today.
</Callout>

## Types of power of attorney

| Type | What it is for | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Special** | One precise act: buy or sell **this** property | Low: the recommended one |
| **General** | Manage all your affairs | **High**: very broad powers, and not enough to sell |

**Always prefer the special power of attorney.** To sell, the powers must be express (a general mandate is not enough).

## How to make one from abroad

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 1" title="Ask the Moroccan notary which form is accepted">

Before any signature, ask in writing the **notary handling the transaction in Morocco**: the exact form required, mandatory mentions, accepted validity period, and whether he accepts a deed made in your country of residence. If needed, also check with the **land registry (ANCFCC)**.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 2" title="Choose how to make it">

- **In Morocco**, before an authorised professional (notary, or another professional whose intervention the text in force accepts), if you visit Morocco.
- **In your country of residence**, before a **local notary**, then **apostille** or legalisation, and translation if needed. See [apostille and legalisation](/en/apostille-legalisation-documents-morocco).
- **At the Moroccan consulate**: sources disagree on what it can do for a real-estate act. **Choose it only with the Moroccan notary's written agreement.**

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 3" title="Draft with the essential clauses">

See the list below. Have the Moroccan notary review the text before signing.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 4" title="Apostille, translation, shipping">

Have the deed apostilled, translated if needed by a sworn translator, then send the original by tracked means. Keep a **digital copy** and the shipping receipt. Allow **several weeks**.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 5" title="Follow up, then revoke">

Stay reachable during the transaction. At the end, **have the end of the mandate recorded** or revoke it in writing.

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## The essential clauses

A serious power of attorney states, in black and white:

- the **full identity** of principal and agent (national ID or passport);
- **the property concerned** (address, land title number, area);
- **the authorised acts**, and only those (promise, sale, signing at the notary, registration request);
- **for a sale: a minimum price** below which the agent cannot sell;
- **for a purchase: a maximum price**;
- **the bank account in your name** into which funds must be paid (never the agent's);
- **a short duration** (a few months) and the **end date**;
- a **ban on substituting** someone else without written consent;
- the **right of revocation**.

## Documents to prepare

- ID or passport of principal and agent (legible copies).
- Land title or recent ownership certificate of the property.
- The draft promise or deed, or failing that a precise description of the transaction.
- Proof of residence abroad, if the local notary asks.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **Giving a general power** "to do everything" to a cousin or friend.
- **Letting the agent collect the price**: require funds to arrive in your account.
- **A power with no end date**: a forgotten deed can be used years later.
- **Making a private-signature power of attorney** and seeing it refused on signing day.
- **Keeping no copy**: keep the shipping record and digital copies.
- **Not revoking** after use.
- **Trusting a blog article** (this one included) over the notary who will sign.

## What to do if in doubt

If the power of attorney is not accepted, or you suspect abuse, **revoke it immediately** in writing and inform the notary concerned and the land registry. For purchases read [buying property from abroad](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad); for inheritance, [property inheritance](/en/property-inheritance-morocco-mre); for a build, [building from abroad](/en/build-house-morocco-from-abroad-permit); for purchase aid, [Daam Sakane](/en/daam-sakane-housing-aid-mre).

- Accepted form confirmed in writing by the Moroccan notary
- Special power (one act, one property), never general
- Minimum (sale) or maximum (purchase) price stated
- Bank account in my name for the price
- Short duration and end date
- Apostille or legalisation, translation if requested
- Digital copies and shipping receipt kept
- End of mandate recorded or revocation made in writing
