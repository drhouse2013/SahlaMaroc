A power of attorney lets someone sign **in your place** in Morocco. It's handy when you live abroad, but it is also one of the riskiest documents: a dishonest agent or an overly broad power can cost you an entire property. Here's how to secure it.

<Callout type="warning" title="The basic rule">
For acts involving **real estate**, the power of attorney must follow a **strict form**. A badly drawn one is **refused** by the notary or by the **land registry (ANCFCC)**. **Before signing anything, ask your notary in Morocco which exact form they accept.**
</Callout>

## Types of power of attorney

| Type | What it's for | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Special** | One specific act: buy or sell **this** property | Low, the most recommended |
| **General** | Manage all your affairs | **High**: very broad powers |

**Always prefer the special power of attorney.**

## How to set it up from abroad

Several routes exist depending on the rules in force:

1. **Before a notary, adoul or authorised lawyer in Morocco**, if you pass through Morocco or can do it through a representative.
2. **Before an authority in your country of residence** (a foreign notary), then **legalisation or apostille** depending on the country, and translation if needed.
3. **At the Moroccan consulate** where you live: possible for some acts, but the rules for property acts have changed in recent years. **Check first** that the Moroccan notary and the ANCFCC accept this type of power of attorney.

Allow **a few weeks** between signature and use in Morocco. Plan generously.

## Essential clauses

A serious power of attorney states in black and white:

- the **full identity** of the principal and the agent;
- the **property concerned** (address, land title number);
- the **authorised acts**, and only those;
- **for a sale: a minimum price** below which the agent can't sell;
- **for a purchase: a maximum price**;
- the **bank account** in your name that must receive the funds (never the agent's account);
- a **short duration** (a few months);
- the **right to revoke**.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **Granting a general power** "to do everything" to a cousin or friend.
- **Letting the agent collect the price:** require the funds to arrive directly in your account.
- **No end date:** a forgotten power of attorney can be used years later.
- **No copy kept:** keep the delivery record and digital copies.
- **Not revoking** after use: have the end of the mandate recorded when the operation is over.

## What if in doubt?

If the power of attorney isn't accepted, or you suspect abuse, **revoke it immediately** in writing with the notary concerned and inform the land registry.
