Sleeping under the stars among the **Erg Chebbi** dunes near Merzouga is the trip many visitors remember for a long time. From Marrakech the road is long: the classic format is **3 days and 2 nights**, crossing the High Atlas, passing the kasbahs of the south and arriving at the dunes. This guide details the itinerary, preparation, season and how to choose a tour without falling for a programme too good to be true. It starts from **Marrakech**: if you leave from Fes, see the [Fes to Merzouga tour](/en/fes-to-merzouga-desert-tour).

<Callout type="tip" title="In brief">
- **3 days / 2 nights**: realistic minimum, with long driving hours each day. **4 days** if you want to breathe.
- **Shared or private tour:** shared is usually cheaper, private lets you choose stops and pace.
- **Season:** prefer autumn, winter and early spring; summer is very hot, winter nights cold.
- **Prices, timetables, mileage:** confirm in writing with the operator.
</Callout>

## Day-by-day itinerary

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Day 1" title="Marrakech → High Atlas → Ait Benhaddou → Dades valley">

Leave Marrakech early to cross the High Atlas by the **Tizi n'Tichka pass** (about 2,260 m), a spectacular hairpin road. Stop at **Ait Benhaddou**, an earthen ksar on the UNESCO list and famous film set: take time to climb to the communal granaries for the view. The route continues through Ouarzazate to the **Dades valley** for a first night in a hotel or guesthouse.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Day 2" title="Todra gorges → palm groves → Merzouga and bivouac">

In the morning, walk the **Todra gorges**, where the walls rise on both sides of a narrow stream. Then a long drive through palm groves and stony plains to **Merzouga**, with a stop in Rissani if you like. Late in the afternoon, reach the **bivouac** in the Erg Chebbi dunes by camel or 4×4 depending on the package: sunset, dinner, music, a night under the stars.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Day 3" title="Sunrise and return to Marrakech">

Climb a dune for sunrise, return to the village, breakfast then the long drive back to Marrakech. Expect long hours in the vehicle and an evening arrival; the exact duration depends on stops and traffic, confirm with your operator.

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

Erg Chebbi is a dune field extending over several tens of kilometres, with dunes that can reach around a hundred metres. For the village, places and accommodation, see the [Merzouga city page](/en/cities/merzouga) and [where to stay in Merzouga](/en/where-to-stay-in-merzouga).

## Best time

- **Autumn, winter, early spring:** daytime temperatures more bearable. Desert nights can be cold in winter.
- **Summer:** very hot in the south, with gruelling driving days. Avoid if you fear heat.
- **Snow:** the Tizi n'Tichka pass can be hit by snow in winter and complicate the road. Free cancellation is valuable.

Check the weather and road conditions just before leaving.

## Difficulty level

No technical difficulty, but the tour is **tiring**: long hours on the road, a few short walks (Ait Benhaddou, Todra), then the camel and the dune climb. People prone to motion sickness, young children and those who dislike long drives should choose 4 days.

## Choosing your tour well

- **Read recent reviews** about the driver, vehicle and camp, not just the overall rating.
- **Check the bivouac type:** standard camps with shared facilities or "luxury" camps with private tents and bathroom.
- **Ask for inclusions in writing:** dinners and breakfasts are often included, lunches rarely, entry fees (ksar, gorges) sometimes not.
- **Do not confuse** a cheap "bivouac" with an overcrowded camp: ask how many tents.
- **Free cancellation**: useful in winter or in case of illness.

We recommend no operator names. A serious operator answers your questions precisely and agrees to detail the programme.

## What to pack

- A **warm jacket** (even in spring) and a scarf against wind and sand
- Closed shoes, sunglasses, sunscreen
- Power bank: electricity may be limited at the bivouac
- **Cash** for lunches, tips to drivers and camel handlers: see [tipping](/en/tipping-in-morocco)
- A headlamp and a small bag for the camp night, the main luggage staying in the vehicle

## Common mistakes

1. **Underestimating the driving hours** and planning connections on the return day.
2. **Choosing on price alone**, risking overcrowded camps or shortened stages.
3. **Forgetting warm layers**: the night is cold in autumn and winter.
4. **Not asking about lunch** and entry fees, charged extra.
5. **Confusing Agafay and Merzouga**: the first is a stone desert, the second real dunes.
6. **Leaving no margin** if the pass is closed by snow.

## Short on time? The alternatives

The **Agafay desert**, close to Marrakech, offers a stone desert, a dinner and a camel ride in one evening: it is not the Sahara, but it is a good short option. See [day trips from Marrakech](/en/day-trips-from-marrakech). For the same destination from the north, see the [Fes to Merzouga tour](/en/fes-to-merzouga-desert-tour).
