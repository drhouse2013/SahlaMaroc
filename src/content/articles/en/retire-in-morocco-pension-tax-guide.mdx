Retiring in Morocco appeals as much to Moroccans coming home as to Europeans drawn by the climate and the cost of living. But "just" moving a pension involves **five separate topics**: the fund that pays you, tax, health cover, the right to stay and your budget. This guide complements the [permanent return guide](/en/permanent-return-morocco-mre-guide) and states, for each point, what an official source establishes and what still needs confirming.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **Your fund can keep paying** a retirement pension abroad, usually against proof that you are alive (a life certificate); the procedure depends on each fund.
- **Moroccan (MRE) or foreign is not the same path**: the official tax guide for Moroccans living abroad describes a favourable regime for foreign-source pensions transferred to Morocco; for a foreign retiree we could not confirm that it applies in the same way.
- **No tax rule is universal**: the treaty between Morocco and your home country can change the answer. Confirm with **your pension fund** and **a tax adviser**.
- **Do the budget before you leave**: the [retirement budget calculator](/en/retirement-budget-morocco) compares your estimated spending with the net pension you enter.
</Callout>

## Two profiles to tell apart

| | **Moroccan retiree (MRE) coming home** | **Foreign retiree settling in** |
|---|---|---|
| Main framework | Permanent return, MRE tax guide | Rules on foreigners' residence, your country's tax treaty |
| Pension taxation | Regime described in the 2026 MRE tax guide (see below) | To confirm with the tax authority (DGI) and a tax adviser: the MRE guide does not explain it for non-Moroccans |
| Residence | No permit: Moroccan ID and passport | [Residence permit](/en/morocco-residence-permit-guide) to apply for after arrival, plus the [90-day rule](/en/morocco-visa-90-day-rule) |
| Health | AMO if you have rights, or cover through your fund under a treaty | Private insurance, or cover through your fund if a treaty provides for it |

## 1. Having your pension paid in Morocco

**General principle**: pension funds generally pay pensions abroad, either into a bank account in your country of residence or into an account you keep at home. The rules (change of address, supporting documents, delay before the first payment, currency) are specific to each fund: **tell yours before you leave**, in writing, and keep the confirmation.

### The life certificate

To keep paying a pension abroad, a fund regularly asks for **proof of existence**. According to CLEISS (the French information centre on international social protection), for French pensions:

- the fund sends an **annual proof-of-existence form**; **without it, the pension stops being paid**;
- some retirees can use the biometric app "Mon certificat de vie"; otherwise the form can be completed by the **consulate** or **local authorities** and sent back; the **Info Retraite** portal can also be used;
- retirees living in nine European countries (including Germany, Belgium, Spain, Italy and the Netherlands) are generally exempt thanks to information exchange between states; **Morocco is not on that CLEISS list**, so expect an annual proof of existence.

In the other direction (a Moroccan pension paid to a resident of France), CLEISS says the basic pension is justified to the CNSS (Casablanca) before March, quoting the Moroccan registration number, and the supplementary pension (CIMR) each quarter with proof of residence. Check the deadlines with the fund.

<Callout type="info" title="German, Belgian, Spanish, Dutch fund…?">
We only detail procedures we could verify. For another fund, look for the "pension abroad" section of its website or its helpdesk. In Germany, the Deutsche Rentenversicherung has a page dedicated to Morocco.
</Callout>

## 2. Treaties: social security and tax

Two families of agreements coexist and should not be confused.

- **Social security agreement**: it governs how pensions are calculated and **paid abroad**, and sometimes health cover. Verified example: the France–Morocco convention of 22 October 2007 (in force on 1 June 2011 according to the CCME) is based on transferring benefits to the beneficiary's place of residence.
- **Double taxation treaty**: it says which country may tax which income. Morocco has signed them with many countries; the official list is on the DGI website (tax documentation, international conventions).

What the texts we consulted say about pensions:

| Country | Tax treaty | Pensions (text consulted) |
|---|---|---|
| France | Paris, 29 May 1970 (amending protocol of 18 August 1989) | Art. 17: pensions and life annuities are taxable only in the state where the recipient is tax-domiciled |
| Spain | Madrid, 10 July 1978 (BOE of 22 May 1985) | Art. 18: private pensions taxable only in the state of residence. Art. 19: public pensions in principle in the paying state (exception if the recipient is resident and a national of the other state) |
| Netherlands | Rabat, 12 August 1977 | Art. 19: private pensions taxable only in the state of residence; public pensions taxable by both states |
| Germany | In force since 8 October 1974 (according to GTAI) | Not verified |
| Belgium | Treaty exists (summary sheet on the DGI website) | Not verified: read the text in force |
| Italy | Signed in Rabat on 7 June 1972 (protocol of 28 May 1979) | Not verified |

These dates and articles are those of the published texts; **protocols or a more recent text may apply**. For Germany, Belgium and Italy we say nothing about pensions because we did not consult a source: read the treaty.

## 3. Tax residence

Your tax residence determines where your income is taxed. In Morocco, according to article 23 of the General Tax Code as reported by professional sources, tax domicile is tied to a permanent home, the centre of economic interests or a stay of **more than 183 days** (within any 365-day period, continuous or not). The sources we read do not present these criteria in the same way, and treaties can settle a conflict between two states. Remember two things:

- **the 183-day rule alone does not settle the question**: get a tax adviser's analysis;
- your home country applies **its own criteria**: you can remain tax resident there despite living in Morocco, and the treaty then breaks the tie, hence the value of an individual analysis (tax residence certificate).

See also the guide to [MRE taxes: what to declare](/en/mre-taxes-morocco-what-to-declare).

## 4. Morocco's regime for foreign-source pensions

The **tax guide for Moroccans living abroad, 2026 edition** (published by the ministry in charge of Moroccans abroad) describes, for anyone who "chooses to settle in Morocco" with a foreign-source pension:

- a **flat-rate allowance** on the gross taxable amount: **70%** on the part up to **168,000 DH** a year and **40%** above;
- an **80% reduction of the tax** due on the transferred pension, **provided** the pension is transferred to Morocco **permanently, in non-convertible dirhams**;
- to attach to the return: a **certificate of pension payments** (from the payer or equivalent) and a **bank certificate** showing the foreign-currency amount received and its dirham equivalent on the transfer date;
- an **annual global income return**, filed online with payment of the tax, **before 1 March** of the following year.

**What we cannot state**: this guide is addressed to MREs; we could not confirm, in the text of the General Tax Code, that a **foreign** retiree benefits on the same terms. Nor do we say what amount will be taxed or exempt in your case: it depends on the treaty, your tax residence and the nature of the pension (private or public). Amounts and conditions can change with every finance law.

Confirm with your pension fund and a tax adviser.

## 5. Health and health insurance

- **Retirees of the Moroccan scheme**: the CNSS manages AMO for private-sector retirees; CLEISS cites a minimum pension of 500 MAD a month to qualify. Confirm the current threshold with the CNSS.
- **French retirees**: according to CLEISS, if you have no entitlement under the Moroccan scheme, the France–Morocco convention allows cover in Morocco on the basis of your French pension. The **French fund that pays your pension** issues the registration form (SE 350-07) to present to the CNSS. Health contributions may be deducted depending on your situation; CLEISS states that people who are no longer French tax residents do not pay CSG, CRDS or CASA.
- **Other nationalities**: ask your health insurer about its rights abroad; otherwise take **private health insurance** covering Morocco (hospital stays, evacuation). See [health and insurance in Morocco](/en/health-morocco-travellers-insurance-emergencies).
- A **spouse** is not always covered: CLEISS notes, for example, that a spouse accompanying you on a temporary stay in France is not covered by the French scheme and advises taking out insurance.

## 6. Residence and housing

Foreign retirees apply for a **residence permit** in Morocco. The [residence permit guide](/en/morocco-residence-permit-guide) lists the documents generally requested for a retiree (proof of pension, proof of regular transfers to a Moroccan account, health insurance): **confirm these requirements with the foreigners' office in your city**, as we found no official text setting them. A Moroccan [bank account](/en/open-bank-account-morocco-mre) makes it easier to receive your pension.

Renting before buying is the prudent choice: test the city and the neighbourhood before committing. If you buy, see the guide to [buying property from abroad](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad) and [property inheritance](/en/property-inheritance-morocco-mre).

## 7. What does living cost?

Our estimates (unofficial, to be confirmed on the ground) give, for one person, roughly **8,000 to 10,000 MAD a month** on a budget lifestyle and **13,000 to 18,000 MAD** for a comfortable one, with large differences between cities: see the [cost of living in Morocco](/en/cost-of-living-morocco). Health, insurance and unexpected costs are often underestimated. The [retirement budget calculator](/en/retirement-budget-morocco) lets you edit every item and compare it with your net pension, without calculating any tax.

## Suggested order of steps

1. Contact your **pension fund**: payment abroad, documents, health cover.
2. See a **tax adviser**: tax residence, treaty, departure filing.
3. Do your **budget** and start with a trial rental.
4. Prepare your **documents** (certificates, legalisation: [apostille](/en/apostille-legalisation-documents-morocco)).
5. Open a **bank account** in Morocco and arrange the pension transfer.
6. Apply for your **residence permit** (foreigners) or prepare your [permanent return](/en/permanent-return-morocco-mre-guide) (MRE).
7. File your income declarations every year according to your situation.
