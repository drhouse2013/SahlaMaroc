Sleeping in Merzouga means choosing between **a night in the dunes** (bivouac or camp), **an auberge at the foot of the erg**, or a mix of both. This guide helps you decide and book without nasty surprises; the day-by-day itinerary is in our tour guides.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **First time, one night:** a **camp in the dunes** with a camel ride, dinner and breakfast.
- **Comfort and cold nights:** a camp with **bedded tents and private bathrooms** (often called "luxury"), or an **auberge** at the foot of the dunes.
- **Tight budget or not keen on a tent:** an **auberge or hotel** in Merzouga, then a day trip into the dunes.
- **Season:** spring and autumn; **winter** = very cold nights, **summer** = extreme heat.
</Callout>

## Where is Merzouga?

Merzouga is the gateway village to **Erg Chebbi** in south-east Morocco. According to Wikipedia it is about **50 km from Erfoud** and **562 km from Marrakech** (confirm for your own route). Le Routard gives **Rissani at about 40 km**, **Errachidia (airport) about 2 hours by road**, and buses from Marrakech or Fes in **roughly 9 to 10 hours**. Hassilabied is a small village before Merzouga (Wikivoyage).

For driving and choosing a tour, see [Marrakech to Merzouga](/en/marrakech-to-merzouga-desert-tour) and the [Fes to Merzouga tour](/en/fes-to-merzouga-desert-tour); we do not repeat them here.

## Three ways to sleep

### 1. Bivouac or camp in the dunes

Formats range from basic to very luxurious, usually with a traditional dinner and music around a fire (Le Routard). The classic package cited by Hespress includes the **camel ride, dinner, breakfast** and entertainment. Some camps lie further out with more refined tents.

- **Standard camps:** shared tents or structures, shared bathrooms.
- **"Luxury" camps:** tents with a bed, sometimes a private bathroom; pricier and often deeper in the dunes. "Luxury" is not a regulated term: ask for details and recent photos.
- **Camps at the edge of the erg or deeper in:** check the distance from the village and how you get there.

### 2. Auberge or kasbah hotel in Merzouga

Wikivoyage describes about fifty small places along the dunes, from mud-brick houses to higher-end hotels. Upside: **a real bed, a real shower, heating or air-conditioning**, and you can still ride a camel at sunset without sleeping in a tent. Downside: you lose a bit of the feeling of being "in" the desert.

### 3. Mixed formula

Many hotels and auberges offer both rooms and a camp a few kilometres away. Useful if you want one night in a tent and one in comfort.

## What to check before booking

Make a list, because this is where misunderstandings happen:

- **What is included:** meals (dinner and breakfast? water?), tea, transfer, camel, a **shower** after the ride, private or shared bathrooms.
- **Transport to camp:** **camel** (the most common choice according to Hespress, less tiring and cheaper than a 4x4) or **4x4**. According to [Infoglobe](https://www.infoglobe.cz/en/traveller-guide/morocco-merzouga-staying-over-night-in-desert) the camel ride takes about an hour and starts one to two hours before sunset; ask for the real duration and the starting point.
- **Bed and blankets:** ask whether blankets are provided and whether you need a sleeping bag.
- **Cancellation and deposit:** written terms, including for bad weather.
- **Arrival time:** plan to arrive before the camels leave, not after dark.
- **Price:** it varies with season and format. Get it in writing with what it covers.

## Cold, heat and season

- **Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November)** are presented by Le Routard as the best times.
- **Summer (June to August):** extremely hot; the sand can be too hot to walk on barefoot.
- **Winter (December to February):** **very cold** nights according to Le Routard and Hespress, and winter is also high season, especially around the end-of-year holidays (Hespress). Infoglobe goes as far as advising against winter for people who dislike the cold, since nights can fall below freezing. No night-time temperature figure was confirmed here: check the forecast before you go.
- **Pack:** a warm jacket, long sleeves, closed shoes and a scarf against sand (Le Routard).

## Common scams and pitfalls

These points come from [Wikivoyage](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Merzouga) and our general [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers) guide:

- **Touts in Rissani, Erfoud or Merzouga:** you may be offered a cheap ride to Merzouga that ends at a particular auberge, followed by pressure to buy a camel ride and a night. If you refuse, getting back can be costly and awkward.
- **Book with the property or agency directly**, not with a stranger on the street.
- **Confirm what is included before leaving**; extras (drinks, shower, transfer) can appear at the end.
- **Very quick friendly or romantic attention:** Wikivoyage notes that some travellers, especially young women, have been targeted for money or favours. Stay cautious.
- **Cash:** Wikivoyage mentions a single ATM that can be unreliable; bring cash. See [money and ATMs](/en/money-cash-atms-morocco).

## Safety and common sense

- The French foreign ministry ([France Diplomatie](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/conseils-aux-voyageurs/conseils-par-pays-destination/maroc/)) advises against driving at night, driving off-piste and travelling alone in isolated places. For quad biking, it notes that local rescue capacity is often limited in remote areas.
- Tell someone where you are staying and keep your phone charged; see [health for travellers](/en/health-morocco-travellers-insurance-emergencies) for insurance.
- Remember tips for guides and camel handlers: [tipping in Morocco](/en/tipping-in-morocco).

## Alternative

For a more remote desert, some choose Erg Chegaga via M'Hamid El Ghizlane (about two hours by 4x4 according to Hespress), which is practically unreachable by camel or on foot.
