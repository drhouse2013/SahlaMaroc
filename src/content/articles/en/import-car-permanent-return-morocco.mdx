Moving back to Morocco for good with your car is one of the most expensive parts of the return, and one of the most misunderstood. Duties and taxes are worked out on a value that **customs sets**, not on what you paid, and several benefits can be used **only once in a lifetime**. This guide complements the [permanent return guide](/en/permanent-return-morocco-mre-guide) and the [180-day rule](/en/car-in-morocco-180-day-rule): it covers the regimes, the bill, the documents and the steps.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- Your car first enters under **temporary admission**: **180 days per calendar year**, no extension. To keep it in Morocco you must **clear it** (pay duties and taxes) or re-export it.
- Two benefits exist: the **“3-year ageing”** (**25%** abatement, permanent return after working abroad, car **under 3 years old**) and the **90% abatement** (**aged 60+**, **more than 10 years** of actual residence abroad, as-new value capped at **300,000 MAD**). They **cannot be combined** and can be used **once**.
- The bill is made of **import duty**, a **parafiscal import tax (0.25%)** and **20% VAT**. The import duty rate could not be confirmed in an official text: see below.
- Get an order of magnitude with our [customs clearance cost calculator](/en/morocco-car-customs-clearance-cost-calculator), then **have ADII confirm it** before buying or shipping.
</Callout>

## The possible regimes

| Regime | Who | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| **Temporary admission** | Any Moroccan living abroad on a visit | Foreign car admitted **180 days per calendar year**, personal and tourist use, **no extension** (ADII circular no. 5945/311 of 4 June 2019) |
| **Permanent return, “3-year ageing”** | Moroccans abroad who held a permanent paid activity | **25%** abatement on the as-new value, **one** car **under 3 years old**, once (ADII guide for Moroccans abroad) |
| **90% abatement** | Moroccans abroad **aged 60+** | **90%** abatement on the as-new value, capped at **300,000 MAD**, once in a lifetime |
| **Ordinary rules** | Everyone else | Full duties and taxes on the value customs uses |

For a permanent return, the customs guide says the car is admitted for **6 months**: it must then be **cleared** or **re-exported**. The benefit request must be filed **before** that temporary admission period ends.

## Eligibility conditions

### 90% abatement (Moroccans abroad aged 60+)

According to the 2019 ADII circular and the Marhaba circulars as reported in the press in 2025 and 2026, **all** of these apply:

- **aged 60 or over** and **more than 10 years** of actual residence abroad, proven (for example a consular registration certificate less than 6 months old);
- not already **permanently settled in Morocco**: the circular excludes such people (see “To confirm” below);
- a **passenger car** (heading 87.03) with **at most 9 seats**, driver included, approved for the road; motorbikes, quads, utility and mixed-use vehicles, camper vans, vans, pick-ups and double cabs are **excluded**;
- **one vehicle per lifetime**; not combinable with the 3-year ageing, free-trade agreements or diplomats' benefits;
- filed **in person** at the customs office of your choice: **powers of attorney are not accepted**;
- as-new value **capped at 300,000 MAD**: the part above follows ordinary rules.

### 3-year ageing (permanent return)

According to the ADII guide: a Moroccan returning for good who held a **permanent paid activity** abroad (shown by **2 years** of payslips or tax returns), **one passenger car under 3 years old**, **once**. The vehicle is treated as if it were 3 years older, which amounts to a **25%** abatement on the as-new value. The guide cited dates from 2011 and the press repeats it in 2026: confirm the details with customs.

### Vehicle age

The general rule is **under 5 years old**. Exception reported in the 2019 circular and in the 2026 press: up to **10 years** for a saloon with **at most 9 seats** if the owner is a retired Moroccan abroad (more than 10 years of residence abroad) or returning for good. For vehicles aged 5 or more, registration centres generally no longer issue an identification certificate (apart from that exception); beyond 10 years, a car cannot in principle be cleared.

## What you pay

| Item | Rate / rule | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Import duty** | Varies with the tariff heading, engine type and origin. Commonly quoted: **17.5%** (petrol or diesel) and **2.5%** (hybrid or electric) | **Indicative**: not found in an official text we could read; other sources quote 10% to 40%, or 0% for electric |
| **Parafiscal import tax** | **0.25%** | Reported by Germany's foreign-trade agency (GTAI, February 2026) and the press; legal text not verified here |
| **Import VAT** | **20%** on the base + import duty + parafiscal tax | Standard rate; base as reported by the trade press |
| **“Luxury car” stamp duty** | 5% to 20% by bracket from **400,000 MAD**, collected by customs since 2018 | Reported by the press (2017 finance law); not calculated by our tool |

**The base** is, according to the texts on the 90% abatement, the **as-new value estimated by customs** for your make, model and specification. It is not your purchase price. For used cars outside the abatement, trade sources also describe an abatement that grows with the vehicle's age, but that scale was not found in an official text: ask customs for the exact taxable value.

**Three examples, for illustration** (indicative rates of 17.5% and 0.25%, 20% VAT, rounded to the dirham):

| Value used | Abatement | Base | Estimated total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100,000 MAD | none | 100,000 MAD | 41,300 MAD (duty 17,500 + tax 250 + VAT 23,550) |
| 80,000 MAD | 25% | 60,000 MAD | 24,780 MAD |
| 154,000 MAD | 90% | 15,400 MAD | 6,361 MAD |

## Special cases

- **Hybrid or electric**: the press, citing ADII (January 2026), reports an import duty of **2.5%** for these vehicles, while other sources say 0% for electric: check the tariff heading. For the 90% abatement, the 2019 circular and a June 2026 press report list petrol, diesel, hybrid and electric engines, while other reports list only petrol, diesel and hybrid: have the electric case confirmed.
- **Older vehicle**: beyond 10 years, in principle no clearance; between 5 and 10 years, only in the cases described above.
- **Registration card in a third party's name**: for the 90% abatement the registration card must be in your name, or proof of transfer of ownership must be provided.
- **New car bought abroad**: if it is less than 3 months old, provide the purchase invoice.

## Documents to prepare

The lists below come from ADII circulars and guides as reported in the press; customs may ask for more.

- **90% abatement**: application form (French and Arabic), Moroccan ID card or passport, residence permit or passport showing your address abroad, consular residence certificate less than 6 months old, copy of the temporary admission card, registration card in your name (or proof of transfer), vehicle identification certificate, invoice if the vehicle is under 90 days old.
- **3-year ageing**: application form, change-of-residence certificate, payslips or tax returns for the last 2 years, identification certificate (2 copies), copy of the temporary admission document (D16 ter or D16 bis), copy of the ID card, purchase invoice if the vehicle is under 3 months old.

## The steps, in order

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Before buying or shipping" title="Check eligibility">

- Check your age, years of residence, and the car's age and type.
- Ask ADII for the **value used** for your model and simulate the duties on the customs portal.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Entering Morocco" title="Temporary admission">

- Declare the car at the border: registration card, driving licence, insurance valid in Morocco ([car insurance](/en/car-insurance-morocco-green-card-border)).
- Keep the temporary admission document: you need it for the file.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="During temporary admission" title="Build the file">

- Get the **identification certificate** from the vehicle registration centre.
- Gather the documents for your regime.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Before the deadline" title="File and pay">

- File at the customs office (**in person** for the 90% abatement).
- Pay duties and taxes; keep the receipt and the clearance certificate.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="After clearance" title="Register the car">

- Apply for Moroccan registration (registration card): documents, fees and timing are to be checked with the National Road Safety Agency (NARSA).
- Budget for the annual vehicle tax (vignette) and Moroccan insurance.

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

Processing times are not officially published: leave a margin before temporary admission ends.

## After clearance

The ADII guide (2011 edition) says that under the 90% abatement the vehicle **may be neither sold nor transferred for 5 years** (except on the beneficiary's death, according to our [permanent return guide](/en/permanent-return-morocco-mre-guide)). The consequences of an early sale are not spelled out in the texts we read: ask customs before considering a sale.

## Common mistakes

- **Letting temporary admission expire**: the 2019 circular sets penalties of **1,000 to 10,000 MAD** depending on the overrun, and the vehicle can leave only after payment.
- **Confusing purchase price with the value used**: a good deal does not necessarily lower the base.
- **Buying before checking**: a van, a pick-up or a car that is too old does not qualify.
- **Sending a proxy** for the 90% abatement: powers of attorney are refused.
- **Counting on two benefits**: ageing and the 90% abatement cannot be combined.
- **Forgetting side costs**: broker, transport, technical inspection, registration card, vignette and insurance come on top of duties.
- **Waiting for the last day**: the file must be filed before temporary admission expires.

## To confirm with ADII

- The **exact import duty rate** for your vehicle (sources disagree).
- The **age-based abatement scale** outside the favourable regimes.
- Whether a Moroccan **coming back to settle permanently** can get the 90% abatement, since the circular excludes people already permanently settled.
- The consequences of a **sale within 5 years** and the rules for electric cars.
