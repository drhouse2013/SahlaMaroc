Moroccans abroad sent more than **MAD 122 billion** to Morocco over 2025, according to the Office des changes (a figure relayed by the business press; the bulletin itself was not re-read line by line). On such sums, a few percent in fees weigh heavily on families. This guide is for an MRE sending money to family, or an expat funding their own account, and shows how to pay less without taking risks.

<Callout type="tip" title="The golden rule">
Always compare **the final amount received in dirhams**, not the advertised fees. The biggest cost is often hidden in the **exchange-rate margin**.
</Callout>

## The options

- **Your European bank:** convenient, but fixed fees and often a weak rate.
- **Moroccan banks present in Europe:** dedicated MRE offers, sometimes attractive for regular sends to an account at the same bank.
- **Cash transfer agencies:** fast and everywhere in Morocco, but check the rate and the dirham payout.

## Steps to send safely

1. **Note the day's reference rate** before comparing.
2. **Ask each service** for fees, rate applied, exact amount received and delay.
3. **Choose the payout:** bank deposit, cash pickup, mobile wallet.
4. **Check the beneficiary:** exact name, RIB/IBAN or ID.
5. **Send** and keep proof (receipt, reference number).
6. **Confirm receipt** with your relative.
7. **Archive** the proofs.

## For a large sum or a purchase

Prefer a **bank transfer to a convertible-dirham or foreign-currency account**: the record of foreign origin helps for a property purchase or a later retransfer. See [transferring your savings](/en/transfer-savings-morocco-mre) and [buying in Morocco from abroad](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad). Cash complicates everything: a customs declaration is mandatory from MAD 100,000 (IGOC 2026).

## 5 reflexes

1. Compare the **amount received**, not the fee percentage.
2. Group your sends: one monthly transfer often costs less than four small ones (depending on tariff).
3. Avoid double conversion (your bank **and** the recipient's).
4. Beware of "zero fee" promises: the margin is in the rate.
5. Keep the proofs.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing only fixed fees.
- Sending through an unlicensed provider.
- Mistyping the IBAN or beneficiary name.
- Forgetting the recipient may pay a payout fee.
- Not keeping proof for a future purchase or retransfer.

## FAQ

**How long does it take?** It varies by service and bank; ask before sending, no single official delay is guaranteed.

**Is there a limit?** Limits depend on the provider. For foreign-currency and convertible accounts, no inflow cap appears in the IGOC text read, but the bank may ask for the origin of funds.

**Must I declare it?** A bank transfer goes through licensed banks that carry out the checks. For tax on your income: [MRE taxes](/en/mre-taxes-morocco-what-to-declare).

**Can I send to an account without a RIB?** Always prefer a named account.

## Comparison example (fictional figures)

Formula: **amount received = amount sent × rate applied − fees**. Fictional example for €500 with a reference rate of 10.00: service A shows €0 fees but applies 9.70, i.e. MAD 4,850; service B charges €5 and applies 9.95, i.e. 495 × 9.95 = MAD 4,925. "Zero fee" is here the worse deal. Redo the calculation with the day's real figures.

## Regular or one-off sends

For monthly sends to family, a service offering a **scheduled transfer** and a saved beneficiary avoids mistakes. For a single large send, compare in advance and allow time: a transfer to a convertible account can take several days.

## Protecting yourself from scams

Never share your card PIN or login; do not send money to a stranger promising a "good deal"; check the provider's website (address, licence); beware of urgent requests by messaging apps. See also [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers).

## Three typical cases

**Helping parents every month.** A scheduled transfer to your parents' account, or an app transfer to a wallet or bank account, avoids queues. Note the amount received each time to spot rising costs.

**Funding works or a purchase.** For a large sum, a bank transfer to a convertible-dirham or foreign-currency account keeps a record of the origin of funds: [transferring your savings](/en/transfer-savings-morocco-mre). Ask the bank for its fees and rate **before**.

**Sending money to a relative with no bank account.** A cash pickup at a licensed agency is possible, but the rate applied is key: compare the dirham amount handed over at the counter.

## Extra questions

**Can my relative collect cash without an account?** Depending on the service, a branch pickup is possible with ID: check the conditions.

**Can a transfer be cancelled?** It depends on the provider and the transfer's status: ask the terms before sending.

**Which is best, a bank or an app?** There is no universal answer: compare the final dirham amount, delay and convenience for each send.

## Keeping records

Create a simple log of date, amount sent, rate, fees and amount received for each transfer. Over a year it shows which service really costs least, and it provides proof if you later need to justify the origin of funds for a purchase or a bank query.

## Checklist

1. Reference rate noted.
2. Final amount compared across 3 services.
3. Beneficiary checked.
4. Proof of sending kept.
5. Receipt confirmed.
