## The three Moroccan operators

Morocco has three mobile networks: **Maroc Telecom** (also branded *IAM*), **Orange Maroc** and **inwi**. In big cities — Casablanca, Rabat, Marrakech, Tangier, Agadir — all three work well. If you plan to head into the Atlas mountains or the Sahara, ask locally which network is strongest on your route: coverage varies valley by valley.

**5G launched on 7 November 2025** with all three operators. Your existing SIM supports it; you just need a compatible phone (iPhone 12 or newer, most mid/high-end Androids from 2020 onwards). Outside major cities you'll mostly be on 4G, which is perfectly fine for video calls.

## Where to buy a SIM card

1. **At the airport** — Casablanca (CMN), Marrakech (RAK) and Rabat have operator counters in arrivals. Prices are normally the same as in town.
2. **Official operator shops** in any city centre — the safest option.
3. **Avoid street sellers**: unclear registration and plans.

**Bring your passport.** Prepaid SIMs must be registered to an ID; the shop does it in a few minutes. Ask staff to activate your data pack before you leave the counter and check that mobile data works.

### Topping up

Recharge cards are sold in almost every corner shop (*hanout*) and tobacco kiosk. You can also top up and activate passes from each operator's app. Keep your receipt: it often shows the activation code.

## When a travel eSIM makes more sense

A travel eSIM (Airalo, Holafly, etc.) costs more per GB than a local SIM, but:

- you land **already connected** — no queue at the airport counter;
- you keep your home SIM active for bank SMS codes;
- it's ideal for short trips and multi-country itineraries.
