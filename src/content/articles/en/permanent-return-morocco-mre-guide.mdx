Moving back for good is a life decision, not just a trip. Mistakes are costly: a badly declared removal, a car you can't resell, a pension taxed at the wrong rate. This guide gives you **the order of operations** and the key rules, and replaces a month of forum reading.

<Callout type="tip" title="The 4 points that save (or cost) you the most">
- **Change-of-residence certificate:** without it, no removal allowance. The **6-month** deadline runs from its issue date.
- **One removal per family**, in 1 or 2 shipments at most, through the **same customs office**.
- **Car:** a **90 %** reduction is possible from **age 60**, but resale is banned for **5 years**.
- **Tax:** beyond **183 days** a year in Morocco you are tax resident. Plan for tax on your foreign income.
</Callout>

## The return countdown

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="12 to 6 months out" title="Decide and prepare the file">

- Sit down with a **tax adviser**: income, assets, pension, foreign accounts.
- Tell your **pension fund** and check how the pension will be paid.
- Start **sorting**: everything you take must be on the inventory.
- If you are buying or building in Morocco, read our [property](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad) and [MRE mortgage](/en/mortgage-morocco-mre-guide) guides.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="3 months out" title="Get the supporting documents">

- **Change-of-residence certificate**, issued by the **town hall of your last place of residence** or the competent **Moroccan consulate**.
- **Proof of residence abroad:** residence permit, work contract, trader's card or a visa longer than 6 months.
- **Detailed, dated and signed inventory** of personal effects and furniture.
- For people who held a permanent paid activity abroad: a **separate list** of used equipment and tools.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="1 month out" title="Organise transport">

- Choose a **carrier** or mover used to Morocco and ask about their customs experience.
- Confirm the **customs office** of arrival: any second shipment must go through the same one.
- Notify banks, insurers, tax office, social security and your children's school.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Arrival in Morocco" title="Clear customs within 6 months">

- File the inventory at the **first import operation**.
- Keep **all originals** and at least two copies.
- Once cleared, keep the documents for several years.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="First 6 months" title="Settle your new life">

- Declare your new **tax residence** if you exceed 183 days.
- Open a resident **bank account** and organise the transfer of your pension.
- Car: start customs clearance if you are eligible (see below).

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## Removal and customs

### What enters duty-free

For an MRE settling permanently, customs grants **full exemption from duties and taxes** for:

- **used furniture**, personal effects and clothing in use;
- **household appliances**, new or used, **one unit per appliance category**;
- **used equipment and tools** up to **150,000 MAD**, for people who held a permanent paid activity abroad;
- a **40,000 MAD** ceiling for personal effects and non-commercial items (raised from 30,000 to 40,000 MAD under Operation Marhaba 2026).

Beyond these ceilings, duties and taxes are due at the going rate.

### Procedure rules to remember

- **One removal per family.**
- Goods arrive **in one go, or at most in two operations**, through the **same customs office**, within **6 months** of the change-of-residence certificate date.
- The **whole list** must be on the inventory filed at the first operation.
- Clearly **commercial** quantities (several identical screens, many new appliances) will be taxed.

## Clearing your car

A specific regime exists for MREs **aged 60 and over** returning for good:

| Condition | Rule |
|---|---|
| Age | 60 and over |
| Residence abroad | At least 10 years |
| Benefit | **90 %** reduction on the vehicle's value as new |
| Value cap | **300,000 MAD** |
| Frequency | **Once** in a lifetime |
| Resale | **Banned for 5 years** (except on the beneficiary's death) |
| Approval | Vehicle must meet homologation conditions |

This reduction cannot be combined with the age allowance for permanent returns, free-trade agreements or diplomats' advantages. **Other profiles** fall under a different regime: ask customs (ADII) for an exact calculation before buying or shipping the vehicle. See also the [180-day rule](/en/car-in-morocco-180-day-rule) if you keep the car under temporary admission.

## Tax and pension

- **Tax residence:** you are tax resident in Morocco if you spend **more than 183 days a year** there (consecutive or not).
- **Foreign-source pension:** Morocco provides a **flat-rate allowance** on the taxable gross amount and, if the pension is **permanently transferred** to a Moroccan bank account in dirhams (with no right to transfer it back), an **80 % reduction of the tax** due on that pension. The transfer condition must be proven in writing.
- **Tax treaty:** the treaty between your country of departure and Morocco decides which country taxes what. Get advice before transferring anything.
- **Foreign accounts and income:** some remain taxable in the country of origin. A tax adviser will help you avoid double taxation.

## Your permanent-return checklist

<Checklist id="permanent-return" lang="en" title="Permanent return: my steps">

- Meeting with a tax adviser (income, pension, assets)
- Pension fund notified, payment method checked
- Change-of-residence certificate obtained (town hall or consulate)
- Proof of residence abroad (residence permit, contract, visa over 6 months)
- Detailed, dated and signed inventory
- List of used equipment and tools (if paid activity abroad)
- Carrier chosen, customs office identified
- Goods cleared within 6 months, in 1 or 2 shipments at most
- Car: 60+ regime eligibility checked, or other regime studied
- Moroccan bank account opened
- Pension transfer organised and documented
- Bank, insurers, tax office, school notified

</Checklist>

<Callout type="warning" title="General information">
Amounts (allowances, ceilings) and procedures change regularly, sometimes with Operation Marhaba or the finance law. This guide is not legal or tax advice: check each point on the Moroccan customs (ADII) website and with a tax adviser.
</Callout>
