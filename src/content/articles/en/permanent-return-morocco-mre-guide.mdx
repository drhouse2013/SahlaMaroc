Coming home for good is a life decision, not just a trip. Mistakes are expensive: a badly declared removal, a car cleared under the wrong scheme, a foreign pension taxed without the reduction you were entitled to. This guide gives the **order of operations** and the key rules, based on the customs administration's (ADII) MRE guide and the ministry's MRE tax guide.

<Callout type="tip" title="The 4 points that save (or cost) the most">
- **Change-of-residence certificate**: without it, no removal duty exemption. Operations must be completed within **6 months** of its issue date.
- **One removal per family**, in one or at most two shipments, through the **same customs office**, with the full list filed at the first shipment.
- **Car**: a permanent return opens a 3-year "ageing" allowance in the tax calculation (one vehicle, once in a lifetime). The 90% allowance is a different scheme for people aged 60 and over: read the box below before choosing.
- **Foreign pension**: an 80% reduction of the tax due is possible if you transfer it permanently to Morocco and can prove it.
</Callout>

## Who is this for, and how long does it take?

This guide is for an MRE (Moroccan living abroad) who **moves back to settle**: employee, retiree, trader, professional, sometimes a student. Allow at least **6 to 12 months** of preparation: the customs file takes a few weeks, but tax, pension and housing take longer. Typical example: a retired couple who have lived in France for over 30 years and want to settle in Marrakech or Oujda with a container of furniture, a car and a pension paid by a French fund. Each of those three falls under a different scheme.

## The return timeline

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="12 to 6 months before" title="Decide and prepare the file">

1. Talk to a **tax adviser**: income, property, pension, foreign accounts, and the tax treaty between your departure country and Morocco.
2. Tell your **pension fund** and ask how the pension will be paid abroad or in Morocco.
3. Start **sorting**: everything you take must appear on the inventory.
4. If you are buying or building in Morocco, see our [property](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad) and [mortgage](/en/mortgage-morocco-mre-guide) guides.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="3 months before" title="Get the supporting documents">

1. **Change-of-residence certificate**, issued by the **municipal authority of your last place of residence** or by the **competent Moroccan consulate**, stating your status (employee, trader, retiree, student...).
2. **Proof of residence abroad**: residence permit or other proof of habitual residence, plus employment card or contract, trader's card, residence visa of more than 6 months or equivalent.
3. A **detailed, dated and signed inventory** of personal belongings and furniture.
4. If you carried on a permanent paid activity abroad: a **separate dated and signed list** of used equipment and tools.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="1 month before" title="Organise transport">

1. Choose a mover who knows Moroccan customs clearance and ask which **customs office** the container or truck will go through.
2. Remember that both shipments, if any, must go through **the same office**.
3. Notify bank, insurers, tax office, social security and your children's school (see [children and school on return](/en/return-morocco-children-school-equivalence-cnss)).

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="On arrival in Morocco" title="Clear customs within 6 months">

1. File the inventory at the **first import operation**.
2. Keep **all originals** and at least two copies.
3. Keep the clearance documents for several years afterwards.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="First 6 months" title="Settle your new life">

1. Open a resident **bank account** and organise the pension transfer.
2. Check your health cover: [AMO after the return](/en/amo-health-insurance-return-morocco-mre).
3. Start car clearance if you are eligible (see below).

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## Removal and customs

### What is duty-free

For an MRE employee, trader or professional returning for good, the ADII grants **full exemption from duties and taxes** for:

- **used furniture**, personal effects and clothing in use;
- **household appliances**, new or used, **one unit per category** (one fridge, one washing machine, one cooker...);
- **used equipment and tools** up to **MAD 150,000**, for those who carried on a permanent paid activity abroad;
- non-commercial items in limited quantities, within a value cap. The 2025 ADII guide states **MAD 30,000**; several media report an increase to **MAD 40,000** for Marhaba 2026 (ADII circular 6741/311, which we have not read). Check the current cap with customs before sizing your shipment.

This value cannot be concentrated on a single type of item (for example only shoes). Above the caps, duties and taxes are due at the standard rate.

### Procedure rules

- **One removal per family.**
- Goods arrive **in one go, or at most in two shipments**, through the **same office**, within **6 months** of the certificate date.
- The **whole list** must be on the inventory filed at the first operation.
- Clearly commercial quantities (several identical screens, many new appliances) will be taxed.

## Clearing your car

Two benefits exist and they must not be confused.

<Callout type="warning" title="3-year ageing allowance and 90% allowance: two separate schemes">
**Permanent return: ageing.** An MRE returning for good gets an extra allowance of **3 years** when determining the taxable value (a recent car is treated as more than 3 years old). One passenger car per person, once in a lifetime. You need, among other things, the change-of-residence certificate (6 months maximum), the temporary admission card, the identification certificate and your ID card.

**MRE aged 60 and over: 90% allowance** on the new-vehicle value (capped at MAD 300,000), for people who lived abroad for more than 10 years, once in a lifetime. The ADII guide states that this allowance "cannot be granted to persons settled permanently in Morocco". Ask a customs office which of the two profiles applies to you before shipping the car.
</Callout>

Other rules: the vehicle must meet type-approval conditions (NARSA); in principle vehicles under 5 years old are accepted, up to 10 years for a retired MRE or one returning for good. For details and simulators: [clearing your car on a permanent return](/en/import-car-permanent-return-morocco). If you keep the car under temporary admission meanwhile, respect the [180-day rule](/en/car-in-morocco-180-day-rule).

## Tax and pensions

- **Tax residence**: the usual test is **more than 183 days** in Morocco over a 365-day period, but other criteria exist (home, centre of economic interests). We have not re-read the article of the General Tax Code: have your situation confirmed by the tax authority (DGI) or an adviser.
- **Foreign-source pension** (MRE tax guide 2026): a flat allowance of **70%** on the gross annual amount up to **MAD 168,000**, then **40%** on the excess; then an **80% reduction of the tax due** on the pension, provided it is **permanently transferred to Morocco in non-convertible dirhams**. Attach to your return a pension payment certificate and a bank certificate of the foreign-currency amount and its dirham equivalent.
- **Return**: the global income of the previous year is filed electronically before **1 March**.
- **Double taxation**: the treaty between your departure country and Morocco says who taxes what.

## Common mistakes

1. Delivering a first shipment without a complete inventory, then finding the second shipment is no longer covered.
2. Letting the certificate's **6 months** lapse.
3. Shipping the car before having the duties calculated (simulator on douane.gov.ma).
4. Transferring the pension without keeping the documents that prove the permanent transfer.
5. Forgetting to cancel or move contracts (insurance, health cover, subscriptions) in the departure country.

## Mini-glossary

- **ADII**: Moroccan customs and indirect taxes administration.
- **Temporary admission (AT)**: scheme allowing a foreign car to stay in Morocco for a limited time.
- **Change-of-residence certificate**: document from the departure place that starts the 6-month clock.
- **Ageing**: a 3-year age allowance on the car when calculating taxes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I stagger several removals?** No: one removal per family, in one or at most two shipments, through the same office.

**Is new furniture exempt?** The ADII refers to used furniture; household appliances, new or used, are admitted at one unit per category.

**What about my spouse, also an MRE?** The removal is one per family. For vehicles, the benefit is individual.

## Your permanent-return checklist

<Checklist id="retour-definitif" lang="en" title="Permanent return: my steps">

- Meeting with a tax adviser (income, pension, property)
- Pension fund notified, payment method checked
- Change-of-residence certificate obtained (town hall or consulate)
- Proof of residence abroad (residence permit, contract, visa over 6 months)
- Detailed, dated and signed inventory
- List of used equipment and tools (if paid activity abroad)
- Mover chosen, customs office identified
- Customs clearance of belongings within 6 months, in 1 or 2 shipments
- Car: applicable scheme (3-year ageing or age 60+) confirmed with customs
- Moroccan bank account opened
- Pension transfer organised and documented
- Bank, insurers, tax office and school notified

</Checklist>
