Meknes, the imperial capital of Moulay Ismail, is smaller and calmer than Fes and often visited in a day or two. As in Fes, the area you choose matters more than the property: the city is made of two distinct centres, the **medina** and the **Ville Nouvelle (Hamria)**, separated by the Boufekrane wadi.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **Cultural visit, 1 to 2 nights:** a riad or guesthouse in the **medina**, near **Place el-Hedim** and **Bab Mansour**.
- **Late arrival, train, restaurants, parking:** the **Ville Nouvelle (Hamria)**, near the stations.
- **Base for Fes or Volubilis:** the Ville Nouvelle, simpler if you move around a lot.
</Callout>

## Two cities in one

The **Historic City of Meknes** has been on the UNESCO list since **1996**: it includes a medina and a kasbah (the imperial city), separated by ramparts pierced by nine monumental gates ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/793/)). Bab Mansour, completed in 1732, is the best-known gate and lies in the historic part; the [Moroccan National Tourist Office](https://www.visitmorocco.com/en/travel/meknes) describes it as a masterpiece of Hispano-Moorish art, on El Hedime square at the entrance to the medina. According to Rough Guides, most sights are in or near the medina and Place el-Hedim is the usual starting point; the Ville Nouvelle is described as modern and without monuments ([Rough Guides](https://www.roughguides.com/article/meknes-morocco/), [Wikivoyage](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Meknes)).

The two parts are less than about five kilometres apart (Rough Guides says "less than three miles"): a short taxi ride, or a walk if you like.

## Medina: atmosphere and heritage

- **Best for:** couples, history lovers, anyone who wants to wake up in an old city.
- **Pros:** monuments, souks and cafés on foot; riads and guesthouses.
- **Good to know:** lanes are narrow and car access is limited. Ask your lodging where to park and how to be met on arrival. See our [riad or hotel](/en/riad-or-hotel-morocco-choose) guide for how riads work.

## Ville Nouvelle (Hamria): practical

- **Best for:** families, travellers by train or car, short stays with an early departure.
- **Pros:** restaurants (Wikivoyage mentions Rue Antsirabe), hotels with parking, easy taxis.
- **Good to know:** there are no monuments here; you take a taxi to the medina. The feel is of a modern city, not a medina.

## Getting to Meknes

- **Train:** Meknes is on the Fes–Rabat line. There are **two stations**: Meknès-Ville, the larger one, on the Hamria side, and Meknès-Amir Abdelkader ([Wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekn%C3%A8s)). Check which one your train serves. Wikivoyage gives about 40 minutes from Fes and 2 h 15 from Rabat; see the [trains guide](/en/trains-in-morocco-al-boraq) for current timetables. The ONMT also gives Fes as 63 km from Meknes (about 1 h 11 by car).
- **Bus:** the CTM bus station is close to Meknès-Ville station; see the [CTM and Supratours guide](/en/ctm-supratours-bus-morocco).
- **Volubilis:** the Roman ruins (the largest Roman archaeological site in Morocco according to the ONMT) are no more than about 40 minutes by car according to Rough Guides, and are often combined with Moulay Idriss. Check transport (taxi, excursion) before you go.

## When to come

Meknes sits at about 500 m and has a Mediterranean climate with continental influence: hot summers and cool winters (the coldest month averages 0 to 7 °C according to Wikipedia). Spring and autumn are generally more pleasant.

## Precautions

- Agree the taxi price beforehand, or ask for the meter in petit taxis; see [taxi apps](/en/taxi-apps-morocco).
- Confirm cancellation terms and parking.
- For general advice, read [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers).

## Combining with Fes

Meknes pairs well with [Fes](/en/where-to-stay-in-fes): some travellers sleep in Fes and visit Meknes, others the reverse. Choose according to train times and how much calm you want.
