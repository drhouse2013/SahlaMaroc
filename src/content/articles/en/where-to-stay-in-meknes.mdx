Meknes is smaller, calmer and less crowded with tourists than Fes, so the right question is not "which neighbourhood?" but **what Meknes is for in your itinerary**. This 17th-century imperial city can be a one-night stopover, a base for the Roman ruins of Volubilis, or a real slow pause. The plan below follows those three scenarios, shortest first.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **One night en route (Fes – Rabat):** Ville Nouvelle (Hamria), near a station.
- **One to two nights "imperial city":** a riad or guesthouse in the medina, near Place el-Hedim.
- **Base for Volubilis:** the area matters little, but arrange transport the day before.
</Callout>

## Scenario 1: the one-night stopover

If you sleep in Meknes because it sits between two other cities, efficiency comes first. The **Ville Nouvelle (Hamria)** groups hotels with parking, restaurants (Wikivoyage mentions Rue Antsirabe) and taxis. It is a short ride from the medina and close to Meknes-Ville station. There are no monuments in this area: you head to the medina in the morning.

## Scenario 2: one or two nights in the imperial city

Meknes was capital under Moulay Ismail, and its historic centre has been on the UNESCO list since 1996 ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/793/)). Sleeping in the **medina**, around **Place el-Hedim** and **Bab Mansour**, puts you within walking distance of the souks, cafés and several places on the [Meknes city page](/en/cities/meknes#spots): Bab Mansour, the Mausoleum of Moulay Ismail, the Heri es-Souani stables, the Bou Inania Madrasa and the Dar Jamai Museum.

- **Pros:** small-town feel, little traffic around el-Hedim, meals on the square.
- **Limits:** narrow lanes, limited car access; ask where to park and how your host will meet you.

## Scenario 3: base for Volubilis and the surroundings

Volubilis, presented by the Moroccan tourist board as the largest Roman archaeological site in Morocco, is often visited together with Moulay Idriss Zerhoun. A two-night stay leaves a full day for the excursion. Meknes works well as a calm base, but local transport to the sites is limited, so book a taxi or tour in advance.

## Stations and access

Meknes lies on the Fes – Rabat line. There are two stations: Meknes-Ville and Meknes-Amir Abdelkader. Check which one your train uses. Wikivoyage gives about 40 minutes from Fes and 2 h 15 from Rabat; see the [train guide](/en/trains-in-morocco-al-boraq) for current times. For buses, see [CTM and Supratours](/en/ctm-supratours-bus-morocco).

## Season and climate

Meknes sits at about 500 m; summer is hot and winter cool (average temperatures of the coldest month range from 0 to 7 °C according to Wikipedia). Spring and autumn are the mildest. In winter, ask whether your riad's rooms are heated.

## Safety and tips

Meknes has a reputation for being more relaxed than Fes, but keep the usual precautions: agree the taxi fare or insist on the meter ([taxi apps](/en/taxi-apps-morocco)), confirm cancellation terms and parking, and read [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers). To compare formulas, see [riad or hotel](/en/riad-or-hotel-morocco-choose).

## Combining with Fes

If you want both imperial cities, also read [where to stay in Fes](/en/where-to-stay-in-fes): Fes is the intense choice (huge medina, many places to stay), Meknes the pause. Many travellers spend two nights in Fes and one in Meknes on the way to Rabat.
