Marrakech is two cities in one: the walled **medina** of riads and souks, and the **new town** of avenues, cafés and apartments. Where you sleep changes your whole trip.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **First visit, 3–5 nights:** a riad in the medina, close to a drivable gate.
- **Digital nomads and long stays:** an apartment in **Gueliz**, near cafés, coworkings and supermarkets.
- **Pool, calm and comfort:** **Hivernage** or a resort in the **Palmeraie**.
</Callout>

## The Medina: the riad experience

Staying in a riad, a traditional house built around a courtyard, is the most memorable way to see Marrakech. You wake up to the call to prayer, eat breakfast on the roof terrace and walk to the souks in minutes.

- **Mouassine and around Bab Doukkala:** central, quieter, full of design shops and cafés.
- **Riad Zitoun and the Kasbah:** close to Bahia Palace and the Saadian tombs, easy taxi access from the Kasbah side.
- **Mellah:** the old Jewish quarter, authentic and good value.

**Watch out for:** narrow alleys where taxis can't go, noise near Jemaa el-Fna and wifi that's not always good enough for video calls.

## Gueliz: the practical choice for long stays

Gueliz is the modern centre built in the French protectorate era. Think wide avenues, cafés with laptops, gyms, supermarkets and fibre internet. A petit taxi to the medina takes about 10 minutes.

**Best for:** remote workers, long stays and anyone who wants a normal daily routine.

## Hivernage: hotels and nightlife

Between Gueliz and the medina, Hivernage has large hotels, clubs and restaurants. It's comfortable and walkable to the Koutoubia, but less local.

## Palmeraie: resorts and space

North of the city among palm groves, the Palmeraie offers villas and resorts with big pools. It's great for families who want to rest, but you'll need a car or taxis for every outing.
