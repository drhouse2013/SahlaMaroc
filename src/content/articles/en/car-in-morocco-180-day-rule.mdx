Driving to Morocco in your European-registered car is easy as long as you follow the **temporary admission** rules. The key number: **180 days per calendar year**.

<CarDaysCounter />

## The 180-day rule

- Your vehicle can stay in Morocco for **180 days in total per calendar year**, in one or several stays.
- This period **cannot be extended**, except for specific year-end provisions (admissions expiring from 1 December).
- The car must be for **strictly personal and tourist use**.
- If you exceed the limit, a **penalty** applies when you leave.

## Documents you need

Insurance: see [Car insurance for Morocco](/en/car-insurance-morocco-green-card-border). Moving back for good? A different regime applies: [permanent return guide](/en/permanent-return-morocco-mre-guide).

- The **original registration certificate** (or the owner's authorisation if it isn't in your name).
- A valid **driving licence**.
- **Insurance valid in Morocco**: check that Morocco (MA) isn't crossed out on your green card, or buy border insurance.
- **Passport or national ID**.

The temporary admission declaration is made at the border during customs. Keep the document until the car leaves Morocco.

## Tips

- Note your entry and exit dates each trip, or use our [180-day counter](/en/morocco-car-180-day-counter).
- Don't let a Morocco resident use your car while you're away without checking first: it's often not allowed.
- Want to leave the car in Morocco for good? It must be **cleared through customs**: ask customs (ADII) for an exact calculation before deciding.

<Callout type="warning" title="General information">
Customs rules change. Check your situation on the Moroccan customs (ADII) website or with a licensed customs agent.
</Callout>
