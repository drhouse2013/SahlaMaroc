Driving to Morocco in a European-plated car is simple as long as you respect the **temporary admission (AT)** scheme. The rule to remember: **180 days per calendar year**. Misunderstood, it can cost up to MAD 10,000 in penalties. This guide is based on the ADII (customs) MRE guide.

<CarDaysCounter />

## The 180-day rule

- AT is granted for a passenger car or light utility vehicle, for **strictly personal and tourist use**.
- It lasts **180 days, used continuously or in several stays** during the calendar year (1 January to 31 December).
- This period **cannot be extended** during the year.
- If a continuous stay straddles two years, the unused balance of the current year lets you stay without leaving the country and without a dispute.
- A vehicle that has already spent 6 months in Morocco in a year cannot be readmitted the same year, **even by a third party with a power of attorney**.
- Year-end case: an AT that expires **from 1 December** can be extended by **6 more months**, without interrupting the stay and without penalty, counted against the following year. If the AT expired before 1 December and you come forward after that date, the facility is still possible but with the overstay penalty. The request is made at a customs office with the original registration certificate and AT card.

## A concrete example

A family from Lyon arrives at Tanger Med on 20 June with its car and leaves on 31 August: about 72 days used. They return at Christmas for two weeks: 14 more days, about 86 for the year. There is margin, but not enough to spend the whole winter in Morocco. The [counter](/en/morocco-car-180-day-counter) saves you doing the sums by hand.

## Documents you need

- **Original registration certificate** (or the owner's authorisation if it is not in your name).
- A valid **driving licence**.
- **Insurance valid in Morocco**: check that "MA" is not crossed out on your green card, otherwise buy border insurance (see [car insurance for Morocco](/en/car-insurance-morocco-green-card-border)).
- **Passport or ID card**.

The AT declaration is made at customs on entry and you receive a **temporary admission card**: keep it until the vehicle leaves. If lost, the nearest customs office issues a duplicate on a sworn statement. The card replaces neither the registration certificate nor the insurance.

## What happens if you overstay?

Go to customs **voluntarily**. The penalty depends on the delay: **MAD 1,000** up to 30 days, **MAD 2,500** for 31 to 60 days, **MAD 5,000** for 61 days to 6 months, **MAD 10,000** beyond 180 days of overstay. A vehicle past its deadline cannot be driven without ADII authorisation. At expiry you must either **re-export** the car or **clear** it by paying duties and taxes; the fine remains due in both cases.

## Common mistakes

1. Thinking the clock resets at every crossing: it is a cumulative total over the calendar year.
2. Letting a relative resident in Morocco drive the car in your absence without checking.
3. Leaving without the original registration certificate, or with "MA" crossed out on the green card.
4. Waiting until the last day to regularise.
5. Confusing AT with a permanent return: clearing a car follows other rules, see the [permanent return guide](/en/permanent-return-morocco-mre-guide) and [clearing your car](/en/import-car-permanent-return-morocco).

## Mini-glossary

- **AT**: temporary admission, duty-free, for non-residents.
- **Clearance**: releasing the vehicle for consumption with payment of duties and taxes.
- **Re-export**: taking the vehicle out of the country when AT expires.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the limit 180 days or 6 months?** The ADII guide says 180 days per calendar year; other customs documents say six months. Count 180 days to be safe.

**Can I lend my car to a resident cousin?** Ask customs first: AT is tied to the non-resident importer.

**Can I also bring a jet ski or quad?** AT can cover, in addition to the car, one item of a different type (moped, quad, jet ski, pleasure boat), with its own rules.

**I am returning for good: what now?** Do not rely on AT: move to customs clearance under the permanent-return scheme.

## Your stay, step by step, from border to exit

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Before leaving" title="Check documents and your remaining days">

Original registration document (or owner's authorisation), licence, passport or CNIE, insurance with **MA not crossed out**. Work out the days already used this year with the [180-day counter](/en/morocco-car-180-day-counter).

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="At entry" title="Declare the temporary admission">

At the customs office of the port or border, declare the vehicle: you receive the **temporary admission card**. Check the details (plate number, date) before driving off.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="During the stay" title="Keep the card and follow the counter">

Keep the card with the registration document. Note your entry date and count every stay in the year, including short Christmas or Easter trips.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="At exit" title="Get the file closed">

Present the car and the card to customs when leaving. Do not leave without having the exit recorded; the exact closing procedure should be confirmed with ADII.

</RoadmapStep>

## Special cases to anticipate

- **Stay spanning two years**: the unused balance of the current year lets you stay without leaving, but count every day of the next year.
- **Car in a third party's name**: bring the owner's written authorisation with a copy of their ID.
- **Trailer, caravan, jet ski, quad**: they have their own temporary-admission rules; ask customs before taking them.
- **Sale or loan in Morocco**: a car under temporary admission cannot be sold locally without customs clearance. For a life project in Morocco, see [importing your car](/en/import-car-permanent-return-morocco) and [buying locally](/en/buy-car-morocco-new-used-mre).
- **Accident or theft**: keep the temporary admission card and notify your insurer and customs; a police report will be needed for theft. Detailed procedure not verified: confirm with ADII.
