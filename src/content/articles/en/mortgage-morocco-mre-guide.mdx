Borrowing in Morocco while living in Europe is possible, and the exchange rule was relaxed in 2026. But between the **official rule** (financing cap, dirham account, repatriation) and **bank practice** (rates, down payment, documents) there is a gap worth knowing before you sign a promise of sale.

<Callout type="tip" title="What is official (Office des changes, 2026 Instruction)">
- A dirham loan to an MRE to **buy or build** property in Morocco can reach **80% of the property price**, with **no limit on the number of properties** financed.
- The **balance (at least 20%)** is settled by the borrower under the exchange rules (for example with foreign currency sold).
- The loan is **paid into a dirham account opened in your name**.
- The **net proceeds of a resale** can be repatriated, up to the foreign-currency down payment, principal repayments made in foreign currency and any capital gain.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="What is up to the banks, not the regulation">
**Rates** (typical 2026 ranges around 4 to 6% fixed), **term** (up to 25 years, sometimes longer), the **down payment required** (often 20 to 30%) and **file fees** depend on each bank and your profile. These figures are **indicative and unofficial**. Insist on a written offer.
</Callout>

## How much do you really need to put in?

With 80% financing, the minimum down payment is **20% of the price**, but that is not all. You must add **acquisition costs**, which the loan does not always cover: registration duties (4% for housing, 5% for bare land), land registry (1.5% of the price, plus a fixed fee of 100 MAD and a minimum charge of 500 MAD), notary fees and sundry costs. In practice expect **6 to 8%** of the price in costs. Check with the [property buying costs calculator](/en/morocco-property-buying-costs-calculator).

## Step by step

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 1" title="Set the budget and simulate">

Price, costs, works, furniture. Simulate your monthly payment with the [mortgage calculator](/en/morocco-mortgage-calculator): the payment, plus your costs abroad, should not exceed a third of your net income (a usual banking rule of thumb, not a regulation).

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 2" title="Compare at least three banks">

Ask several banks for written offers (for example Attijariwafa bank, Bank of Africa, Banque Populaire, CIH Bank), including those with an MRE desk in Europe. Compare the **APR**, borrower insurance, file fees, early-repayment fees and whether the rate is fixed.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 3" title="Build the file">

ID documents, proof of residence abroad, recent payslips or accounts, bank statements, land title or ownership certificate, promise of sale. The bank then has the **property valued**.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 4" title="Get approval and sign the promise">

Have the promise of sale include a **loan condition precedent** with a precise deadline. See [buying property from abroad](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad).

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 5" title="Deed and mortgage">

At the notary, the sale deed and the **bank's mortgage** are registered at the land registry (ANCFCC). If you cannot attend, the power of attorney must follow a strict form: [power of attorney for property](/en/power-of-attorney-morocco-property-mre).

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## Documents usually requested

- Passport or ID and proof of residence abroad.
- Recent payslips and an employer certificate, or accounts if self-employed.
- Tax assessment and recent bank statements showing savings and the origin of funds.
- The promise of sale or preliminary agreement, and the land title or ownership certificate.
- For a new build: building and occupancy permits, developer contract.

For MREs, the Instruction does not require the sworn statement of non-ownership that it requires from foreign non-residents.

## Loan in Morocco or in Europe?

A dirham loan avoids exchange risk if you repay with Moroccan income, but you most often repay with euros: the **exchange risk** is then yours. A euro loan may cost less but needs collateral on a European property. Either way, use a traceable banking channel and keep every receipt: they condition the **repatriation** of resale proceeds. See [sending money to Morocco](/en/send-money-to-morocco).

## Common mistakes

- Signing a promise **without a loan condition precedent**.
- Forgetting **acquisition costs** in the down-payment budget.
- Trusting a rate "seen on a comparison site" with no written offer.
- Making transfers with no clear trail, then being unable to prove where the funds came from.
- Not planning for the **monthly payment in euros** as the dirham moves.

<Checklist id="credit-immobilier-mre" lang="en" title="Mortgage: my checklist">

- Total budget calculated (price + costs + works)
- Monthly payment simulated with a margin for exchange rates
- Three written offers compared (APR, insurance, early repayment)
- Complete file: income, statements, ID, land title
- Loan condition precedent in the promise of sale
- Down-payment transfers kept with receipts

</Checklist>
