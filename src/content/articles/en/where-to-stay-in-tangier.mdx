Tangier is not a destination like the others: it is first of all a **gate**, between Europe and Africa, by ferry, high-speed train, motorway or air. Your point of arrival therefore decides the right area even more than atmosphere. The city is made of a steep medina, a perched Kasbah, a modern Ville Nouvelle and a waterfront stretching east.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **Charm and heritage:** medina and Kasbah (riads, guesthouses).
- **Residential calm:** Marshan, just west of the Kasbah.
- **Beach and pool for families:** Malabata.
- **Practical, car, restaurants:** Ville Nouvelle or the Corniche.
</Callout>

## Medina and Petit Socco: the historic centre

The medina slopes down to the port in steep lanes. It holds the Petit Socco and several places on the [Tangier city page](/en/cities/tangier#spots), including the Great Mosque and the Kasbah Museum. It is ideal for a night or two of atmosphere, on foot. Luggage, however, is carried by hand, some streets are steep, and café noise or the call to prayer can start early or run late.

## Kasbah and Marshan: views and calm

On the heights, the Kasbah offers views over the strait, riads and guesthouses. Just west, **Marshan** is more residential and quiet: perfect if you want the old-town feel without the din. The Marshan Palace and the American Legation Museum are nearby. Walking access means climbing; car access is limited.

## Ville Nouvelle and Grand Socco: the cosmopolitan side

Around the Grand Socco, the Ville Nouvelle has wide streets, historic cafés, contemporary hotels and restaurants. It is the most practical zone if you arrive at night, travel by car or want evening life. The medina is close, on foot or by a short taxi ride.

## Corniche and Malabata: the sea

The Corniche runs along the bay facing the Mediterranean, with a marina and modern venues. **Malabata**, further east, is a seaside zone of large hotels, suited to families who put beach and pool first. Cap Malabata is on the city page. Count on a taxi to reach the medina, especially in the evening.

## Choose by how you arrive

| You arrive by... | Recommended area | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Early or late ferry | Ville Nouvelle or near the port | Avoid rushing |
| High-speed train | Ville Nouvelle (Tangier-Ville station) | Simple taxi access |
| Car from the south | Corniche, Malabata, Ville Nouvelle | Parking |
| Plane | Depends on your plans; taxi transfer | See [airports](/en/morocco-airports-to-city-centre) |
| Night before Chefchaouen | Medina or Ville Nouvelle | Bus next day |

## Access and journeys

- **Ferry:** see the [ferry guide](/en/ferry-to-morocco-guide) and, for the summer return, [Marhaba](/en/operation-marhaba-guide) and the [motorway from Tanger Med](/en/tanger-med-motorway-tolls-route).
- **Al Boraq high-speed train:** it links Tangier to Rabat and Casablanca; durations and times are in the [train guide](/en/trains-in-morocco-al-boraq).
- **Excursion:** [Chefchaouen from Tangier](/en/chefchaouen-day-trip-from-fes-tangier) is a possible day trip by bus; for the Mediterranean coast, also see [Tetouan and M'diq](/en/where-to-stay-in-tetouan-mdiq).

## Season and weather

Tangier is exposed to wind and can be cool and damp in winter; summer is milder than inland. The sea can be rough: follow swimming advice. In winter, heated accommodation is preferable.

## Safety and tips

Approaches in the medina and near the port exist: decline improvised "guides", agree the taxi fare before getting in or insist on the meter, and keep your papers on you.
