When you live abroad and have income or property in Morocco, one question keeps coming back: **who taxes me, on what, and where do I declare it?** The answer depends on your tax residence and the treaties signed between countries. Here is the general framework, with the right reflexes.

<Callout type="tip" title="Landmarks to remember">
- **Tax residence:** you are tax resident in Morocco if you spend **more than 183 days a year** there (or if your economic interests are centred there).
- **A non-resident MRE** is in principle taxed in Morocco only on **Moroccan-source income** (rent, dividends, interest, real estate gains).
- **Your income earned abroad** (salary, business) is in principle **not** declared in Morocco.
- **Tax treaties** prevent double taxation: ask your tax office for a **tax residency certificate**.
</Callout>

## What's your tax status?

| Situation | Taxed in Morocco on… |
|---|---|
| **Tax resident in Morocco** (> 183 days/year, or centre of interests in Morocco) | Their **worldwide** income, subject to treaties |
| **Non-resident (MRE living abroad)** | **Moroccan-source** income only |

The number of days counts, but not only that: your home, activity and economic interests all matter. If in doubt, have a tax adviser establish your position.

## Moroccan income to know about

- **Rent:** taxed in Morocco. See [property taxes when selling and renting](/en/property-tax-selling-renting-morocco-mre).
- **Gain on a property:** real estate profit tax (20 %, 3 % minimum).
- **Dividends and interest:** subject to withholding taxes depending on their nature.
- **Foreign-source pension** (for a retiree who settles in Morocco): a specific regime with allowance and tax reduction if the pension is permanently transferred to Morocco. See [moving back to Morocco for good](/en/permanent-return-morocco-mre-guide).

## Avoiding double taxation

Morocco has signed more than **50 tax treaties**, notably with **France, Spain, Belgium and Canada**. On property income, these treaties generally provide that the income is taxable **in the country where the property sits**, i.e. Morocco. Your country of residence may then take that tax into account (tax credit or exemption with progression), but **you must declare it**.

**To do:**

1. Identify the **treaty** between Morocco and your country of residence.
2. Obtain a **tax residency certificate** from your country.
3. Declare your Moroccan income **in your country**, in the relevant boxes, with proof of tax paid in Morocco.

## Calendar and obligations

- The **annual income declaration** in Morocco is generally due by **the end of February** of the following year (moved to the next working day if it falls on a weekend).
- Some taxes can be paid **online** on the tax authority portal, including from abroad.
- Keep **leases, receipts, statements and proofs** for several years.

## Checklist for MRE owners and investors

<Checklist id="mre-taxes" lang="en" title="MRE taxes: my checklist">

- Tax status (resident or non-resident) confirmed
- Days spent in Morocco tracked every year
- Tax treaty between Morocco and my country identified
- Tax residency certificate obtained
- Moroccan income declared in Morocco on time
- Moroccan income declared in my country of residence
- Proofs (leases, receipts, taxes paid) archived
- Local taxes (municipal services tax, residence tax) up to date

</Checklist>
