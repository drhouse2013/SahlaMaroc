Morocco has a decent healthcare network in large cities, especially in the **private sector**, but it works differently from Europe: you will often be asked to **pay on the spot** before being reimbursed. This guide suits a few days as well as several months. It does not replace medical advice; for anything touching your health, consult a professional before leaving.

<Callout type="tip" title="The essentials">
- **Before leaving:** routine vaccines up to date, a medical consultation if you take treatment, are pregnant, travel with a baby or have a chronic illness.
- **Insurance:** travel cover with a high medical limit and repatriation; the European card does not cover you.
- **On site:** bottled water, sun protection, duty pharmacies, a copy of your prescriptions.
- **Emergencies:** indicative numbers to confirm (see below).
</Callout>

## Before leaving

### Vaccines and prevention

For a traveller from Europe, no vaccination is presented as compulsory in the sources consulted. The Institut Pasteur lists vaccines to consider depending on the trip: hepatitis A routinely, hepatitis B, typhoid and rabies for long, repeated or isolated stays, and states that there is **no malaria transmission** in Morocco ([Institut Pasteur country sheet](https://www.pasteur.fr/en/node/1673)). The list depends on your age, history and itinerary: book an appointment at an international vaccination centre or with your doctor, preferably a few weeks ahead.

**Measles** deserves a check: the French foreign ministry displays a regional alert "rise in measles cases" dated March 2026 ([travel advice](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/fr/conseils-aux-voyageurs/conseils-par-pays-destination/maroc/)); the alert text was not readable when consulted. Make sure your vaccinations are up to date, especially for children.

### Papers, prescriptions and medicines

- Carry treatments in original packaging with a **prescription** in French or English, and bring a reasonable reserve.
- Some medicines (narcotics, psychotropics) are strictly controlled at the border: see [customs and what to bring](/en/morocco-customs-what-to-bring).
- Keep a note with your blood group, allergies and the active ingredient of your medicines (brand names differ between countries).

### Insurance

The European Health Insurance Card does not apply in Morocco. Look for **travel insurance** or an assistance contract with:

1. a high medical-expense limit;
2. cover of **private clinics** (advance of costs or fast reimbursement);
3. **medical repatriation**;
4. a helpline reachable from abroad.

Compare offers with the [travel and repatriation insurance comparison](/en/compare-travel-repatriation-insurance-morocco-mre). If you return to live in Morocco, AMO is what matters: see [AMO health insurance](/en/amo-health-insurance-return-morocco-mre).

## On site

### Pharmacies and duty pharmacies

Pharmacies are numerous in towns. Outside normal hours, **duty pharmacies** cover nights, Sundays and public holidays; the duty pharmacy's address is generally posted on the door of ordinary pharmacies. Many medicines are dispensed without a prescription; still keep your prescription for important treatments. Brands and doses may differ from your country: ask the pharmacist for the equivalent by active ingredient.

### Doctors, clinics and hospitals

- In big cities (Casablanca, Rabat, Marrakech, Tangier, Agadir, Fes), **private clinics** are the ones travellers use most. They often ask for immediate payment or a guarantee.
- Keep **invoices, prescriptions and reports**: without them reimbursement by your insurer will be difficult.
- Outside big cities the offer is more limited; when travelling in the South or mountains, locate the nearest facility in advance.
- For a child or a pregnancy, ask your insurer for a list of partner clinics before leaving.

### Water and food hygiene

Traveller's diarrhoea is the most common nuisance. A few habits: wash hands often, prefer **sealed bottled water**, avoid ice of uncertain origin, eat at busy places, peel fruit. To discover dishes without needless risk, see [Moroccan food](/en/moroccan-food-dishes-to-try).

### Animals, rabies and bites

Stray dogs and cats roam many towns. Avoid petting or feeding them. After a bite or scratch: wash the wound immediately with soap and water for several minutes, then see a doctor quickly and inform your insurer. For rabies, prevention depends on your travel profile (see above).

### Heat, sun and altitude

- **Summer:** hydration, hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, avoid midday effort. Medina visits are better in the morning and late afternoon.
- **Mountains:** cold and altitude, with large temperature swings: see [Toubkal ascent](/en/toubkal-hike-2-days-imlil).
- **Desert:** cold nights in winter, hot days; bring warm layers for the [Fes to Merzouga tour](/en/fes-to-merzouga-desert-tour).
- **Road:** avoid driving at night in rural areas, a recurring piece of advice from the French foreign ministry; see [car rental](/en/car-rental-morocco-guide).

## By profile

| Profile | Plan for |
|---|---|
| Short tourist trip | Travel insurance, prescriptions, bottled water |
| Family with young children | Vaccination record, fever medicine, clinic list |
| Senior or chronic illness | Medical advice, double supply, high insurance limit |
| Long stay / nomad | International health insurance or AMO, remote GP |
| Hiker | First-aid kit, mountain assistance |
| Moroccan living abroad on holiday | Ask your fund in your country of residence |

## For Moroccans living abroad and residents

If you live in Europe, your health fund says whether it covers care in Morocco and on what terms: this varies by country of residence. Keep all documents. If you settle for good, the Moroccan scheme is AMO: see [AMO health insurance](/en/amo-health-insurance-return-morocco-mre) and the guide on [retiring in Morocco](/en/retire-in-morocco-pension-tax-guide).

## Useful numbers (indicative)

| Service | Number cited by the sources read |
|---|---|
| Police | 19 |
| Royal Gendarmerie | 177 |
| Civil protection / firefighters | 15 or 150 depending on sources |
| SAMU / emergency medical aid | 141 (scope to confirm) |

These numbers come from travel guides and embassies, not from an official Moroccan site consulted for this article. **To confirm** before departure with the Ministry of the Interior, civil protection or your assistance provider. On site, your accommodation knows the most suitable number for the town.
