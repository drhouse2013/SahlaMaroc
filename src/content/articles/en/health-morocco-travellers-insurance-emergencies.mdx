Morocco has a good care network in big cities, but the system works differently from Europe: the **private sector** is fastest, and you'll often be asked to **pay on the spot**. Here's how to prepare.

<Callout type="tip" title="The essentials">
- **No compulsory vaccines** for travellers from Europe; the country has been certified malaria-free by the WHO since 2010. Check your routine vaccines.
- **Travel insurance** with a **high medical limit** and repatriation: essential to access private clinics.
- **Emergencies:** **SAMU 141**, **civil protection 15**.
- **Water:** drinkable in cities according to some sources, but **prefer bottled water**.
</Callout>

## Before you go

- **Routine vaccines** up to date (tetanus, etc.). Depending on length and type of stay, health authorities also often recommend **hepatitis A**, **typhoid** and, for some profiles, other vaccines: ask your doctor or an international vaccination centre.
- **Prescriptions** for your treatments and a reasonable quantity: see [customs and medicines](/en/morocco-customs-what-to-bring).
- **Travel insurance:** check the medical limit, coverage of private clinics, assistance and repatriation. For long stays: see [visa and 90 days](/en/morocco-visa-90-day-rule).
- **European Health Insurance Card:** it does not apply in Morocco, so private insurance is needed.

## On site

### Pharmacies

Pharmacies are generally open Monday to Saturday (morning and afternoon, with a lunch break). **Duty pharmacies** cover nights, Sundays and holidays: the address is posted on each pharmacy. Many medicines are available without prescription, but keep your documents.

### Doctors, clinics and hospitals

- In **Casablanca, Rabat, Marrakech** and other big cities, **private clinics** offer good-standard care.
- Doctors and clinics often ask for **immediate payment**; keep invoices and reports for your insurer.
- In a serious emergency: call **141 (SAMU)** or **15 (civil protection)**.

### Food hygiene

Traveller's diarrhoea is the most common problem. Wash your hands, eat in busy places, prefer bottled water and fruit you can peel. See [Moroccan food](/en/moroccan-food-dishes-to-try).

## Heat, sun, altitude

- **Summer:** hydrate, avoid the midday sun, wear a hat and sunscreen.
- **Mountains:** altitude and cold: see [climbing Toubkal](/en/toubkal-hike-2-days-imlil).
- **Desert:** cold nights in winter, bring warm layers: [Fes – Merzouga tour](/en/fes-to-merzouga-desert-tour).

## For MREs

If you live in Europe, ask your health insurer about any coverage of care in Morocco: it depends on your country of residence and on agreements. Keep all supporting documents.
