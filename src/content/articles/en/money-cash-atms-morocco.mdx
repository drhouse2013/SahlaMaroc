Card payment is spreading in Moroccan cities, but **cash is still king day to day**: souks, petit taxis, cafés, tips and most small shops. This guide works for a visitor and for an MRE passing through: how to get dirhams without needless fees, safely and within the exchange-control rules.

<Callout type="tip" title="In short">
- The **Moroccan dirham (MAD)** is a currency whose banknotes may only be taken in and out in limited amounts: you mostly get it once in Morocco. Always check the **daily rate** at [Bank Al-Maghrib](https://www.bkam.ma).
- Withdraw at **bank ATMs** (in or beside a branch), not at stand-alone machines in tourist areas.
- Use a low-fee travel card (for example <AffiliateLink provider="wise">Wise</AffiliateLink>) and always choose to be **charged in MAD**, not euros.
</Callout>

## What the exchange-control rules say

According to the 2026 General Instruction on Foreign Exchange Operations (Office des changes, [oc.gov.ma](https://www.oc.gov.ma)):

- **Importing and exporting dirhams is prohibited**, except travellers may carry up to **2,000 MAD** in notes.
- Foreign currency may be imported **without limit**, but a **customs declaration is mandatory from 100,000 MAD** (or equivalent). Below that it is optional but useful as proof.
- For spending in Morocco, non-residents exchange currency for dirhams at **banks, approved exchange operators or sub-delegated establishments**. Bank-run **exchange machines** are capped at the equivalent of **6,000 MAD per transaction**.
- Non-residents may keep their foreign currency on them and exchange only as needed.

In practice: spend or exchange your last dirhams before leaving and keep your exchange slips.

## Steps: getting and managing your money

1. **Before leaving:** tell your bank, check your card limit and foreign withdrawal fees, take a second card.
2. **On arrival:** withdraw a first amount at a bank ATM in the arrivals hall or exchange a small sum at a counter. Airport rates are often poorer: stick to what you need.
3. **In town:** use ATMs at bank branches, preferably in daytime.
4. **At the ATM:** if the screen offers to convert to euros, **decline** (dynamic currency conversion, poor rate).
5. **Keep receipts** for withdrawals and exchanges.
6. **Before leaving:** spend the cash or reconvert within what you can justify (receipts).

## ATMs: where and how

- **Best:** ATMs of the major banks (Attijariwafa bank, Bank of Africa, Banque Populaire, CIH, Société Générale…), in or in front of the branch.
- Each withdrawal is capped by the machine **and** by your own bank. Exact amounts vary: check your card terms; no single national cap is confirmed.
- A fixed fee may apply per withdrawal: better to withdraw more, less often, without accumulating large sums.

## Cards: where they work

Hotels, supermarkets, chain restaurants, petrol stations, malls and many shops in Guéliz, Casablanca or Rabat take cards. Riads increasingly do, sometimes with a surcharge. Plan **cash** for medina stalls, small eateries, petit taxis and markets.

## Exchange offices

An exchange office must be **approved by the Office des changes**. Compare the displayed rate with Bank Al-Maghrib's reference rate: the gap is the margin. Bring clean, intact notes: damaged notes are often refused. Decline street changers.

## How much cash to carry

For a typical day in town, carry a dirham amount that fits your budget (taxis, meals, coffee, tips): see the [budget calculator](/en/morocco-budget-calculator) and the [cost of living](/en/cost-of-living-morocco). Split it across two pockets, keep small notes and coins, and leave the rest at your lodging.

## Common mistakes

- Accepting conversion to euros at the ATM.
- Changing all your money at the airport.
- Arriving with crumpled or torn notes.
- Using an isolated ATM without checking the card reader for tampering.
- Leaving with more than 2,000 MAD in notes, hoping to reconvert abroad.
- Forgetting to tell your bank: card blocked.

## FAQ

**Can I order dirhams before leaving?** The IGOC limits dirham note import/export to 2,000 MAD per traveller: do not count on a large sum in advance.

**Must I declare my euros?** Mandatory from 100,000 MAD or equivalent; optional below (useful for a later deposit into a convertible account).

**Does Wise work in Morocco?** A Wise card can be used for payments and withdrawals as a traveller; for a Moroccan resident, restrictions exist (see [opening an account](/en/open-bank-account-morocco-mre)). Check the service's conditions.

**Tips in cash?** Yes, in small change: see [tipping in Morocco](/en/tipping-in-morocco).

## Safety at the ATM

Prefer machines inside a branch or an airport hall. Cover the keypad, check the card slot for anything unusual, and withdraw during opening hours so a bank can help if the card is retained. If a machine keeps your card, contact the issuing bank at once and the branch next to it.

## A typical first day

Land, withdraw a first amount at the arrivals-hall ATM, buy a SIM, take a taxi with the price fixed in advance, then change a little more cash in town if needed. Keep your receipts and spend your dirhams before leaving.

## Extra questions

**What are bank opening hours?** They vary by bank and city: do not count on a counter at weekends, prefer ATMs.

**My ATM swallowed my card, what now?** Contact your issuing bank immediately and the branch beside the machine.

**Can I pay in euros?** Shops generally expect dirhams; some hotels accept foreign currency, often at a poor rate.

## Checklist

1. Bank informed, card limit checked.
2. Second payment method.
3. Withdraw in MAD, no dynamic conversion.
4. Receipts kept.
5. Dirhams spent before departure.
