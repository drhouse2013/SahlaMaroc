Card payments are spreading in Moroccan cities, but **cash still runs daily life**: souks, petit taxis, cafés, tips and most small shops. Here's how to get dirhams cheaply and safely.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- The **Moroccan dirham (MAD)** is a closed currency: you get it once you're in Morocco. In October 2026, **1 € ≈ 11 MAD**.
- Withdraw from **bank ATMs** (inside or next to a bank branch), not from standalone machines in tourist spots.
- Use a card with low foreign fees, such as <AffiliateLink provider="wise">Wise</AffiliateLink>, and always choose to be **charged in MAD**, not in your home currency.
</Callout>

## A closed currency: what it means for you

You can't order dirhams from your bank at home, and you shouldn't plan on taking them out of the country either. UK government guidance mentions a limit of **2,000 MAD** in or out, while US guidance is stricter. In practice, spend or change your last dirhams **before** you reach airport security, and keep your exchange receipts in case you want to convert cash back.

Foreign currency above the equivalent of **100,000 MAD** must be declared at the border.

## ATMs: where and how

- **Best:** ATMs of the big banks (Attijariwafa bank, Bank of Africa, Banque Populaire, CIH, Société Générale…). They're everywhere in cities and in arrivals halls.
- Each withdrawal is capped, often at a few thousand dirhams. Your own bank may also charge a fixed fee per withdrawal, so take out larger amounts less often.
- When the screen offers to "convert" the amount into euros or dollars, **decline**. That's dynamic currency conversion, and its rate is almost always worse.

## Cards: where they work

Hotels, supermarkets, chain restaurants, malls and many shops in Gueliz, Casablanca or Rabat take cards. Riads increasingly accept them, sometimes with a surcharge. Expect **cash only** in medina stalls, local eateries, petit taxis and markets.

## Exchange offices

Official exchange offices (*bureaux de change*) in towns usually give fair rates with no commission. Airport counters tend to be worse. Bring clean, undamaged notes: torn notes are often refused.

## How much cash to carry

For a typical day in a city, **300 to 600 MAD** covers taxis, meals in local places, coffee and tips. Split it between two pockets, keep small notes (20 and 50 MAD) and coins for tips, and leave the rest in your accommodation.
