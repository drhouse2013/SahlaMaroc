Moving savings to Morocco is done in practice by **bank transfer from abroad** into an account held in Morocco. The framework is set by the **Office des changes** (General Instruction on Foreign Exchange Operations, IGOC). This guide summarises what its texts confirm and flags what is not confirmed.

<Callout type="info" title="Confirmed and not confirmed">
**Confirmed (Office des changes, oc.gov.ma):** the operating rules of MRE foreign-currency and convertible-dirham accounts, summarised below. **Not confirmed:** each bank's fees and exchange rates, any internal thresholds or documents your bank asks for, and the exact tax treatment of your case. Ask your bank and the tax authority (DGI) in writing.
</Callout>

## Three ways to bring money in

1. **Transfer to a convertible-dirham account:** funds are converted to dirhams on arrival. According to the Office des changes, this account can be freely debited for any spending in Morocco, to **buy foreign currency**, or to open term deposits.
2. **Transfer to a foreign-currency account:** the money stays in euros (or another currency). Crediting by transfer from abroad is free, and so is debiting to abroad.
3. **Transfer to an ordinary dirham account:** fine for spending in Morocco, but without the same freedom to send money back out. See [opening a bank account in Morocco](/en/open-bank-account-morocco-mre).

These accounts are opened on presentation of a document proving residence abroad, kept by the bank (Office des changes, articles 45 and 49 of IGOC 2013).

## Cash: avoid it

Paying foreign banknotes into these accounts requires the **currency import declaration** made at the border, dated **six months at most**, or a recent exchange slip. Without it, cash is hard to place. A bank transfer leaves a trace and simplifies the paperwork.

## Limits and documents

- **Incoming transfer limit:** no ceiling appears in the Office des changes articles consulted on crediting these accounts. Your bank may still ask for the origin of funds (anti-money-laundering checks): keep statements, payslips, sale deeds.
- **Sending money out:** for the foreign-currency account, transfers abroad are free according to the Office des changes. For the convertible-dirham account, the holder may freely buy foreign currency.
- **Dirham origin:** dirhams earned or received in Morocco may not have the same regime: do not assume they are transferable without checking.

Keep the **exchange slip** and transfer advice: they prove funds came from abroad, useful for a property purchase or a later transfer out. See [buying property from abroad](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad).

## Fees and exchange rates

Real cost = sending bank fees + receiving bank fees + **margin on the exchange rate**. There is no single official tariff: each institution sets its own. Compare the **final amount received in dirhams**; an app's displayed rate may differ from your bank's.

## Declaration and taxes

- **Declaration on arrival:** a bank transfer goes through an authorised bank, which performs the checks. According to the texts consulted, you file no separate form for a simple savings transfer; this is not confirmed for every case, so ask your bank.
- **Tax:** moving your own capital is not itself income; tax depends on income (interest, rent, capital gains) and your tax residence. Not confirmed here case by case: see [MRE taxes in Morocco](/en/mre-taxes-morocco-what-to-declare) and the DGI (tax.gov.ma).
- **Country of departure:** your country of residence may impose its own reporting of foreign accounts. Check with its tax authority.

## Checklist

1. Choose the account type in Morocco.
2. Ask the bank for its fees and rate **before** transferring.
3. Make a bank transfer, note the date and rate.
4. Keep the transfer advice and exchange slip.
5. Check tax consequences in both countries.

Sources: Office des changes, MRE section (foreign-currency and convertible-dirham accounts, IGOC), oc.gov.ma.
