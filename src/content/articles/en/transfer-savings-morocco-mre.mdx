Moving savings to Morocco is done in practice by **bank transfer from abroad** into an account at a Moroccan bank. The framework is set by the **Office des changes** in its General Instruction on Foreign Exchange Operations (IGOC, 2026 edition, [oc.gov.ma](https://www.oc.gov.ma)). This guide is for an MRE (Moroccan living abroad) who wants to place savings in Morocco (property project, retirement, safety net) and separates what is read in the IGOC from what depends on your bank.

<Callout type="info" title="What is confirmed, what is not">
**Confirmed (IGOC 2026, Office des changes):** how foreign-currency and convertible-dirham accounts work, rules on banknotes, the 100,000 MAD threshold. **Not confirmed:** each bank's fees and exchange rates, extra documents a bank may require, the exact tax treatment of your case. Ask your bank in writing and check with the [tax authority (DGI)](https://www.tax.gov.ma).
</Callout>

## Three accounts that can receive the money

The IGOC allows banks to open foreign-currency and convertible-dirham accounts for Moroccans residing abroad. These accounts may not go overdrawn.

1. **Convertible-dirham account:** you send euros and the bank converts them. Credits: transfers from abroad and from other convertible accounts. Debits: any payment in Morocco or abroad, **including cash withdrawals**, and term deposits.
2. **Foreign-currency account:** the money stays in euros (or another quoted currency). Same operating rules.
3. **Ordinary dirham account:** handy for spending locally, but without the free retransfer of the two above. See [opening a bank account in Morocco](/en/open-bank-account-morocco-mre).

For a purchase, a convertible account keeps a record of the foreign origin of funds, which helps later (see [buying property from abroad](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad)).

## Steps to transfer your savings

1. **Define the goal and amount:** everyday spending, a purchase or an investment. That decides the account type.
2. **Open or identify the account:** bring your passport or national ID and proof of residence abroad, which the bank keeps.
3. **Get the Moroccan RIB/IBAN and SWIFT code**, and check the holder's name letter by letter.
4. **Compare the total cost:** your sending bank's fee, the Moroccan bank's fee and the margin on the exchange rate. Use the [transfer cost comparator](/en/money-transfer-cost-morocco) and the [euro-dirham converter](/en/euro-dirham-converter).
5. **Send a bank transfer** with a clear reference ("personal MRE savings") and note the date and rate.
6. **Keep** the transfer advice and the exchange slip the bank issues.
7. **Track arrival** (usually a few working days; the texts do not guarantee a delay).

## Cash: best avoided

Importing foreign currency is free with no cap, but a **customs declaration is mandatory from 100,000 MAD** (or equivalent). The IGOC states the import declaration is used to prove the origin of banknotes when you credit a foreign-currency or convertible-dirham account. To pay notes into such an account the bank asks for the original declaration, **dated six months at most**. Without it, cash is hard to place. A transfer leaves a trail and simplifies everything. For dirhams, importing or exporting notes is **limited to 2,000 MAD** per traveller.

## Taking currency back out

An MRE may buy back from a bank or exchange operator, and carry out, the currency repatriated and sold over the **last twelve months, up to 100,000 MAD per year**, on presentation of the annotated import declaration and exchange slips. Amounts credited to convertible-dirham accounts are excluded, because those accounts already have their own freedom of debit.

## Common mistakes

- Arriving with a large sum in cash and no declaration.
- Comparing only fixed fees and forgetting the exchange-rate margin.
- Sending many small transfers when fees are fixed (check the tariff).
- Not keeping the slips: they are missing when you buy property or retransfer.
- Assuming dirhams earned in Morocco can be sent abroad: not automatic.
- Forgetting foreign-account reporting duties in your country of residence.

## Tax: what to know

Moving your own capital is not income. Tax applies to what the money earns (interest, rent, capital gains) and depends on your **tax residence**: see [MRE taxes](/en/mre-taxes-morocco-what-to-declare) and the DGI. Your country of residence may require you to report accounts held abroad: ask its tax office.

## FAQ

**Is there a cap on incoming transfers?** No cap appears in the credit rules for these accounts in the IGOC text read. Your bank may still ask for the origin of funds (anti-money-laundering): keep statements, payslips, a sale deed.

**Can I take the money out later?** For foreign-currency and convertible-dirham accounts, the IGOC allows payments abroad on the debit side. Check your bank's own conditions.

**How long should I keep the documents?** The IGOC passages read set no period; keep them at least for the length of your project and any check.

**What is the best rate?** Always compare the final amount in dirhams. ## Typical case

An MRE selling a flat in France to build in Morocco sends the proceeds by bank transfer to a convertible-dirham account, keeps the exchange slips, then uses the funds for the project: see [building a house from abroad](/en/build-house-morocco-from-abroad-permit).

## Extra question

**Can I transfer the proceeds of a sale in France in several instalments?** Nothing in the IGOC passages read forbids it, but each transfer is checked by the bank: keep the sale deed to justify the origin.

## Checklist

1. Choose and open the account type.
2. Ask for fees and rate **before** sending.
3. Bank transfer, no undeclared cash.
4. Keep the transfer advice and slip.
5. Check tax consequences in both countries.
