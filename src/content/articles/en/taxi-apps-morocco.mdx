Getting around Moroccan cities is cheap, as long as you know the system. Here are the taxis, the apps and a few habits that will save you money and stress.

## Petit taxis

Small, colour-coded cars (red in Casablanca, beige in Marrakech, blue in Rabat…) that run **inside a city** and carry up to 3 passengers.

- Ask for the **meter** (*le compteur*). If the driver refuses, agree on a price before you get in, or wait for the next one.
- Fares go up at night.
- Drivers may pick up other passengers going the same way. That's normal.

## Grand taxis

Larger cars (often old Mercedes or newer vans) for **trips between towns** and to airports. They usually leave when full, from a dedicated station, with a fixed price per seat. You can also pay for all the seats to leave right away.

## Ride-hailing apps

- **Careem:** has been used to book licensed taxis in several big cities, but its presence in Morocco has shrunk in recent years: check that it works in your city.
- **inDrive:** you propose a price and drivers accept or counter-offer. It's popular and cheap, but it operates in a regulatory grey area.
- **Uber:** returned in **November 2025**, starting in Casablanca and Marrakech, using licensed transport operators. It has since expanded to **Agadir and Tangier** (July 2026) and reinforced its service in Rabat and Casablanca.
- **Yango** is also used in the big cities; **Heetch** has operated in some cities, so check availability.

Availability changes quickly, so check the app when you arrive in each city.

## Other useful apps

- **Google Maps** or **Maps.me** (offline maps, very useful in medinas)
- **ONCF** for trains. See our [train guide](/en/trains-in-morocco-al-boraq).
- **Glovo** for food and grocery delivery
- **Avito** for long-term rentals and second-hand items
- **WhatsApp**, which everybody uses, including riads and drivers

You'll need mobile data for all of these. See our [SIM and eSIM guide](/en/best-sim-card-esim-morocco).

## Steps to take a petit taxi

1. **Spot a free taxi** in the city's colour.
2. **State the destination before getting in** and ask for the **meter**.
3. **If the meter is refused,** agree the price before leaving or wait for the next taxi.
4. **Give the precise address** and a well-known landmark.
5. **Keep small change:** the driver may not be able to break a large note. See [cash and ATMs](/en/money-cash-atms-morocco).
6. **Round up** for good service: [tipping](/en/tipping-in-morocco).

## Common mistakes

- Getting in without mentioning the meter or the price.
- Being led to a "friend's" shop: see [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers).
- Calling a ride app without mobile data: [SIM guide](/en/best-sim-card-esim-morocco).
- Assuming an app works in every city.
- Paying for all seats of a grand taxi without meaning to.

## FAQ

**Are fares regulated?** Taxis follow local rules; no official tariff could be read for this guide: use the meter and check on arrival. For airports see [Casablanca airport](/en/casablanca-airport-to-city-centre) and [Marrakech airport](/en/marrakech-airport-to-medina).

**Can I pay by card?** In practice, plan cash.

**Is an app safer?** It leaves a trace of the trip and driver, but availability varies by city; the regulatory status of ride-hailing keeps evolving.

**And to leave the city?** A grand taxi or a coach: [CTM and Supratours buses](/en/ctm-supratours-bus-morocco).

## Three practical situations

**Arriving at night with suitcases.** Avoid walking in an unfamiliar medina: take a taxi from the official station at the train station or airport, state the address and price, and warn your lodging. If the driver cannot enter the lanes, he will drop you at the nearest gate: keep your host's number so they can come and meet you.

**Short city ride, in daytime.** A petit taxi with a meter is simplest. If you prefer an app, check that it works in your city and that your data plan is active (see the [SIM guide](/en/best-sim-card-esim-morocco)). Always keep a fallback: a taxi hailed in the street.

**Trip between two nearby cities.** The grand taxi leaves when full: allow for waiting time, or pay for the empty seats to leave at once. For long distances, the train or coach is more comfortable: see [trains](/en/trains-in-morocco-al-boraq) and [buses](/en/ctm-supratours-bus-morocco).

**For an MRE with family.** With children, luggage and gifts, a booked transfer or a grand taxi for the whole family at a price agreed in advance is better. Petit taxis take a limited number of passengers: ask before loading the bags.

## Extra questions

**Do drivers speak French or English?** Often French and a little English; have the address written in Arabic or French on your phone.

**Can I share a taxi?** A petit taxi may pick up other passengers going the same way: that is normal, but the price must stay clear.

**What if I leave something behind?** If you booked by app, contact support; otherwise it is hard to trace a hailed taxi: note the plate number.

## Good to know before you go

Download the city map offline, save your lodging's address in French and Arabic, and keep your host's number. During Ramadan, rush hours shift around the breaking of the fast: taxis are harder to find at that time. See [public holidays and Ramadan](/en/morocco-public-holidays-ramadan-2027).

## Checklist

1. Mobile data on.
2. Small change.
3. Meter requested or price fixed.
4. Address and landmark given.
5. App checked for the city.
