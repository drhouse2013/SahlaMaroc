Working remotely from Morocco is perfectly possible, provided you **secure your connection rather than assume it**. The country has very high mobile coverage, fibre is growing in big cities and 5G launched in late 2025. But real quality depends on the address, the neighbourhood, the power supply and the operator. This guide helps you build a reliable setup, step by step.

<Callout type="tip" title="The setup that avoids trouble">
- **Home fibre** for video calls and big transfers, if the address is eligible.
- **4G/5G mobile plan** as backup: see [SIM and eSIM](/en/best-sim-card-esim-morocco).
- **Coworking** on days with important calls.
- **A speed test** requested from the host before booking.
</Callout>

## What the regulator's figures say

The national telecoms regulator ([ANRT](https://www.anrt.ma)) publishes quarterly indicators. At the end of March 2026, the business press, which relays these data, reports (to confirm on the ANRT site):

- about **57.06 million** mobile subscriptions, a penetration rate of around 155%;
- about **41.08 million** internet subscriptions, of which **38.1 million** on mobile, **1.4 million** ADSL and **1.5 million** fibre (FTTH), the latter up about 32% on a year ([TelQuel, 26 June 2026](https://telquel.ma/instant-t/2026/06/26/telephonie-mobile-5706-millions-dabonnes-a-fin-mars-2026_1996296/); [Le360](https://fr.le360.ma/economie/les-telecoms-portes-par-la-fibre-et-la-5g_5BD4VYX2T5G5LET2DBH5UHZEJA/)).

The takeaway: **mobile internet dominates** by far; fibre exists but still reaches a fraction of homes. For a nomad, that means mobile access is often plan A in a mid-size town and plan B in a big city.

## Home fibre

The three operators (Maroc Telecom, Orange Maroc and inwi) sell fibre offers. These are orders of magnitude noted in earlier checks, **not reconfirmed for this article**:

| Operator | Subscription order of magnitude | Advertised speed |
|---|---|---|
| Maroc Telecom | From about 400 DH/month | Up to 1 Gb/s |
| Orange Maroc | From about 249 DH/month | Up to 500 Mb/s |
| inwi | From about 299 DH/month | Up to 1 Gb/s |

Before committing, check with the operator: **eligibility** of your address, the **current price** (promotions and launch offers), **connection fees**, the **commitment period** and the documents required. For a stay under three months, fibre is not always worth it; prefer an already-connected home.

## 4G, 5G and backup connection

5G launched from November 2025. According to an ANRT document relayed by Casainvest, the goal was 25% population coverage in 2026 and 70% by 2030: this is a **target**, not measured coverage. In practice 4G remains the base almost everywhere, 5G a bonus in some big-city districts. A **plan with lots of data** and hotspot sharing serve as backup. For plan prices and the SIM card, read our [SIM and eSIM guide](/en/best-sim-card-esim-morocco).

## Electricity and power cuts

Power cuts or voltage drops can happen, especially outside big cities. Bring a **power bank**, a surge-protected strip and, if you rely on a box, a small backup supply. Schedule your most important calls after checking that the neighbourhood is stable.

## Coworking spaces and cafés

Big cities have several coworking spaces, with day, week or month passes. No address is recommended here: offers change, and recent reviews are the reference. To choose:

- ask for a **trial day** to test the connection;
- check **opening hours** and weekend access;
- look for **call rooms** if you do video calls;
- compare the **commute** from your accommodation, which matters more than the price.

Morocco's legal time has been GMT all year since September 2026, so the gap with Europe varies with Europe's clock change: see the [Morocco time zone guide](/en/morocco-time-zone-gmt) before setting meeting times.

## Which city to work from?

Big cities (Casablanca, Rabat, Marrakech, Tangier) offer the best fibre and coworking availability; smaller coastal towns (Essaouira, Taghazout) win on setting but need more connection checks. See [best cities for digital nomads](/en/best-cities-digital-nomads-morocco), [where to stay in Marrakech](/en/where-to-stay-in-marrakech), [Agadir](/en/where-to-stay-in-agadir) and the [cost of living](/en/cost-of-living-morocco).

## Visa and status: a point not to neglect

Working online for a foreign employer or clients from Morocco does not settle the question of your stay. For the permitted length, see [visa and the 90-day rule](/en/morocco-visa-90-day-rule); for a longer stay, [residence permit](/en/morocco-residence-permit-guide). For the activity itself (invoicing clients in Morocco), ask the competent authorities.

## Remote worker's checklist

<Checklist id="teletravail" lang="en" title="Remote work in Morocco: my checklist">

- Speed test obtained from the host
- Fibre eligibility checked or a 4G/5G backup box planned
- Local SIM or backup eSIM
- Power bank and surge-protected strip
- Coworking space or café identified for big days
- Noise-cancelling headset for video calls
- Visa and length of stay checked: [visa and 90 days](/en/morocco-visa-90-day-rule)

</Checklist>
