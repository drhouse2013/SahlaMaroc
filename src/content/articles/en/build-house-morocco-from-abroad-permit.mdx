Building back home is many MREs' dream. It is also one of the projects where you lose the most money when you're far away: poorly supervised site, unchecked land, forgotten permit. Here is the method, **in order**, to cut the risk.

<Callout type="tip" title="The 5 golden rules">
- **Land first:** land title, recent ownership certificate, check the plot can be built on.
- **A registered architect** signs the plans and supervises the works (required in most cases).
- **Permit before the first digger:** the application is filed online on **Rokhas**.
- **Staged payments** tied to progress, always by traceable transfer.
- **Occupancy permit** at the end: without it, no easy resale.
</Callout>

## The 7-step path

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 1" title="Secure the land">

- Ask the land registry for a **recent ownership certificate** (under 3 months old): it shows the owner, mortgages and charges.
- Prefer land with a **land title**. See [buying property from abroad](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad).
- Check that the plot is **buildable** and know the rules (height, footprint, setbacks).

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 2" title="Get the urban planning information note">

This document, issued by the **urban agency**, states what the development plan allows on your plot. It is required in the permit file (its validity is limited: allow a few months).

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 3" title="Choose an architect and a technical office">

- The **architect** designs the plans, signs the file and takes responsibility. Choose one registered with the **Order of Architects**.
- The **technical design office (BET)** calculates the structure.
- Ask for **references, a written contract and a schedule**. Avoid vague "all-inclusive" quotes.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 4" title="File the permit on Rokhas">

The national **Rokhas** platform (Ministry of the Interior) lets you file and track the application **online**. The file generally includes: the signed request, the land title or ownership certificate, the urban planning note, the plans (PDF and DWG), the BET study and a project description.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 5" title="Review">

Allow **30 to 60 days** of legal review depending on the municipality, and **2 to 6 months** in practice if the file is complete and compliant. Delays mostly come from missing documents and plans that don't match zoning.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 6" title="The building site">

- Display the permit on site as required.
- Pick the contractor on **checked references**, with a contract setting price, deadlines and late penalties.
- Pay **in instalments tied to progress** (foundations, structure, finishes), never a large advance.
- Have the architect **supervise the site** and plan regular visits, or a trusted relative with photos and videos.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 7" title="Occupancy permit">

When works end, the municipality issues the **occupancy permit** (housing) or **certificate of conformity** (other use) after inspection, notably on presentation of the architect's certificate of conformity and the utility connection certificates. Without it you cannot lawfully occupy the building, nor sell it easily.

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## Budget: keep a margin

On top of construction costs come architect and BET fees, taxes and file costs, utility connections, landscaping and fencing. Keep a **10–15 % contingency** and model the total cost with the [budget calculator](/en/morocco-budget-calculator). If you borrow, see the [MRE mortgage guide](/en/mortgage-morocco-mre-guide).

## Classic pitfalls

- **Building without a permit:** demolition or fines possible, and you can't resell cleanly.
- **Contractor paid upfront** who disappears: pay on progress.
- **Land in co-ownership** or without a title: require written consent from all co-owners.
- **Plans changed on site:** any major change must be authorised.
- **No supervision:** without regular visits, defects are found too late.

## Power of attorney and remote follow-up

If you can't be there to sign, read [power of attorney for Moroccan property](/en/power-of-attorney-morocco-property-mre) and grant **precise, time-limited powers**.

<Callout type="warning" title="General information">
Exact documents, deadlines and fees vary by municipality and as rules evolve. Check with the municipality, the urban agency and your architect. This guide is not legal advice.
</Callout>
