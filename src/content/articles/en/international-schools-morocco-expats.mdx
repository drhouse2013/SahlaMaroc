Moving to Morocco with a family quickly raises the school question. The country has one of the world's largest **French-curriculum networks**, **American and British schools** in big cities, Spanish and other national schools, and **Moroccan** education, public or private. The right choice depends on your children's language, how long you stay and their study plans. This guide gives a method, not a ranking.

<Callout type="tip" title="The essentials">
- The French network counts **44 accredited schools** and nearly **49,000 pupils** for 2025-2026 according to the press quoting the AEFE; Morocco is one of the top three countries in the global network.
- **Fees** vary enormously: ask for the full schedule and its extras, in writing.
- **Enrol early**: limited places, long files.
</Callout>

## Which families of schools?

| Type | For whom | Points of attention |
|---|---|---|
| **French curriculum** (AEFE network, accredited or partner schools) | French or French-speaking families, curriculum continuity | Limited places in big cities; fees under discussion |
| **American schools** | US curriculum, sometimes International Baccalaureate | Different calendar and diplomas |
| **British schools** | British curriculum, end-of-cycle exams | Own start dates and rhythm |
| **Spanish, Belgian or other schools** | By nationality | Offer concentrated in a few cities |
| **Moroccan schools, private or public** | Families settling long-term | Arabic, French and sometimes Amazigh; child's adaptation |

No specific school is recommended here: visit, compare educational projects, published results and parent reviews.

## Fees: ask the right questions

The press and private guides mention annual fees running from a few tens of thousands to over a hundred thousand dirhams depending on school and level, with American, British and international-curriculum schools at the top. These are **unverified orders of magnitude**: do not budget on them. Ask each school for:

1. registration and re-registration fees;
2. annual fees by level, and their expected evolution;
3. canteen, transport, activities and trips;
4. refund conditions if you leave mid-year;
5. exam and certification fees.

To build your overall budget, see [cost of living in Morocco](/en/cost-of-living-morocco).

## Enrolling a child: the process

1. **Choose continuity** of curriculum (French, IB, American, British) according to the child's study future.
2. **Contact several schools early**, ideally 6 to 12 months before the school year, and ask for the exact list of documents.
3. **Prepare the file**: reports from the last two years, birth certificate, vaccination record, passport or ID, photos, and sometimes recommendation letters or entrance tests.
4. **Visit** and meet the management: ask about welcoming pupils who arrive mid-cycle, Arabic learning and French support.
5. **Plan housing**: urban traffic can lengthen school runs. See [where to stay in Casablanca](/en/where-to-stay-in-casablanca) and [where to stay in Rabat](/en/where-to-stay-in-rabat), then [renting a home long-term](/en/rent-home-long-term-morocco-mre).

## The Moroccan school: not to be dismissed

For a family settling for good, Moroccan education, private or public, offers immersion in Arabic and French and locally recognised diplomas. Adjustment takes preparation, especially for children schooled abroad. For equivalence and file-transfer procedures after a return, read [returning to Morocco with children: equivalence and CNSS](/en/return-morocco-children-school-equivalence-cnss). The [Ministry of National Education](https://www.men.gov.ma) publishes the official information.

## By situation

| Situation | What to favour |
|---|---|
| 1–2 year stay, return planned | Same curriculum as the home country |
| Long-term settlement | Mix of continuity and immersion; Moroccan or bilingual school |
| Returning Moroccan abroad (permanent) | Official equivalence and file transfer |
| Nomad with children | À-la-carte schooling, remote lessons, or a short-term international school depending on local offer |
