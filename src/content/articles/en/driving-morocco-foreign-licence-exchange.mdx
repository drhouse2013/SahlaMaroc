Renting a car, driving your own or settling: in every case the question of the **driving licence** arises. Rules are simple for a short stay and stricter for a resident or an MRE returning for good. This article draws on law 52-05, the 2010 order no. 02-11 on exchange and the Ministry of Transport's information pages.

<Callout type="tip" title="At a glance">
- **Short stay:** a **valid foreign licence** is enough for **up to one year** from the start of the stay (law 52-05, art. 2). For MRE, the year is counted from their **residence in Morocco**.
- **Licence not in Latin or Arabic script:** bring an **international driving permit** as well; it is not always compulsory but strongly advised.
- **After one year:** **exchange** for a Moroccan licence if your country has an agreement with Morocco, otherwise **Moroccan tests** (art. 3).
- **Car rental:** rental firms and insurers have their own seniority requirements. See [renting a car in Morocco](/en/car-rental-morocco-guide).
</Callout>

## Short stay: tourist or visiting MRE

- Drive with your **valid national licence**, photo legible. Always carry it with the registration papers and insurance.
- If you bring your own car, read the [180-day rule](/en/car-in-morocco-180-day-rule) and [car insurance](/en/car-insurance-morocco-green-card-border): the licence is only one of the documents to show.
- If your licence is not in Latin or Arabic characters, an **international permit** (official translation) avoids discussion at a check and at the rental desk.
- A temporary document does not always replace the licence at a rental firm: ask before booking.

## MRE and settling: licence exchange

For an MRE returning for good or a foreign resident, law 52-05 provides two routes after the first year:

1. **Exchange** of the foreign licence for a Moroccan one, if the issuing state has signed a recognition agreement with Morocco (art. 3), or if the agreement provides reciprocity for Moroccan licences.
2. **Moroccan tests** if exchange is not possible.

### Which countries?

Order no. 02-11 on exchange first names **South Korea, France, Japan, Poland and Romania**, then its annex adds, under recognition agreements, notably **Bahrain, Belgium, Benin, Egypt, Spain** (categories A and B), **Italy, Jordan, Oman, Portugal, Switzerland, Syria** and the **UMA** countries. Conditions vary: residence in Morocco, licence in force, nationality required for some states (Bahrain, Jordan). This list dates from 2010 and has been amended since; an agreement with **Portugal** was signed as recently as **June 2026**. **No up-to-date list could be read on an official site**: ask NARSA or the provincial Transport delegation before putting your file together.

### Documents usually cited

(Older) Ministry of Transport information pages cite, for a resident: an exchange application form with the stamp and fee receipt, a **certified copy** of the registration certificate in force (or the application receipt with a recent residence certificate), a **medical certificate** under 3 months old from an approved doctor, **two photos** 35 x 45 mm, and the **original foreign licence** with an **Arabic or French translation** if needed. A third-party guide also mentions a certificate of regular stay. Exact fees and processing time are not confirmed here.

## A permanent-return timeline

| When | To do |
|---|---|
| **Before leaving** | Have the licence translated if needed, check its validity, ask for a certified copy of the file |
| **On arrival** | Note the date: the year runs from residence in Morocco for an MRE |
| **Months 1 to 6** | Gather the medical certificate, photos, residence or registration card |
| **Before the end of 12 months** | File the exchange application with the Transport delegation |
| **After filing** | Keep the receipt: ask how long it is valid and what it allows |

## To avoid

- **Driving with an expired or illegible licence.**
- **Exceeding the one-year period** without exchange or tests: insurance may refuse to pay out after an accident.
- **Assuming your country is on the list** because a forum says so: ask the administration.
- **Lending your foreign-plated car** to a Moroccan resident: the temporary admission regime forbids it.
- **Ignoring local rules:** speed limits, speed cameras, seat belts, phone use, frequent checks. To get around cities without a car, see [taxis and apps](/en/taxi-apps-morocco).

## Checklist

<Checklist id="driving-licence" lang="en" title="Driving licence: my checklist">

- Licence valid and legible, in Latin or Arabic script
- International permit if needed or requested by the rental firm
- Registration papers and insurance certificate valid in Morocco
- Arrival (or residence) date noted: start of the one-year period
- Issuing country checked with NARSA (recognition agreement)
- Exchange application filed before the end of 12 months (residents and returning MRE)
- Receipt kept while waiting for the Moroccan licence

</Checklist>
