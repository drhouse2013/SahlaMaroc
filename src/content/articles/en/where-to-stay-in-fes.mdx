In Fes the question is not only "riad or hotel" but **which gate you enter the medina by**: the historic medina, Fes el Bali, is a pedestrian maze where the last kilometre is on foot. A room 300 metres away as the crow flies can mean twenty minutes of lanes with your luggage. This guide helps you choose by access, not just atmosphere.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **First visit, 2–3 nights:** a riad in Fes el Bali, on the Bab Boujloud or Talaa Kebira side.
- **Calmer, more local medina:** the Andalusian quarter or around Batha.
- **Family, pushchair, long stay, car:** the Ville Nouvelle.
</Callout>

## Bab Boujloud and Talaa Kebira: the easy entrance

Bab Boujloud is the most practical gate: taxis stop there and many riads offer to send someone to meet you. The two main lanes starting here (Talaa Kebira and Talaa Seghira) lead into the heart of the medina. It is ideal for a first visit, with cafés, restaurants and quick access to the madrasas. It is also livelier and noisier in the evening.

## Kairouyine, souks and workshops: the buzzing core

Deeper in the medina, the area around the Al-Qarawiyyin mosque and library, the souks and craft workshops (Chouara tanneries, Bou Inania and Al Attarine madrasas, all listed on the [Fes city page](/en/cities/fes#spots)) is the richest. It is also the farthest from road access: book here if you travel light and enjoy getting lost.

## Andalusian quarter and Batha: more calm

East of the river, the Andalusian quarter is residential and quieter, with the Andalusian Mosque. Around Batha you find the Dar Batha museum and a garden; access is often simpler than in the heart of the medina. These areas suit those who prefer a more local pace and a gentler budget, at the cost of a slightly longer walk to the main souks.

## Fes el Jdid and the heights

Fes el Jdid, the medieval "new city", is airier than Fes el Bali. The hills to the north, with the Borj Nord and the Marinid Tombs, offer views over the medina: few people stay there, but many climb up at the end of the day.

## Ville Nouvelle: the simple choice

The modern city offers wide avenues, cafés, supermarkets and the station. It is a good base for families, remote workers and late arrivals. The medina is a short taxi ride away; you lose the evening riad atmosphere but gain comfort.

## Who sleeps where?

| Profile | Area | Reason |

| Photographer, small lanes | Kairouyine | Heart of the crafts |

| Remote work | Ville Nouvelle | More reliable connection, cafés |
| Early train | Ville Nouvelle | Station nearby |

## Season and comfort

Summer is hot; winter is cool and damp, with cold nights. A shaded patio in summer, heating in winter: ask before booking. Spring and autumn are generally the most pleasant for walking for hours in the medina.

## Safety and pitfalls

In the medina, young people offer to "guide" you to a tannery or workshop; decline politely or fix the terms before following. Official guides are identifiable. Carry your riad's card and a charged phone. See also [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers) and [riad or hotel](/en/riad-or-hotel-morocco-choose).
