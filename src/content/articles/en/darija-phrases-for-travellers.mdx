Moroccans speak **darija**, a Moroccan Arabic mixed with Amazigh, French and Spanish words. French is widely understood in cities, English is growing, and the country's official languages are Arabic and Tamazight. But a few words of darija open doors, defuse a haggle and are quickly remembered. This guide does not claim to teach the language: it gives you **30 safe phrases**, how to say them and when to use them.

<Callout type="tip" title="The 5 most useful words">
**Salam** (hello) · **Choukran** (thank you) · **La, choukran** (no thanks) · **Bchhal?** (how much?) · **Wakha** (okay)
</Callout>

## How to read the tables

- The "Darija" column gives the Arabic script, a useful reference if you ask someone for help.
- The "Pronunciation" column is a simple Latin transcription. **There is no single spelling**: you will see "choukran", "shukran" or "shokran" depending on the site. Only pronunciation counts.
- A few sounds do not exist in English: *kh* (throat, like the Scottish *loch*), *gh* (close to a gargled *r*) and *q* (further back in the throat). An approximation is enough to be understood.

## Greetings and politeness

| Darija | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| السلام عليكم | *salam alikoum* | Hello (reply: *wa alikoum salam*) |
| صباح الخير | *sbah lkhir* | Good morning |
| شكرا | *choukran* | Thank you |
| عافاك | *afak* | Please |
| سمح ليا | *smeh liya* | Sorry / excuse me |
| بسلامة | *bslama* | Goodbye |
| لاباس؟ | *labas?* | How are you? (reply: *labas, lhamdoulilah*) |

In Morocco, people greet before asking anything and ask after the other person: conversation starts with politeness, not with the request. See [etiquette and culture](/en/morocco-etiquette-culture-tips).

## At the souk

| Darija | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| بشحال؟ | *bchhal?* | How much? |
| غالي بزاف | *ghali bzaf* | Too expensive |
| نقص شوية | *n'qes chwiya* | Lower it a bit |
| آخر ثمن؟ | *akher taman?* | Your last price? |
| لا، شكرا | *la, choukran* | No, thank you |

Haggling is a social game, not a conflict: smile, compare two stalls, only offer if you intend to buy. See also [tipping](/en/tipping-in-morocco) and [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers).

## Getting around

| Darija | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| فين…؟ | *fin…?* | Where is…? |
| ديني ل… | *dini l…* | Take me to… |
| شغل الكونتور عافاك | *chaghel lcontor afak* | Please turn on the meter |
| وقف هنا | *wqef hna* | Stop here |
| نيشان | *nichan* | Straight ahead |

## Numbers for prices

1 *wahed* · 2 *jouj* · 3 *tlata* · 4 *rb'a* · 5 *khamsa* · 10 *'achra* · 20 *'achrin* · 50 *khamsin* · 100 *mya*

In markets, prices are sometimes quoted in **rials**, an old unit still used orally: 20 rials make 1 dirham. If a price seems enormous, ask "*b dirham?*" (in dirhams?). For currency, see [cash and ATMs](/en/money-cash-atms-morocco).

## Replies and connecting words to know

- **Wakha**: okay, that works.
- **Inch'allah**: God willing, often used for a future plan, which is not necessarily a refusal nor a firm promise.
- **Bslama**: goodbye, but also simply the end of a conversation.
- **Hamdoulilah**: praise be to God, a common reply to "how are you?".
- **Smeh liya**: to get past someone in a lane or market.

## Four frequent mistakes

1. **Speaking too fast or too quietly.** Speak slowly, even imperfectly: you will be corrected kindly.
2. **Mixing up darija and standard Arabic.** Phrases learnt from a Modern Standard Arabic textbook sound scholarly and are poorly understood on the street.
3. **Ignoring gender.** A few forms change depending on whether you address a man or a woman; a mistake is never serious.
4. **Haggling with no intent to buy.** A smiling "no thank you" beats an abandoned negotiation.

## How to practise

Pick five phrases a day, for example *salam alikoum*, *choukran*, *bchhal?*, *afak* and *bslama*, and use them at every chance (bakery, taxi, café). After a week, add the numbers and two or three transport phrases. For Tamazight, the second official language, see the [Royal Institute of Amazigh Culture](https://www.ircam.ma).
