Buying in Morocco while living in Europe is possible and common. But from a distance, mistakes are expensive. Here are the steps and the traps to avoid.

<Callout type="tip" title="In short">
- Prefer a property with a **land title** (*titre foncier*) and use a **notary**.
- Budget about **6–8 % in costs** on top of the price: [calculate them here](/en/morocco-property-buying-costs-calculator).
- New home and first purchase: you may qualify for **Daam Sakane** aid (70,000–100,000 MAD). See [our guide](/en/daam-sakane-housing-aid-mre).
</Callout>

## 1. Set your total budget

Price + buying costs + any works and furniture. If you borrow, simulate your monthly payment with the [mortgage calculator](/en/morocco-mortgage-calculator).

## 2. Check the legal status

- **Land title (registered property):** ownership is recorded at the Land Registry (ANCFCC). Ask for a recent **ownership certificate** to check the owner, mortgages and charges.
- **Melkia (unregistered property):** ownership proven by an adoul deed. It's riskier: get professional help.

## 3. Sign a preliminary contract with the notary

The *compromis* sets the price, conditions (such as getting a loan) and the deposit. **Never** pay cash without a receipt or to an intermediary.

## 4. Finance and transfer the funds

Moroccan banks offer mortgages for MREs. For money coming from abroad, use a traceable bank channel and keep the records: you'll need them if you sell and repatriate the funds.

## 5. Signing remotely: power of attorney

If you can't travel, you can give a **power of attorney** to someone you trust, drawn up and certified according to consulate or notary rules. It must be precise (property, price, powers).

## 6. Final deed and registration

The notary drafts the deed, pays the registration duty and records the sale at the Land Registry. You then receive a new ownership certificate in your name.

## Classic traps

- Buying off-plan without checking the developer, building permit and guarantees.
- Under-declaring the price "to pay less": it's illegal and risky when you sell.
- Buying without a visit: send someone you trust, with photos and video.

<Callout type="warning" title="General information">
This guide isn't legal advice. Have every file checked by a notary.
</Callout>
