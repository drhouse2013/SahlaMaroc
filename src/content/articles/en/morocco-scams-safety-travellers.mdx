Morocco is a welcoming country, and the great majority of interactions go well. But as in any tourist destination, a few **classic traps** target newcomers, especially in the medinas. Knowing them is almost always enough to avoid them, without losing your smile.

<Callout type="tip" title="The reflex that solves 90 % of situations">
A **calm, firm "no, thank you"** without stopping is enough. Say *la, choukran* (see [Darija for travellers](/en/darija-phrases-for-travellers)) and keep walking. Never feel obliged to discuss or explain.
</Callout>

## The most common scams

| Trap | How it works | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **"The street is closed"** | Someone claims the way is blocked or the market is only open today, then "guides" you and asks for money | Ignore, check your map, step into a shop to ask directions |
| **Fake guide** | A stranger offers an impromptu tour | Politely decline; if you want a guide, choose an **official guide** through your hotel or the tourist office |
| **Forced henna** | Your hand is grabbed and a design started, then a high price demanded | Pull your hand back before the design begins, or agree the price **first** |
| **Taxi without meter** | Meter "broken", exaggerated fare, longer route | Insist on the meter or **agree the price before** getting in; otherwise take another taxi |
| **Commission shops** | A "friend" takes you to a shop where he earns a commission | Say you're only out for a walk |
| **Menu without prices** | An inflated bill at the restaurant | Ask the prices **before** ordering |

## Prices: how not to overpay

- **Haggle** in the souks, with a smile. Start low and accept the price that suits you: the deal is done when both are happy.
- **Ask your hotel** for taxi fares before going out. For fixed fares, use the apps: see [taxis and apps](/en/taxi-apps-morocco).
- Keep **small change**: see [tipping](/en/tipping-in-morocco) and [cash and ATMs](/en/money-cash-atms-morocco).

## Everyday safety

- **Pickpockets:** mostly in crowds (Jemaa el-Fna, stations, trams). Keep your bag in front and your phone in an inside pocket.
- **Night:** avoid empty medina alleys alone; use a taxi.
- **Photos:** ask permission before photographing a person.
- **Drones:** banned without authorisation: see [customs](/en/morocco-customs-what-to-bring).
- **Local respect:** suitable dress in religious places, discretion during Ramadan: see [public holidays and Ramadan](/en/morocco-public-holidays-ramadan-2027).

## Useful numbers

| Service | Number |
|---|---|
| Police | **19** |
| Royal Gendarmerie | **177** |
| Civil protection / ambulance | **15** |

Numbers as given by travel guides and administrations; sources differ on the civil protection line (**15** or **150**) and the current scope of **141**. To be confirmed with the Civil Protection and the Ministry of Health.

Also keep the details of your **embassy or consulate** and your **travel insurer**.

<Callout type="info" title="Keeping a balanced view">
These tips aren't meant to worry you: most travellers visit Morocco without any incident. Preparation is simply the best way to enjoy the trip. Check your own government's official travel advice before you go.
</Callout>

## What to do if you are caught out

1. **Stay calm** and do not get drawn into discussion.
2. **Do not pay under pressure:** offer a small reasonable amount and leave, or step into a shop.
3. **If you are robbed,** go to the nearest police or gendarmerie post and ask for a **complaint receipt**, useful for insurance.
4. **Block your cards** by phone if they are gone.
5. **Tell your embassy or consulate** if papers were stolen.
6. **Contact your travel insurer:** see [travel health and insurance](/en/health-morocco-travellers-insurance-emergencies).

## Common mistakes

- Accepting help from a stranger "to find your riad".
- Leaving a visible phone on a café table.
- Haggling without knowing the reference price.
- Withdrawing cash at an isolated ATM ([cash and ATMs](/en/money-cash-atms-morocco)).
- Believing an announced "closure" of a monument or street without checking.

## FAQ

**Is Morocco dangerous?** For most travellers, no: the risks are mainly petty scams and pickpocketing. Check your own government's official advice before leaving.

**What are the emergency numbers?** Police 19, gendarmerie 177; for ambulance and fire, sources differ (15 or 150): **to be confirmed** with Civil Protection. The DGSN publishes information at [dgsn.gov.ma](https://www.dgsn.gov.ma).

**Should I pay people who offer help?** Not unless you asked for the service; otherwise a "la, choukran" is enough: see [tipping](/en/tipping-in-morocco).

**Solo women?** Same precautions, dress suited to the place and a taxi at night: see [culture and etiquette](/en/morocco-etiquette-culture-tips).

## Three scenes and the right reaction

**A youth approaches you outside the station: "Your hotel is closed, I'll take you."** Do not follow. Call or message your lodging, check the address on your map and take a taxi from the official rank.

**A medina seller pretends to know you.** Stay courteous, you do not have to answer personal questions, keep walking. In a shop, ask the price before touching the goods.

**Someone puts a bracelet or henna on you "as a gift".** Politely withdraw your hand; if it is already done, offer a small reasonable amount and leave. The prices asked at that moment are rarely market prices.

## Advice for MRE

An MRE may be taken for a local tourist or, conversely, assume they know the prices. Learn reference prices before big purchases (cars, jewellery, rugs), and never pay large sums in cash without a receipt. If you buy property or sign a contract, go through a notary or licensed professional: [buying from abroad](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad).

## Extra question

**What if I lose my passport?** Report it to the police for a receipt, contact your consulate for an emergency document, and keep a digital copy of your passport to speed things up.

## Practical routine

Each morning, decide your route, carry only the cash you need, and leave the rest of your money and documents in the safe at your lodging. Keep a photo of your passport on your phone and a backup card in a different bag. At night, prefer taxis and well-lit streets.

## Checklist

1. Digital copy of your passport.
2. Backup bank card.
3. Reference prices known.
4. Emergency numbers noted (to be confirmed).
5. Travel insurance checked.
