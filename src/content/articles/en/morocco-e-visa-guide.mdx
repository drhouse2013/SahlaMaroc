If your nationality is not visa-exempt, you can in some cases apply online for an **e-Visa** instead of going through a consulate. It is also a topic where **intermediary sites** charge a lot for a procedure you can do directly. This article sets out what can be stated, what remains to be confirmed on the portal, and why the procedure matters to MRE families with a foreign relative.

<Callout type="warning" title="One official address only">
The e-Visa application is made **exclusively** on the kingdom's official portal: **acces-maroc.ma**. Many "assistance" sites rank first in searches and add **significant service fees** for the same procedure. Check the address before paying. The portal also offers another electronic authorisation for certain nationalities: choose the procedure that matches your case.
</Callout>

## Who is concerned?

Nationals of the **EU, UK, USA, Canada, Australia** and many other countries can enter **visa-free** for 90 days: they do not need an e-Visa (see the [visa and 90-day guide](/en/morocco-visa-90-day-rule)). The list of exempt or visa-required countries is not in the law: Moroccan authorities maintain it.

According to the conditions published when the e-Visa launched in **July 2022** (relayed by the press, not re-read in a current official text), the e-Visa targets three profiles:

1. **Certain nationalities** on a list drawn up by the Moroccan authorities.
2. **Foreigners residing** in the EU, USA, Australia, Canada, UK, Japan, Norway, New Zealand or Switzerland, with a **residence permit** valid for at least 180 days at the application date.
3. **Holders of a (non-electronic) Schengen, US, Australian, Canadian, British, Irish or New Zealand visa**, multiple-entry and valid for at least 90 days at the application date.

These conditions may have been widened or adjusted since. The portal has an **eligibility check** (nationality, country of residence, passport type, purpose of travel, any other visa): it is the only up-to-date reference.

### The MRE angle: foreign spouses and relatives

For an MRE family, the typical case is a **spouse, in-law or friend** who is foreign, whose nationality is not visa-exempt but who legally resides in Europe. Their profile then often matches point 2 above. For a **marriage** or **settling** in Morocco, do not rely on the e-Visa: see [marriage and civil status](/en/marriage-civil-status-morocco-guide) and the [residence permit](/en/morocco-residence-permit-guide).

## Duration, validity and timing

- **Maximum stay: 30 days**, **validity: 180 days** from issue, **single entry** (launch conditions).
- Purposes accepted at launch were **tourism** and **business**.
- Times announced at launch: **72 hours** standard, **24 hours** express. An incomplete or inaccurate application falls outside these times.
- An e-Visa **cannot be extended** and **does not convert** into a residence permit. Beyond 90 days of presence, law 02-03 requires a registration card.

## Step by step

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 1" title="Check your eligibility">

On acces-maroc.ma, enter nationality, country of residence, purpose and any visa held. The result tells you whether you need an e-Visa, another authorisation or a consular visa.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 2" title="Prepare documents">

Passport valid (with a margin after your return date), **digital passport photo**, and depending on your case: residence permit or current visa. Also keep your return ticket and accommodation address.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 3" title="Fill in and pay on the portal">

Fill in the form, upload documents and pay by card. **The official fees are displayed before you confirm**: note the amount, print or capture the page. We could not read a current official fee schedule; commercial sites advertise very different totals because they add their own fees.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 4" title="Receive and keep the decision">

The answer arrives by email. Print the e-Visa, check name, passport number and dates, and keep it with your passport.

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## Common mistakes

- **Paying an intermediary:** same forms, same documents, extra fees.
- **Passport close to expiry** or a misspelt name: the e-Visa is tied to the passport.
- **Wrong procedure:** the e-Visa and the electronic authorisation for certain nationalities are two distinct procedures.
- **Confusing stay and validity:** 180 days of validity does not mean 180 days on site.
- **Relying on the e-Visa to settle:** you need a registration card, applied for in Morocco.

## Checklist

<Checklist id="e-visa-morocco" lang="en" title="Morocco e-Visa: my checklist">

- Eligibility checked on acces-maroc.ma (not on a third-party site)
- Passport valid, name entered exactly as on the passport
- Compliant digital photo
- Residence permit or visa in force, if your profile requires it
- Official fees noted before payment
- E-Visa printed and a copy on your phone
- Return ticket and accommodation at hand on arrival

</Checklist>
