Tipping (*pourboire*) is part of everyday life in Morocco. Many service jobs are modestly paid, so a few dirhams really count. It's never an obligation, but it's expected for good service.

<Callout type="tip" title="The rule of thumb">
Keep a pocket of **coins and 10–20 MAD notes**. Most tips are between **5 and 20 MAD**. Restaurants are around **10 %**, and guides and drivers are tipped per day.
</Callout>

## Typical amounts

| Situation | Usual tip |
|---|---|
| Café (coffee, mint tea) | Round up, or 2–5 MAD |
| Restaurant with table service | About 10 % |
| Petit taxi | Round up to the next 5 or 10 MAD |
| Porter / help with bags | 10–20 MAD |
| Hammam attendant (scrub) | 20–50 MAD |
| Parking guard (*gardien*) | 2–5 MAD by day, a bit more at night |
| Toilet attendant | 1–2 MAD |
| Official city guide (half day) | 50–100 MAD per group |
| Driver on a multi-day tour | 50–100 MAD per day, per group |

These are common practice, not fixed rules. Adjust to the quality of the service and your budget.

## When you don't need to tip

- When the bill already says *service compris* and the service was average.
- For people who offered help you did not ask for. A polite **"la, choukran"** (no, thank you) is enough before they start.
- At supermarkets and chain stores.

## Tips for tipping

- **Small change is gold.** Break big notes at supermarkets or petrol stations.
- Tip in **dirhams**, not euros: coins in foreign currency can't be exchanged.
- On group tours, agree with the others and give one envelope to the driver at the end.
