Tipping (*pourboire*) is part of everyday life in Morocco. Many service jobs are modestly paid, so a few dirhams really count. It's never an obligation, but it's expected for good service. This guide works for a visitor and for an MRE returning after a long time or hosting foreign friends.

<Callout type="info" title="Customs, not rules">
No official text sets tip amounts. The figures below are **common practice gathered from the field and travel press**, not checked with any administration: that is why this guide shows no verification date. Adjust them.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tip" title="The rule of thumb">
Keep a pocket of **coins and 10–20 MAD notes**. Most tips are between **5 and 20 MAD**. Restaurants are around **10 %**, and guides and drivers are tipped per day.
</Callout>

## Typical amounts

| Situation | Usual tip |
|---|---|
| Café (coffee, mint tea) | Round up, or 2–5 MAD |
| Restaurant with table service | About 10 % |
| Petit taxi | Round up to the next 5 or 10 MAD |
| Porter / help with bags | 10–20 MAD |
| Hammam attendant (scrub) | 20–50 MAD |
| Parking guard (*gardien*) | 2–5 MAD by day, a bit more at night |
| Toilet attendant | 1–2 MAD |
| Official city guide (half day) | 100–300 MAD per group (reported customs vary) |
| Driver on a multi-day tour | about 100 MAD per day, per group |

Adjust to the quality of the service and your budget. A tip is handed over discreetly, hand to hand, with a smile and a "choukran".

## Steps to manage your tips

1. **Make change on arrival:** exchange or break notes into small denominations (see [cash and ATMs](/en/money-cash-atms-morocco)).
2. **Split it:** one pocket for coins, another for 10 and 20 MAD notes.
3. **Read the bill:** "service compris" sometimes means service is already charged; leave a small extra only if deserved.
4. **Tip at the moment of service** (porter, driver) or at the end (guide, tour).
5. **For a group,** decide together and hand over one envelope.
6. **Set a daily cap** so you don't overspend in tourist areas.

## When you don't need to tip

- When the bill already says *service compris* and the service was average.
- For people who offered help you did not ask for (fake guide, "shortcut"). A polite **"la, choukran"** (no, thank you) is enough before they start. See [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers).
- At supermarkets and chain stores.

## Common mistakes

- Tipping in euros: foreign coins are hard for a guard or waiter to change.
- Handing over a large note (100 or 200 MAD) for a few-dirham service: change is rarely given.
- Paying an improvised "guide" before realising he was not official.
- Forgetting the tour driver's tip at the end of an excursion.
- Leaving all your small change at the hotel.

## Tips for tipping

- **Small change is gold.** Break big notes at supermarkets or petrol stations.
- Tip in **dirhams**: for legal tender and denominations, see [Bank Al-Maghrib](https://www.bkam.ma).
- For hammams and excursions, see the [hammam guide](/en/moroccan-hammam-guide-prices) and [day trips from Marrakech](/en/day-trips-from-marrakech).
- Learn a few words: see [Darija phrases](/en/darija-phrases-for-travellers).

## FAQ

**Should I tip a taxi driver?** Not required, but rounding up is appreciated. For taxi fares and rules: [taxis and apps](/en/taxi-apps-morocco).

**What about a riad?** A small amount for the staff at the end of the stay is common; there is no official scale.

**Can I tip by card?** In practice no: carry cash.

**How much for a desert or mountain guide?** Customs vary with length and group size; the table gives an order of magnitude to adapt.

## By place

- **Riad or guesthouse:** a sum for the staff at the end of the stay, handed over personally or in a box provided; there is no official scale.
- **Tourist or local restaurant:** in a tourist-oriented place 10 % is common; in a simple eatery, rounding up is enough.
- **Excursions and desert:** guide and driver are usually thanked at the end of the day or tour. Agree with fellow travellers to avoid awkwardness.
- **Hotel:** a few dirhams to the porter or housekeeper if you wish.

## For an MRE

If you return home with family, explain the habit of small change to children born abroad. Avoid giving foreign currency: staff must exchange it, often at a rate that is poor for them.

## Three scenarios

**A day in town.** A mint tea, lunch at a small place, a taxi, a car-park guard: prepare a few 5 and 10 MAD coins and 20 MAD notes. By the end of the day you will mostly have used coins.

**A multi-day tour with driver and guide.** Agree in advance with your travel companions, hand over an envelope at the end, and adjust to length, group size and service quality.

**A riad stay.** Leave a sum for the staff at the end of the stay, preferably handed over personally or in the box provided.

## Extra questions

**Should I tip in a hotel?** A few dirhams for the porter or housekeeper if you wish.

**What about delivery riders?** A small tip in dirhams is appreciated; no fixed amount.

## A note on fairness

Tipping is a way to recognise good work, not a tax on tourists. If service is poor, you can leave less or nothing. If you travel with a group, agree a simple rule in advance so that nobody feels pressured, and keep amounts in line with local wages and your own budget.

## Checklist

1. Small notes ready.
2. Change split up.
3. Bill checked.
4. Tip handed over discreetly.
5. No "fake guide" paid.
