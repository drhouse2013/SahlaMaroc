Morocco is affordable by European standards, but costs vary a lot by city and lifestyle. This guide is for a **nomad or expat** planning a stay of several months, and for an **MRE** considering settling or spending long periods back home. Use the calculator for your own estimate, then read the details.

## Typical monthly budget (one person)

| Lifestyle | Total per month | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | ~8,000–10,000 MAD | Simple studio, local food, public transport |
| Comfortable | ~13,000–18,000 MAD | 1-bed flat in a good area, mix of restaurants, taxis, coworking |
| Premium | ~25,000 MAD and up | Modern flat or villa, restaurants, car, travel |

These ranges are **indicative estimates**, the same as in the calculator: no official source publishes a typical budget for an expat or nomad, and they are no substitute for your own budget. Casablanca and Marrakech are generally described as the most expensive cities, Fes and Essaouira as the cheapest. To compare specific cities, use the [cost-of-living-by-city calculator](/en/cost-of-living-by-city-morocco-calculator).

**Official price benchmark:** according to the High Commission for Planning (HCP), the consumer price index for August 2026 rose 0.8% month on month and fell 0.3% year on year (food: −3.9%; non-food: +2.5%) ([HCP, August 2026 CPI](https://www.hcp.ma/L-Indice-des-prix-a-la-consommation-IPC-du-mois-d-Aout-2026_a4348.html), read on 10 October 2026). Prices move: re-check rents and rates when you plan your move.

## How to build your budget, step by step

1. **Choose the city:** rent weighs most. Compare two or three cities.
2. **Set the lifestyle** (budget, comfortable, premium) and the number of people.
3. **Fix the rent** from current listings, not old blog figures.
4. **Add fixed costs:** electricity and water, internet, phone, health insurance.
5. **Estimate food:** home cooking and markets, or restaurants.
6. **Plan transport:** taxis, train, possibly a car.
7. **Keep a 10–15 % margin** for the unexpected, travel and price rises.
8. **Convert** at the day's rate ([Bank Al-Maghrib](https://www.bkam.ma)): see the [euro-dirham converter](/en/euro-dirham-converter).

## Rent

Rent is your biggest expense. Furnished apartments in sought-after areas (Gueliz, Maarif, Agdal…) cost much more than unfurnished flats in residential neighbourhoods. For stays under 3 months, short-term platforms are simplest; for longer stays, local listings and agencies are cheaper. A long lease follows rules: see [renting a home long term](/en/rent-home-long-term-morocco-mre) and [where to stay in Marrakech](/en/where-to-stay-in-marrakech).

## Food

Markets and local eateries are very cheap: fresh produce, bread and mint tea cost little. Imported products and international restaurants are priced close to European levels. See what to try in our [food guide](/en/moroccan-food-dishes-to-try).

## Transport

Petit taxis and trams are cheap, and intercity trains are good value. See our guides to [taxis and apps](/en/taxi-apps-morocco), [trains](/en/trains-in-morocco-al-boraq) and [car rental](/en/car-rental-morocco-guide).

## Connectivity

Local prepaid SIM: on 10 October 2026 the operators displayed 30-day internet packs at about **50 MAD (10–11 GB) or 100 MAD (20–24 GB)**, often as promotions, plus the SIM card (20 MAD at Orange, with 20 MAD of credit included). Home fibre is available in big cities (see [fibre and coworking](/en/fibre-internet-coworking-morocco)). See also the [SIM guide](/en/best-sim-card-esim-morocco). The regulator is the [ANRT](https://www.anrt.ma).

## Health and insurance

Plan suitable health cover, especially outside the Moroccan health-insurance system: see [travel health](/en/health-morocco-travellers-insurance-emergencies) and, for a return, [AMO](/en/amo-health-insurance-return-morocco-mre).

## Money

To limit currency-conversion and withdrawal fees abroad, use a low-fee card. See [cash and ATMs](/en/money-cash-atms-morocco).

## Common mistakes

- Trusting rent figures from an old blog without checking recent listings.
- Forgetting a margin for the unexpected and travel.
- Comparing cities without considering the neighbourhood.
- Ignoring health insurance.
- Converting at the wrong rate or accepting euro conversion at the ATM.
- Forgetting that an MRE owner's budget includes local taxes and upkeep: see [MRE taxes](/en/mre-taxes-morocco-what-to-declare).

## FAQ

**Is Morocco cheaper than France?** On local food and transport, generally yes; not on imported goods. No official comparison is published here: test it with the calculator.

**How much for a couple?** Rent is shared but other costs rise; set the calculator to two people.

**Can I live on a foreign pension?** See [retirement budget](/en/retirement-budget-morocco) and [retiring in Morocco](/en/retire-in-morocco-pension-tax-guide).

**Do I need to budget visa costs?** Depending on your nationality: see the [90-day visa rule](/en/morocco-visa-90-day-rule).

## Costs people forget

Deposits (often one to two months' rent, to be agreed in the lease), agency fees, furnishing, a deposit for utilities, laundry, a gym or coworking membership, and bank charges on international card use. Budget these in the first month.

## Two sample profiles

**A remote worker for three months in Marrakech.** Furnished flat from a short-term platform, SIM plus coworking, taxis and a few restaurant meals: budget in the "comfortable" range, then check each line against current prices.

**An MRE planning a gradual return.** Start by renting for a few months in the target city before committing: it shows real costs, neighbourhoods and commuting. See [permanent return](/en/permanent-return-morocco-mre-guide) and the [permanent-return budget](/en/permanent-return-budget-morocco).

## Extra questions

**What budget for a first week?** Compute lodging, meals, transport and connectivity separately with the calculator, then add 10 to 15 %.

**Do prices change with the season?** Yes, notably lodging in tourist cities.

## For an MRE comparing with Europe

Compare like with like: same type of housing, same neighbourhood, same frequency of outings. The real cost also includes flights or the ferry to come back, health insurance and any local taxes on a property. Use the [city calculator](/en/cost-of-living-by-city-morocco-calculator) to refine.

## Updating your budget

Prices drift with inflation and seasons. Re-check rent and utilities every few months and update the calculator with your real receipts, so your estimate stays tied to what you actually spend.

## Checklist

1. City and lifestyle chosen.
2. Rent from recent listings.
3. Fixed costs and health budgeted.
4. 10–15 % margin.
5. Conversion at the day's rate.
