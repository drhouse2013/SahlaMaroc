After the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations, hosted by Morocco (December 2025 – January 2026), and with the 2030 World Cup ahead, demand for tourist rentals is booming in Moroccan cities. But **listing a flat on Airbnb is no longer trivial**: Morocco regulates the activity. Here's what to know before publishing a listing.

<Callout type="warning" title="Key points">
- Renting a furnished property to tourists on a **regular basis** falls under **tourist accommodation establishments** (Law 80-14).
- An **operating authorisation** is required before renting, and the activity must be **declared**.
- Without authorisation, **penalties** apply (fines, administrative closure); the amounts quoted vary between sources: have them confirmed by the regional tourism office.
- Rent is **taxable** and guests must be **declared**.
</Callout>

## What does the law say?

**Law no. 80-14** on tourist establishments and other forms of tourist accommodation, supplemented by an implementing decree that came into force in 2023, classifies a short-term furnished rental to travellers as **tourist accommodation** as soon as it becomes habitual or commercial. Depending on the property, categories include the **guest house**, the **tourist residence**, **home-stay accommodation** or the **tourist furnished rental**.

## Practical obligations

1. **Obtain the operating authorisation** from the competent local authority (file submitted via the regional investment centre, indicative timeline around 30 days).
2. **Declare the activity** to the municipality and the regional tourism delegation.
3. **Meet the safety and comfort standards** of your chosen category.
4. **Declare guests** (identity, length of stay) through the digital system provided.
5. **Collect and pay the tourist tax** where it applies.
6. **Declare your income:** tourist rent counts as business income, taxed under the income tax scale. According to some sources, a reduced **10 % VAT** rate applies to tourist accommodation and VAT liability depends on annual turnover: have your situation confirmed by an accountant or the tax authority (DGI).

## Co-ownership, lease and bank: three things to check

- **Co-ownership rules:** some residences ban tourist use. Read them before investing.
- **Your loan contract or lease:** tourist sub-letting may be forbidden.
- **Your insurance** must cover tourist rental.

## If you don't live in Morocco

You'll need a **trusted local manager** (concierge service or family) for key handovers, cleaning, guest declarations and emergencies. Put it in a **written contract** and, if needed, a [power of attorney](/en/power-of-attorney-morocco-property-mre) limited to these tasks. Tourist rent falls under the tax framework in our guide to [property taxes when selling and renting](/en/property-tax-selling-renting-morocco-mre).

## Checklist before your first listing

<Checklist id="short-term-rental" lang="en" title="Short-term rental: my steps">

- Co-ownership rules read, tourist rental allowed
- Accommodation category identified (tourist furnished rental, guest house…)
- Operating authorisation obtained
- Activity declared to the municipality and the tourism delegation
- Safety standards checked (extinguisher, detector, exits)
- Guest declaration procedure in place
- Tourist tax planned if applicable
- Insurance suited to tourist rental
- Tax regime chosen with an accountant
- Local manager appointed by contract

</Checklist>
