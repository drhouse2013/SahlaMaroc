Renting before buying is a prudent choice on return: you test the city, the neighbourhood and the school without committing. This guide covers the **long-term rental** of a residential home, not tourist rentals (see [short-term rental](/en/short-term-rental-morocco-rules)). It states what is **confirmed**, what comes from **secondary sources**, and what to ask a professional.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- Residential leases fall under **law 67-12** (19 November 2013, per secondary sources); furnished short-term rentals do not.
- Demand a **written contract**, a detailed **inventory** and a **receipt** for each payment, by bank transfer if possible.
- The **security deposit** is returned on departure, minus sums due; the practice cited is **one to two months' rent**, but we found no **legal cap**.
- **Lease registration** rules could not be confirmed: ask the tax authority or a notary.
</Callout>

## The 6-step journey

<Roadmap>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 1" title="Prepare the budget and documents">

- Set your budget with the [cost of living](/en/cost-of-living-morocco): rent, charges, water, electricity, internet, building fees.
- Prepare your documents: national ID or passport, proof of income (even foreign), and if needed a guarantor. Real requirements vary by landlord.
- Decide the duration: a one-year lease lets you test, but do not commit long before visiting.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 2" title="Search and visit">

Use listings from several sources and visit at different times of day (noise, water, neighbours). If you are abroad, **have a relative visit** with a live video. Pay **no advance** before seeing the home and the owner.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 3" title="Check the owner">

Ask for the landlord's identity (national ID) and proof of his right to the home: land title, ownership certificate or deed. For a flat in a co-owned building, ask for the rules and service charges. See [buying property from abroad](/en/buying-property-morocco-from-abroad) for reading an ownership certificate.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 4" title="Draft the lease">

Have it state: duration, rent, charges, payment date and method, notice, renewal conditions, deposit amount and return conditions. A **guarantee** deed is a document separate from the lease: do not sign a guarantee without understanding its scope. Have a notary or lawyer review it if possible.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 5" title="Deposit and inventory">

The deposit covers tenant defaults, not normal wear. **Photograph** the home on entry and exit, sign a **joint inventory** and keep a copy. Pay by transfer and keep the receipt.

</RoadmapStep>
<RoadmapStep when="Step 6" title="Live there, then leave">

Keep your rent receipts. Respect the notice period in the lease. On departure, redo the inventory and demand return of the deposit with a breakdown of deductions.

</RoadmapStep>
</Roadmap>

## Documents to prepare

- National ID or passport, proof of income and employment, possibly a tax assessment.
- Proof of current residence (abroad or in Morocco).
- For the landlord: land title or ownership certificate, national ID.
- Dated photos, signed contract, inventory, receipts for every payment.

## Common mistakes

- **Paying an advance "to hold it"** with no visit or contract: the classic fake-listing scheme.
- **Renting with nothing in writing**, then being unable to prove the rent or deposit.
- **No inventory**: a dispute at departure is certain.
- **Paying cash with no receipt**.
- **Forgetting charges** (building fees, water, electricity) in the budget.
- **Signing a guarantee** without understanding its scope.

## If you are the owner and landlord: tax

Rents are land income declared by the **landlord** (a tenant in principle has nothing to declare). For an MRE owner, the **2026 MRE tax guide** states:

- a **flat 40% allowance** on land income before the progressive scale applies;
- for property rented to **legal entities** or to individuals whose professional income is determined under the actual or simplified regime, a non-final **withholding tax** of **10%** (gross income below 120,000 MAD) or **15%** (120,000 MAD and above), with an **option for a final 20% rate** on that withholding-taxed income;
- the exclusion from income tax of the rental value of property **made available free of charge** to ascendants or descendants;
- **housing tax** and **municipal services tax**, with a 75% reduction of the rental value for an MRE's main home (the reduction targets the main home: a rented home does not in principle benefit from it, to be confirmed with the local tax office).

Detail and examples: [tax on selling and renting](/en/property-tax-selling-renting-morocco-mre). If you buy a new home to live in: [Daam Sakane](/en/daam-sakane-housing-aid-mre) (the aid concerns buying, and renting the property out during the 5-year commitment is not permitted).
