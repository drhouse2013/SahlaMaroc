Marrying, registering a birth or getting a family record book while living between two countries means juggling Moroccan rules and those of your country of residence. Here are the landmarks to prepare your files without nasty surprises.

<Callout type="warning" title="Know this first">
Requirements vary with **nationality**, **declared religion** and the **situation** of the future spouses, and they change. The documents below are the most commonly requested. **Confirm the exact list** with the family court, the adoulary notary or the relevant consulate before putting the file together.
</Callout>

## Basic civil status documents

- **Birth certificate** (full copy or recent extract);
- **Family record book**: it gathers the family's records and serves as proof in many procedures (travel with children, administrative files);
- **Residence certificate**, **certificate of celibacy** or **divorce judgment** depending on your situation;
- **Copy of ID** (CNIE or passport).

Documents issued abroad often need an **apostille** and **translation**. See [apostille and legalisation](/en/apostille-legalisation-documents-morocco).

## Marrying in Morocco

The marriage is celebrated by an **adoulary deed** or before an adoulary notary, after a file is submitted to the family court. The most common documents:

- birth certificates and ID of both spouses-to-be;
- certificate of celibacy or divorce judgment / death certificate of a former spouse;
- **medical certificates**;
- anthropometric forms or similar documents depending on the case;
- residence certificates.

## Mixed marriage (one Moroccan spouse, one foreign spouse)

For a marriage where one spouse is foreign, **additional documents** are generally required, notably:

- a **certificate of marital capacity** issued by the embassy or consulate of the foreign spouse's country (depending on agreements between countries, some nationalities may be exempt);
- **criminal record extracts**;
- a **medical certificate** issued in Morocco;
- **documents on the declared religious status** under the applicable law;
- a **certified copy of the passport** with the entry stamp and recent photos;
- **prior authorisation** from the competent judicial authority.

All documents generally have to be supplied **in several copies, translated** and, for some, **legalised or apostilled**.

## After the wedding: getting the record recognised

- **For an MRE family:** have the **marriage record registered** with the Moroccan civil registry via the consulate if you live abroad, and in the **family record book**.
- **In your country of residence:** a **transcription** of the Moroccan record may be needed for it to be recognised (in France, via the central civil status service, for example).
- **Birth of a child:** declare it on time at the consulate to get the Moroccan civil registration and the family record book.

## Checklist

<Checklist id="civil-status" lang="en" title="Marriage and civil status: my checklist">

- Recent birth certificates of both spouses-to-be
- Certificates of celibacy or divorce judgments
- Medical certificates
- ID documents and certified copies
- For a mixed marriage: marital capacity certificate and criminal record
- Documents apostilled and translated
- Prior authorisation obtained if required
- Record registered at the consulate or civil registry
- Family record book updated

</Checklist>
