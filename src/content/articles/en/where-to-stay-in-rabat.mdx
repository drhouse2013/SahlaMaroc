Rabat is the capital but not the metropolis: a green, airy city with a peaceful medina, major monuments and a tram linking the two banks of the Bouregreg. Its appeal is its human scale. The choice of area therefore depends mostly on the pace you want: heritage on foot, modern neighbourhood life or residential calm.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick answer">
- **Heritage and first visit:** Hassan, or the medina and the Oudayas.
- **Convenience and restaurants:** Agdal.
- **Residential calm:** Souissi, with taxis.
- **Moderate cost and tram access:** the edges of Sale, across the river.
</Callout>

## Medina and Oudayas: the old city, gently

Around the Oudayas Kasbah, whose ramparts overlook the mouth of the Bouregreg, the medina is calmer and less pressing than Marrakech's or Fes's. It holds riads, guesthouses and small addresses. The Oudayas Museum and the medina are on the [Rabat city page](/en/cities/rabat#spots). Streets are narrow; ask your host for a meeting point, especially with suitcases.

## Hassan: monuments within walking distance

The area of the **Hassan Tower** and the Mausoleum of Mohammed V is where the large hotels and walking visits are. It is a very good choice for a first stay of 2–3 nights: everything is close and the evening mood is calm. In exchange, restaurants are less varied than in Agdal.

## Agdal: modern and commercial

Agdal is the modern district: shops, restaurants, cafés, students and professionals. It suits a business trip, a long stay or anyone wanting neighbourhood life. It is a bit far from the monuments: take the tram or a taxi. A station also bears its name, but check which one your train uses.

## Souissi: villas, embassies, silence

Residential, with villas and embassies, Souissi is calm. You get around by taxi or car. It is a choice for rest, not for a visit on foot.

## Sale, across the river

The tram crosses the Bouregreg towards Sale. Sale's medina is on the city page. Sleeping in Sale can be cheaper, but you lose the capital's evening atmosphere. Check the last tram time.

## Beach and ocean

Rabat has an urban beach (listed in the places); it suits a stroll more than an intensive swimming holiday. For a real beach stay, look at other coastal cities.

## Which area for your profile

| Profile | Area | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First stay, culture | Hassan / Oudayas | Monuments on foot |
| Business, restaurants | Agdal | Neighbourhood life |
| Couple, calm | Medina or Souissi | Silence |
| Family | Agdal or Hassan | Parks, taxis |
| Tight budget | Sale or Agdal | Compare reviews |
| Stopover before a flight | Near station or airport | Timetables |

## Stations, tram and airport

The tram links Rabat to Sale, the station and the medina; see [tram and BRT](/en/tram-brt-casablanca-rabat-guide). The Al Boraq high-speed train links Rabat to Tangier and Casablanca; see the [train guide](/en/trains-in-morocco-al-boraq). Rabat-Sale airport is covered in the [airports guide](/en/morocco-airports-to-city-centre). To compare with the economic metropolis, read [where to stay in Casablanca](/en/where-to-stay-in-casablanca).

## Season and safety

The Atlantic climate gives mild summers and cool, sometimes wet winters. Rabat is generally seen as calm, but keep usual precautions on transport and in the medina; see [scams and safety](/en/morocco-scams-safety-travellers). Rabat is among the proposed cities for the 2030 World Cup (FIFA's final list not yet published): see [host cities](/en/world-cup-2030-morocco-host-cities).
